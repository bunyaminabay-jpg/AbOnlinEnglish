Cleft Sentences — Vurgu Cümleleri

C1 seviyesinde nominal clause (What + clause + is) yapısını öğrenmiştin. C2 seviyesinde bu yapı, cümlenin herhangi bir öğesini seçerek vurgulayabildiğin tam bir sisteme dönüşür: cleft sentences (yarılmış cümleler). Bu yapılar İngilizce'de bir bilgiyi diğerlerinden ayırıp ön plana çıkarmanın en doğal, en profesyonel yoludur.

📌 It-cleft: It is/was + X + that...

Sade cümleIt-cleft ile vurgu
Edison invented the phonograph.It was Edison who invented the phonograph.
Hard work makes genius, not talent.It is hard work that makes genius, not talent.
She realised the truth in that moment.It was in that moment that she realised the truth.

📌 Pseudo-cleft (Wh-cleft): What/All + clause + is + X

What-cleft
What made him a genius was relentless practice.
What people forget is the years of failure behind every breakthrough.
All-cleft — daraltma vurgusu
All it takes is patience and repetition.
All that separates the amateur from the master is practice.
💡
TIP — Reverse pseudo-cleft
Pseudo-cleft'i ters çevirebilirsin, ikisi de doğrudur:
What surprised me was her patience.
Her patience was what surprised me.

It-cleft genelde kişi/nesne/zaman/yer vurgular; What-cleft genelde eylem/sebep/sonuç vurgular.

The Myth of Effortless Genius

sarı işaretli kelimeler bu ünitenin hedef yapıları ve kalıplarıdır.

It is a comforting story: the genius who produces a masterpiece in a single flash of insight, untouched by struggle. What the popular imagination rarely acknowledges is quite how misleading this picture is.

Take Mozart, so often held up as proof of pure, innate gift. It was not raw talent alone that produced his mature symphonies — it was more than a decade of gruelling, painstaking training under his father's relentless supervision, beginning before the age of five. What separated Mozart from his contemporaries was not some mysterious spark but thousands of hours others simply never logged.

Psychologists who have studied exceptional performers — most famously Anders Ericsson — have pointed again and again to the same uncomfortable conclusion. What seems to distinguish the eventual master from the merely competent, in many fields, is an almost unreasonable quantity of deliberate, effortful practice. Later research has shown that practice is not the whole story — but it is a large part of it. It is this labour, largely invisible and rarely glamorous, that popular biography quietly edits out.

None of this is to say that innate aptitude counts for nothing — it would be naive to claim that everyone could become Mozart given enough hours. But what the myth of effortless genius does is far more damaging than simply getting the history wrong: it discourages the very people who might otherwise persist, by suggesting that talent, not effort, is what ultimately matters. It is precisely this belief — self-evident to almost no one who has studied the matter closely — that keeps so many gifted amateurs from ever becoming masters.

📝 Comprehension Questions · Anlama soruları

1According to the text, what actually separated Mozart from his contemporaries?

Not innate talent, but over a decade of painstaking, deliberate training that others never undertook.

2Why does the writer say the myth of effortless genius is damaging?

Because it discourages people from persisting, by suggesting talent rather than effort determines success.

3Find 2 it-cleft sentences and 2 pseudo-cleft (What/All) sentences in the text and identify what each one emphasises.

It-cleft examples emphasise the TRAINING and the LABOUR as the true cause; What/All-cleft examples emphasise WHAT separated Mozart from others and WHAT the myth actually does.
