Reading — Pen Pals

📖Pen Pals

My name is Mia. I am eleven years old. I am from İzmir. İzmir is a big, beautiful city near the sea. My school is small but friendly.

My pen pal is Can. He is from Trabzon. Trabzon is in the north of Turkey. Can is twelve years old. He is tall with short black hair.

We are pen pals. We write messages every week. 'Hello Can! How are you?' 'I am fine, thank you! And you?' 'I am great!'

Can and I are very different but we are great friends. His favourite colour is blue. My favourite colour is green.

3.

→ His favourite colour is blue.

A small island. A lonely keeper. A cat who changes everything. — An original graded reader for A1 learners.

The Lighthouse Keeper's Cat
Original story · A1 Level · © 2025 AbOnline English
A1
Chapter 1 — The Island

The island is small. Very small. It is in the North Sea. There is one house on the island. There is one tower. The tower is white and very tall. This is the lighthouse.

Thomas lives in the house. He is the lighthouse keeper. Every night, he goes up to the top of the tower. He turns on the lamp. The lamp is very bright. It helps ships in the dark sea.

Thomas is old. His hair is white. His hands are big and rough. He has a thick grey beard. Thomas is alone. He has no family on the island. He has no friends on the island. He has only the sea, the rocks, the birds, and the lamp.

"Good morning, sea." — Thomas

Thomas says every morning. The sea does not answer. But Thomas does not mind. He is happy. Or he thinks he is happy. He is not sure.

💭 Thomas talks to the sea every morning. What does this tell us about him?
Chapter 2 — The Cat

On Tuesday morning, Thomas walks to the rocks. He counts the ships. Then he sees something. There is something on the big flat rock near the water. It is small and orange and very wet. It is a cat.

Thomas looks at the cat. The cat looks at Thomas. The cat is very thin. Its fur is wet. Its eyes are green and large.

"No." — Thomas
"No cats on this island. No." — Thomas

The cat looks at him. It opens its mouth.

"Miaow." — The cat

It is a small sound. Not loud. But very clear. Thomas is not a cruel man. He can see the cat is hungry. He can see it is cold.

"One night. Only one night. Then you go." — Thomas
💭 Thomas says "one night only." Do you think the cat will stay longer? Why?

Original story · A1 Level · © 2025 AbOnline English
A1
Chapter 1 — Leo and the Dark

Leo is eight years old. He is funny and clever and kind. He has a dog called Bear. He likes football and pizza and comics. But Leo is afraid of the dark.

At night, when the light goes off, things change. The chair becomes a monster. The coat on the door becomes something terrible. Leo knows — he knows — that it is only a chair. Only a coat. But at night, knowing is not enough.

"Mum, can you leave the light on?" — Leo
"Leo, you are eight. The dark is not dangerous." — Mum

His mum always leaves a small light on. Leo sleeps better with it. But he is not happy about this. His friends are not afraid of the dark. He is the only one. Or so he thinks.

One morning in October, there is a new girl in Leo's class. Her name is Mia. Mia is nine. She has short brown hair and round glasses. She walks slowly and carefully. She has a white cane. Mia is blind. She cannot see anything.

💭 Mia notices things Leo does not notice. What does this tell us about her?
Chapter 2 — A Question

For one week, Leo wants to ask Mia a question. But he does not know how. The question is this: Are you afraid of the dark?

It seems strange to ask a blind person about the dark. Mia cannot see the dark. She cannot see the light either. For Mia, it is always dark. Or maybe it is never dark. Leo does not understand.

"Mia — can I ask you something?" — Leo
"Yes." — Mia
"Are you afraid of the dark?" — Leo

Mia thinks for a moment.

💭 What do you think Mia's answer will be?

Reading — The Power of a Morning Routine

📖The Power of a Morning Routine

The Japanese writer Haruki Murakami wakes up at four o'clock every morning. He writes for five or six hours. He doesn't change this routine.

Winston Churchill, on the other hand, never got up before eleven. He worked in bed until noon. He usually had meetings in the afternoon.

What does the research say? Studies show that the best routine is the one you actually follow. Some people always feel energetic in the morning. Others rarely do.

Whatever time you choose, one thing is certain: people who stick to a routine tend to be more productive.

Comprehension Questions
1.
What time does Murakami wake up?
→ He wakes up at four o'clock every morning.
2.
What did Churchill do until noon?
→ He worked in bed until noon.
3.
What do studies say about routines?
→ The best routine is the one you actually follow.
4.
What do productive people tend to do?
→ They tend to stick to a routine.

📖 The Postcard from NowhereAbOnline English Readers · Level A2 · Book 1

Someone is sending postcards. But the sender is dead. — An original mystery for A2 learners.

📄
Level
A2 Elementary
🎭

The Postcard from Nowhere
Mystery · A2 Level · © 2025 AbOnline English
A2
Chapter 1 — The Box

Clara's grandmother died in January. She was ninety-one years old and had a very long, very full life. She lived in a small apartment near the sea in Lisbon. She loved fado music, strong coffee, and rain.

After the funeral, Clara helped to clear the apartment. Her mother took the furniture. Her uncle took the books. Clara took a small green box from the top shelf of the wardrobe. The box was not locked. Inside there were seventy-three postcards.

They were all the same — or almost the same. Each one showed a different place: Paris in spring, Amsterdam in winter, a village in Morocco, a market in Tokyo. But each one had the same handwriting on the back. Small and careful and slightly slanted. And each one said the same thing, more or less:

Thinking of you. I hope you are well. — A friend

No name. Always just: A friend.

💭 Why do you think the sender never wrote their name? What might they be hiding — or protecting?
Chapter 2 — Arles

Clara spent two weeks reading her grandmother's old letters and diaries. In a 1949 diary, she found something. Her grandmother — Inês — wrote about a summer she spent in France. She was twenty years old. She went to study painting in Arles. The diary mentioned a name: Henri. A young French painter.

'Henri and I walked along the river this evening. He showed me how to paint light. He said: the light in Arles is different from anywhere else in the world. He is right.' — 15 August 1949

Clara searched online. Henri Vallette was born in 1928. He lived in Arles all his life. He was a painter. He died in 2021, aged ninety-three.

💭 Henri died in 2021 — but the last postcard arrived three months before grandmother died. Who sent it?

The Market of Lost Things
Fantasy · A2 Level · © 2025 AbOnline English
A2
Chapter 1 — Saturday Morning

Sam's grandfather used to tell him stories every Saturday morning. They sat at the kitchen table — Grandpa with his coffee, Sam with hot chocolate — and Grandpa told stories about his life. The war. The factory. The summer he spent in Scotland.

Then Grandpa got ill. Not suddenly — slowly. First he forgot small things. Then big things. Then he forgot Sam's name. Now Sam visited him at the care home every Sunday. Grandpa was always kind. But he didn't know who Sam was.

One day, Sam heard two older boys talking about a Saturday market on Millbrook Street. A sister found her grandmother's ring there — a ring lost fifteen years ago. Sam went to Millbrook Street. And there it was. A market. But not a normal market. There were jars of something that moved inside — like liquid light. Boxes of photographs that changed when you tilted them. Clocks with no hands.

💭 The market is described as 'not a normal market.' What do you think it sells?
Chapter 2 — The Stall

In the middle of the market, there was a stall with no sign. Nothing for sale on it. Just a woman sitting behind a small table, with a cup of tea. She was very old. She had white hair and bright eyes. She looked at Sam as if she had been waiting for him.

"What are you looking for, boy?" — Woman

Sam told her about Grandpa. About the Saturday mornings. About the stories. He wanted to find the last real afternoon they had together. Before Grandpa got ill. They went to the park and Grandpa told him a story — something about Scotland. Sam wasn't paying attention. And now he couldn't ask again.

"That's a very specific thing to want." — Woman
"Can you find it?" — Sam
"The market can find most things. But it asks for something in return." — Woman
💭 What do you think the market might ask for in return?

Story 1: The Early Bird
A2 Workbook Story · Unit 1
A2

Every morning, Elif gets up at 5:30. She never sleeps past six. 'My friends think I'm crazy,' she says. 'But I always feel amazing by noon.'

First, she drinks a glass of water. Then she exercises for 30 minutes. After that, she reads for one hour. She usually reads non-fiction books.

Research shows that people who tend to wake up early often report higher productivity.

📚How much / How many · some / any
Kural
How much + sayılamayan:How much water? How much is it?
How many + sayılabilen:How many apples? How many students?
some → olumlu:I have some money. Would you like some tea?
any → olumsuz/soru:I don't have any money. Do you have any questions?
