REAL ENGLISH — Akademik yazıda
Güçlü ama tehlikeli: "This proves that X causes Y."
Akademik ve doğru: "The evidence suggests that X may contribute to Y."

Why We Make Irrational Decisions

sarı işaretli kelimeler bu ünitenin hedef yapıları ve kalıplarıdır.

It has long been established that human decision-making is not purely rational. Evidence suggests that we are influenced by cognitive biases that operate largely below the level of conscious awareness — systematic errors in thinking that appear to be hardwired into human cognition.

It appears that the way a choice is framed — rather than its objective content — can significantly alter our decision. People tend to respond differently to "a 90% survival rate" than to "a 10% mortality rate," even though these are logically identical. This phenomenon, known as the framing effect, was described by Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman in 1981 and has been replicated in many studies since.

It is possible that evolutionary pressures have embedded certain irrational tendencies into our cognitive architecture. Loss aversion — the tendency to weigh losses more heavily than equivalent gains — may be among the most powerful and well-documented of these biases.

It could be argued that simply knowing about our biases might help us make better decisions. However, there is some evidence that awareness alone is rarely sufficient. Presumably, breaking deeply ingrained cognitive habits requires deliberate practice over time.

To a certain extent, these findings challenge the classical economic assumption of the "rational actor." It remains unclear whether better institutional design could compensate for individual cognitive limitations — a question that continues to occupy researchers across psychology, economics, and neuroscience.

📝 Comprehension Questions · Anlama soruları

1What is the 'framing effect'?

The tendency to respond differently to the same information depending on how it is presented (e.g. '90% survival' vs '10% mortality').

2What is loss aversion?

The tendency to weigh losses more heavily than equivalent gains.

3Does awareness of biases help us overcome them, according to the text?

Not necessarily — there is evidence that awareness alone is rarely sufficient; it may require deliberate practice.

4Find 4 hedging expressions in the text and classify each as strong/moderate/weak/distancing.

e.g. 'has long been established' (strong) / 'appears' (moderate) / 'is possible' (weak) / 'it could be argued' (distancing)

Social media use has long been associated with increased rates of anxiety among young people. It appears that excessive use may worsen existing depression symptoms, though the causal relationship remains contested. It is entirely possible that the relationship between social media and mental health is considerably more complex than previously assumed. It could be argued, however, that social media also provides important community support for isolated individuals who might otherwise have none.

Sleep deprivation appears to impair memory consolidation quite significantly, according to current research. It has been suggested by several major studies that even mild, chronic sleep loss may meaningfully affect cognitive performance. It is plausible that different individuals are affected to varying degrees depending on their neurology. It seems highly likely that maintaining consistent sleep patterns would produce measurable improvements in academic outcomes.
