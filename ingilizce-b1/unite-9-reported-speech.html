Reported Speech — Dolaylı Anlatım

Birinin söylediğini başkasına aktarırken kullanılır. Türkçe'deki "dedi ki, söyledi ki" yapısına benzer. Ama dikkat — İngilizce'de zamanlar geri kayar!

📌 Zaman kayması tablosu

Direct SpeechReported Speech
Present Simple: "I love coffee."Past Simple: She said she loved coffee.
Present Continuous: "I am working."Past Continuous: He said he was working.
Past Simple: "I saw him."Past Perfect: She said she had seen him.
will: "I will help."would: He said he would help.
can: "I can swim."could: She said she could swim.
must: "You must go."had to: He said I had to go.

📌 Zamir ve zaman değişimleri

said (that) — beyan
"I am tired." → She said (that) she was tired.
"We will come." → They said they would come.
"I have finished." → He said he had finished.
told / asked — soru ve komutlar
"Are you OK?" → She asked if I was OK.
"Close the door!" → He told me to close the door.
"Don't be late!" → She told me not to be late.
💡
TIP — said vs told
said → kime söylendiği belirtilmez veya "to" ile:
She said (that) she was tired. ✅ / She said to me that... ✅

told → mutlaka kişi belirtilir:
She told me (that) she was tired. ✅
❌ She told that she was tired. (kişi eksik!)

The Art of Disagreeing Well

sarı işaretli kelimeler bu ünitenin hedef yapıları ve kalıplarıdır.

Many young people say they find it difficult to disagree with teachers, parents or managers. Some admit they often stay silent, even when they know the other person is wrong.

Psychologist Dr Ayla Demir argued that this was deeply damaging — both to individuals and to society. She claimed that a culture of silent agreement prevented genuine problem-solving. "I would argue that respectful disagreement is not rude — it is essential," she told students at a recent conference. "It can be learned and practised."

She suggested using phrases like "I see what you mean, but..." or "That's an interesting perspective — have you considered...?" She warned that aggressive language almost always escalated rather than resolved conflict.

A student asked whether there was ever a situation where disagreeing simply was not worth it. Dr Demir replied that there was almost always a way to express a different view with dignity — if you chose your words carefully.

"The problem," she said, "is that people confuse disagreement with attack. They are completely different things." She admitted that even she had been guilty of this in her younger years. "I denied being wrong for years," she laughed. "Learning to say 'I was mistaken' was one of the hardest and most important lessons of my life."

Another student asked what single piece of advice she would give. "Listen first," she replied. "Always, always listen first."

1What percentage of young people said they found disagreement with authority difficult?

2What did Dr Demir claim about silent agreement?

3What phrases did she suggest using to disagree politely?

'I see what you mean, but...' or 'That's an interesting perspective — have you considered...?'

4What was her single piece of advice at the end?

'Listen first. Always, always listen first.'

1Dolaylı anlatım

Report what someone said to you recently (a teacher, friend, or family member). Write 4–5 sentences using reported speech. Start: My teacher told me that... / My friend said that she... / She admitted that...

2Tartışma sahne

Write a short dialogue (2–3 exchanges) where two people politely disagree. Then rewrite it in reported speech.

Dialogue: Ali: I think we should cancel the camping trip — the weather forecast looks terrible. Selin: I disagree. I think we can make it work if we bring proper equipment. Ali: Are you sure? It might be dangerous. Selin: I believe we'll be fine. Reported speech: Ali suggested that they should cancel the trip because the forecast looked terrible.
