In Theme 16, you will explore Life in the Universe and Future: astronaut life on space stations, space explorations, and predictions about life in the future. You will learn to talk about achievements that will be complete by a future time with the Future Perfect, describe ongoing activities up to a future point with the Future Perfect Progressive, and check predictions with question tags.

Future Perfect: achievements by a future point in time

1 · Notice
  • By 2035, engineers will have built a permanent base on the Moon.
  • The crew will have spent six months on the space station by the end of the mission.
  • She won't have finished her astronaut training before the next launch date.
  • Will humans have landed on Mars by 2045? — Many scientists think they will.

Which action happens first, and which point in time comes after it? What is the structure: will + have + …?

Rule

The Future Perfect (will have + past participle) describes an action that will be completed before a specific point in the future. It is often used with expressions like 'by 2035' or 'by the end of the mission'.

TRFuture Perfect (will have + fiilin 3. hâli), gelecekteki belirli bir zamandan ÖNCE tamamlanmış olacak bir eylemi anlatır. 'By 2035' (2035'e kadar), 'by the end of the mission' (görevin sonuna kadar) gibi ifadelerle sık kullanılır.

When?

We use it to talk about achievements, missions or changes that will be complete by a future date or event — very common when we predict the future of space exploration.

TRGelecekteki bir tarihe ya da olaya kadar tamamlanmış olacak başarıları, görevleri veya değişiklikleri anlatırken kullanılır — özellikle uzay araştırmaları ve gelecek tahminlerinde.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Scientists will have built a Moon base by 2035.
  • The rover will have explored the surface by next year.

Negative

  • Engineers won't have finished the new rocket by spring.
  • We won't have solved every problem by then.

Question

  • Will astronauts have landed on Mars by 2045?
  • Will this telescope have discovered new planets by 2040?

Short answers

  • Yes, they will. / No, they won't.
  • Yes, it will have. / No, it won't have.
Mistakes
  • By 2035, scientists will build a Moon base.By 2035, scientists will have built a Moon base.

    With 'by + a future time', use will have + past participle to show the action will be completed by then, not the simple future.

    TR'By + tarih' ile eylemin o tarihe kadar TAMAMLANMIŞ olacağını anlatmak için will değil will have + 3. hâl kullanılır.

  • Will astronauts landed on Mars by 2045?Will astronauts have landed on Mars by 2045?

    Don't forget 'have' in the question form: Will + subject + have + past participle.

    TRSoru formunda 'have' unutulmamalı: Will + özne + have + 3. hâl.

Future Perfect Progressive: how long something will have been happening

1 · Notice
  • By the time the mission ends, the crew will have been living in zero gravity for a year.
  • Next month, this rover will have been exploring the planet's surface for twelve months.
  • The engineers won't have been testing the new spacesuit for long before the launch.
  • How long will the telescope have been orbiting the Earth by 2040?

C Your turn

Write two sentences about the future of space exploration, saying how long something will have been happening by a certain time.

By 2040, the station will have been operating for …How long will … have been … by …?
Rule

The Future Perfect Progressive (will have been + verb-ing) emphasises the DURATION of an activity that continues up to a point in the future. It focuses on how long something has been happening, not just the result.

TRFuture Perfect Progressive (will have been + fiil-ing), gelecekteki bir noktaya kadar SÜREN bir eylemin uzunluğunu vurgular. Sonuçtan çok 'ne kadar süredir' devam ettiğine odaklanır.

When?

We use it to say how long a mission, project or habit will have been continuing by a certain future time.

TRBir görevin, projenin ya da alışkanlığın gelecekteki bir zamana kadar ne kadar süredir devam ediyor olacağını anlatırken kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • The crew will have been living on the station for a year by June.
  • This rover will have been exploring the surface for twelve months by then.

Negative

  • Engineers won't have been testing the suit for long before the launch.
  • We won't have been travelling for more than a week by that point.

Question

  • How long will the telescope have been orbiting the Earth by 2040?
  • Will they have been working on this project for over a decade?

Short answers

  • Yes, they will have been. / No, they won't have been.
Mistakes
  • By June, the crew will have living on the station for a year.By June, the crew will have been living on the station for a year.

    Don't forget 'been': will have BEEN + verb-ing.

    TR'been' unutulmamalı: will have BEEN + fiil-ing.

  • How long will the telescope orbiting the Earth by 2040?How long will the telescope have been orbiting the Earth by 2040?

    Include 'have been' in the question form.

    TRSoruda 'have been' eksik bırakılmamalı.

Compare

Future Perfect (will have + 3. hâl) bir eylemin SONUCUNA/tamamlanmasına odaklanır: 'They will have built the base.' Future Perfect Progressive ise SÜREYE odaklanır: 'They will have been building the base for five years.' İkisi de mümkün olduğunda, süre vurgulanacaksa Progressive tercih edilir.

Rule

Question tags check if something is true. If the main sentence is positive (will have + past participle), the tag is negative (won't + pronoun); if the main sentence is negative (won't have + past participle), the tag is positive (will + pronoun). The auxiliary verb in the tag is always 'will'.

TRQuestion tag'ler, cümlenin doğru olup olmadığını teyit etmek için kullanılır. Ana cümle olumluysa (will have + 3. hâl) tag olumsuzdur (won't + zamir); ana cümle olumsuzsa (won't have + 3. hâl) tag olumludur (will + zamir). Yardımcı fiil her zaman aynıdır: will.

When?

We use question tags to confirm a prediction or check someone's opinion — very useful in conversations and debates about the future.

TRBir tahmin veya varsayımı doğrulatmak, karşımızdaki kişinin fikrini sormak için kullanılır; özellikle gelecekle ilgili konuşma ve tartışmalarda kullanışlıdır.

Life Aboard the Space Station

My name is Commander Lena Voss, and I have been living on the International Research Station for four months. By the end of this mission, I will have spent almost a year of my life in zero gravity.

Every morning starts the same way. I check my spacesuit, talk to mission control, and review the day's tasks. Without gravity, even simple things like eating breakfast or brushing your teeth become a small adventure — food floats if you're not careful!

In the afternoon, we usually run experiments. Right now, we are growing plants to see if a colony on the Moon could one day grow its own food. By the time I return to Earth, our team will have completed over two hundred experiments.

Exercise is extremely important here. Without it, our muscles and bones become weak in zero gravity, so we exercise for two hours every day. By the end of my mission, I will have been exercising on this station for almost a thousand hours.

In the evening, I like to look out of the window at Earth. It's easy to forget how small our planet looks from up here. I always remind the younger astronauts: by the time your first mission ends, you will have seen more sunrises than most people see in a lifetime — the station orbits the Earth every ninety minutes!

Life here isn't always easy, but I wouldn't change it. When people ask if it's worth it, I always say the same thing: humans will have learned enough about long space missions by the 2030s to finally send astronauts to Mars — and I want to be part of that story.

Our Mars Colony Project

For our class project this term, we designed a hypothetical colony on Mars. First, we had to decide who would live there: scientists, engineers, doctors, and farmers who could grow food in a sustainable way.

We predicted that by 2100, the first colony will have grown from just a few hundred people to a small, functioning town. Our group decided the colony would need its own sustainable energy system, because sending fuel from Earth would be far too expensive.

One of the biggest challenges was breathing. Mars doesn't have enough oxygen, so every building needs a special system. We imagined that by the time the colony celebrates its fiftieth anniversary, engineers will have developed advanced technology to make the air completely safe, even outside the main buildings.

We also wrote short scripts for our presentation. In one scene, a scientist explains a new breakthrough in growing food on Mars; in another, two astronauts argue about whether humans should build more colonies or focus on protecting Earth instead.

Working on this project taught us that interplanetary travel isn't just about rockets and spacesuits — it's also about people, teamwork, and difficult decisions. We will have learned a lot more about ourselves than about Mars by the time we finish presenting our project to the class!

Life in 2050: A Class Debate

Debate the motion: 'By 2050, technology will have made our lives better, not worse.' One team argues for, the other against, using predictions with the Future Perfect.

Useful language

By 2050, artificial intelligence will have …I don't agree — we won't have solved …Won't people have lost their jobs by then?In my opinion, scientists will have made life easier because …

Adımlar

  1. Split into two teams: 'For' and 'Against' the motion.
  2. In your team, brainstorm three arguments using the Future Perfect.
  3. Choose one speaker to open the debate for each team.
  4. Take turns giving arguments and short responses to the other team.
  5. Vote as a class on which team gave the most convincing arguments.
DestekProvide sentence starters and a list of topics (jobs, health, environment, space travel) for less confident speakers.
HedefThree arguments per team, using the Future Perfect at least twice each.
İleriConfident speakers add a question tag to challenge the other team directly.
Writing90-120 words

My Space Colony Project Plan

Write a project plan for a hypothetical space colony (on the Moon, Mars, or elsewhere). Describe where it is, who will live there, and what the colony will have achieved by a chosen future date. This is preparation for your performance task.

Plan

  1. Choose your colony's location and give it a name.
  2. List who will live there and what their jobs will be.
  3. Write at least three goals the colony will have achieved by your chosen date, using the Future Perfect.
  4. Add one sentence about the biggest challenge the colony will face.

A Message from the Future

Imagine you are an astronaut or scientist writing a short message back to Earth from the year 2060. Describe what will have changed in space exploration and everyday life by then.

Plan

  1. Decide who you are (astronaut, scientist, or futuristic innovator) and where you are writing from.
  2. Choose three changes that will have happened by 2060.
  3. Write your message using the Future Perfect and at least one question tag.
  4. Read your message aloud to check it sounds natural.
