TRSimple Present; okulda düzenli olarak olan şeyleri, ders programını ve gerçekleri anlatmak için kullanılır. He/she/it ile fiile -s/-es eklenir. Olumsuzda don't/doesn't + yalın fiil; soruda Do/Does başa gelir.

We use it to talk about the school timetable, class rules, and daily routines that don't change.

TRDers programını, sınıf kurallarını ve değişmeyen günlük rutinleri anlatırken kullanırız.

C Your turn

Write three rules for your own class: one with must/have to, one with mustn't, and one with don't have to.

In our class, we must …We mustn't …We don't have to …
Rule

Must/have to show something is necessary or an obligation. Mustn't shows something is forbidden — do not do it. Don't have to shows there is no obligation — it is your choice.

TRMust/have to bir şeyin zorunlu olduğunu anlatır. Mustn't bir şeyin yasak olduğunu, yani yapılmaması gerektiğini anlatır. Don't have to ise zorunluluk olmadığını, seçim olduğunu anlatır.

When?

We use these to talk about school rules: what students must do, what is forbidden, and what is optional.

TROkul kurallarını anlatırken kullanılır: öğrencilerin yapması gerekenler, yasak olanlar ve seçime bağlı olanlar.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Students must wear a uniform.
  • We have to be punctual.

Negative

  • You mustn't run in the corridor.
  • We don't have to come on Saturdays.

Question

  • Do we have to wear a uniform on sports day?

Short answers

  • Yes, we do. / No, we don't have to.
Mistakes
  • You don't must run in the corridor.You mustn't run in the corridor.

    Use mustn't for prohibition, not don't must.

    TRYasak için mustn't kullanılır, don't must değil.

  • We must to hand in homework.We have to hand in homework.

    Don't add 'to' after must: must + base verb.

    TRhave to'dan sonra 'to' fiil ile birlikte kullanılır ama must'tan sonra 'to' gelmez.

Compare

Mustn't (yasak, yapma!) ile don't have to (zorunlu değil, istersen yapma) karıştırılmamalı: 'You mustn't run' = koşmak yasak. 'You don't have to run' = koşmak zorunda değilsin, istersen yürü.

Must vs have to

1 · Notice
  • Teacher: You must hand in your project by Friday — it's a class rule.
  • Student: I know, I have to finish it tonight because my parents want me to.
  • The school rules say students must be punctual.
  • I have to leave early today for a doctor's appointment.

Which one comes from a rule (the school/a law)? Which one comes from the speaker's own decision?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

musthave torulepersonal

B Build sentences

  2. mum says I tidy my room tonight
    Model answer

  3. exam rule you switch off your phone
    Model answer

  4. the doctor says I rest for two days

C Your turn

Write two sentences about your own life: one about a rule at your school (use must) and one about something you personally need to do (use have to).

At my school, students must …This week, I have to …
Rule

Must and have to both mean 'it is necessary', but must usually comes from the speaker, an official rule, or a law; have to usually comes from an outside obligation, like someone else's instruction. In everyday speech, they are often used the same way.

TRMust ve have to ikisi de 'zorunlu' anlamına gelir, ama must genellikle konuşan kişiden, resmî bir kuraldan ya da kanundan gelir; have to ise genellikle dışarıdan gelen bir zorunluluğu (başka birinin isteğini) anlatır. Günlük konuşmada çoğu zaman aynı şekilde kullanılırlar.

When?

We choose must for official school rules and strong personal opinions; we choose have to for obligations that come from someone or something else.

TRResmî okul kuralları ve güçlü kişisel görüşler için must seçilir; başkasından ya da bir durumdan gelen zorunluluklar için have to seçilir.

A Day at Our International School

My name is Mert and I'm a Year 10 student at an international school in İzmir. Our school day starts at 8:30, so I have to be punctual every morning. The caretaker, Mr Demir, opens the gate at 7:30, and by 8:25 the corridor is full of students.

At 8:30, we go to the assembly. The headteacher, Ms Whitfield, talks about the news of the week and important events, like sports day or a national day celebration. After assembly, we go to our classrooms.

We must wear a uniform, and we mustn't use our phones during lessons. If we have a problem, we can talk to the school counsellor, Mrs Aydoğan. She is very kind and always has time for students.

At break time, we go to the canteen or the library. The librarian, Mr Costa, helps us find books for our projects. We have to hand in our homework before 4 pm, so I usually work in the library after lessons.

I like my school because everyone is respectful, and there is always something interesting happening — a drama club, a football match, or a celebration for a national day!

Republic Day at School

Every year on the 29th of October, our school celebrates Republic Day. It is one of the most important national days in Türkiye, so we must prepare for it carefully.

In the morning, all students and teachers join a special assembly. We sing the national anthem, and the headteacher gives a short speech about the history of the day.

After the assembly, there is a school parade in the yard. Some students carry flags, and the drama club performs a short play about the celebration. Everyone claps for the performers!

In the afternoon, we don't have normal lessons. Instead, classes prepare posters and presentations about the day. My class made a poster with photos and facts, and we have to present it to the other students.

At the end of the day, there is a small prize-giving ceremony for the best posters. It's a busy day, but it's also one of my favourite days at school.

Useful language

Do you have to wear a uniform?Can you use your phone at break time?Must you be punctual every day?Yes, I do. / No, I don't. / Yes, I can. / No, I can't.

Adımlar

  1. Look at your worksheet: 6 sentences about school rules (e.g. '…wears a uniform every day').
  2. Stand up and ask a classmate a question to check if the sentence is true for them.
  3. If it's true, write their name. If not, ask another classmate.
  4. Try to find a different name for each sentence.
  5. Sit down and share two interesting answers with the class.
DestekGive students the question forms in writing before they start.
Hedef6 questions, mingling for about 10 minutes.
İleriFast finishers add one more true sentence about their own school rules and ask it too.

May I Ask? — Permission Role-Play

With a partner, act out three short situations at school where one of you is a student asking for permission and the other is a teacher answering.

Useful language

Can I …, please?Yes, of course you can.I'm sorry, you can't … because …Can you help me with …, please?

Adımlar

  1. Read the three situation cards (going to the library, borrowing a pen, leaving the classroom).
  2. Decide who is Student A (asks for permission) and who is Teacher B (answers).
  3. Act out each situation using Can I…? or Can you…? and a polite answer.
  4. Swap roles and do it again with new situations.
  5. Perform your favourite situation for another pair.
DestekProvide sentence starters on cards for lower-level students.
Hedef3 role-play situations per pair.
İleriStudents invent one extra situation of their own to role-play.
Writing60-80 words

My School Rules Poster

Write the text for a poster titled 'Our Class Rules' for new students at your school. Include at least two rules with must/have to and two with mustn't/don't have to.

Plan

  1. Choose a title for your poster.
  2. List 4-5 rules using must, have to, mustn't and don't have to.
  3. Add one sentence explaining why the rules are important.
  4. Check your grammar and punctuation.

A Scene for Our School Life Play

Write a short scene (a dialogue with 6-8 lines) for the class performance assignment: 'School Life' drama play. Include two characters (for example, a new student and a headteacher, or a student and a counsellor) and use at least two of this theme's grammar points.

Plan

  1. Choose your two characters and where the scene happens (e.g. the headteacher's office, the corridor).
  2. Decide what the problem or question in the scene is (e.g. asking for permission, explaining a rule).
  3. Write 6-8 lines of dialogue.
  4. Read it aloud with a partner and time it — it should take about 30 seconds.
