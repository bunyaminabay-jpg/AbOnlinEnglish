In Theme 8, you will explore Life in the Universe: the planets and satellites in our solar system, interesting facts about each planet, and what makes Earth so special. You will learn to talk about general truths with the Zero Conditional and real future possibilities with the First Conditional (if/unless).

Konular

  • Planets and satellites in the solar system
  • Facts about planets
  • The Earth as a planet

Dil yapıları

  • Zero conditional
  • First conditional (if / unless)

Anlayabileceğim

  • A text about the planets in our solar system
  • A text about Earth as a special, habitable planet
  • An interview with a space scientist about planets
  • A conversation between two students planning a solar system project

Söyleyebileceğim

  • Talk about facts and features of different planets
  • Ask and answer questions about space exploration
  • Role-play an astronaut describing a space mission
  • Present facts about your chosen planet to the class

Yazabileceğim

Zero conditional: facts about planets and space

1 · Notice
  • If a planet is close to the Sun, its temperature usually is high.
  • Water freezes if the temperature falls below 0°C.
  • If there is no atmosphere, sound doesn't travel.
  • Objects weigh less if the gravity is weaker.

What tense do we use in both parts of these sentences? What kind of facts do they describe?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Look at the Notice sentences again. Complete the rule with the words in the box.

B Build sentences

Use the cues to write Zero Conditional sentences about facts in space. Look at the example first.

  1. a planet be close to the Sun temperature / be high

    ✓ If a planet is close to the Sun, its temperature is high.

  2. temperature fall below 0°C water / freeze
    Model answer

    If the temperature falls below 0°C, water freezes.

  3. there not be / an atmosphere sound / not travel
    Model answer

    If there isn't an atmosphere, sound doesn't travel.

  4. gravity be weak objects / weigh less
    Model answer

    If gravity is weak, objects weigh less.

  5. you stand on the Moon you / weigh less
    Model answer

    If you stand on the Moon, you weigh less.

  6. a planet be far from the Sun it / be very cold
    Model answer

    If a planet is far from the Sun, it is very cold.

C Your turn

Write two Zero Conditional sentences about facts you know about planets or space.

If a planet …If there is no …
Rule

We use the Zero Conditional (If + Present Simple, Present Simple) to talk about general truths, scientific facts, and things that are always true — for example, facts about planets and space.

TRZero Conditional (Sıfırıncı Koşul Cümlesi), genel doğruları, bilimsel gerçekleri ve her zaman doğru olan şeyleri anlatmak için kullanılır — örneğin gezegenler ve uzayla ilgili gerçekler. If + Geniş Zaman, Geniş Zaman yapısı kullanılır.

When?

We use it when a result always happens under the same condition — like a scientific law about planets, gravity, or temperature.

TRBir koşul her zaman aynı sonucu verdiğinde kullanılır — gezegenler, yerçekimi ya da sıcaklıkla ilgili bilimsel bir kural gibi.

3 · Form

Positive

  • If a planet is close to the Sun, its temperature is high.
  • Water freezes if the temperature falls below 0°C.

Negative

  • If there isn't an atmosphere, sound doesn't travel.
  • Objects don't weigh the same if the gravity isn't the same.

Question

  • What happens if a planet has no atmosphere?

Short answers

  • It has no weather, and sound doesn't travel there.
Mistakes
  • If a planet is close to the Sun, its temperature will be high.If a planet is close to the Sun, its temperature is high.

    Don't use 'will' in the Zero Conditional — use the Present Simple in both clauses.

    TRZero Conditional'da 'will' kullanılmaz — her iki cümlecikte de Geniş Zaman kullanılır.

  • If the temperature fall below 0°C, water freezes.If the temperature falls below 0°C, water freezes.

    Remember -s/-es with a singular subject like 'temperature' in the if-clause.

    TRIf cümleciğinde özne tekilse (temperature gibi) fiile -s/-es eklenir.

First conditional: real possibilities with if and unless

1 · Notice
  • If astronauts travel to Mars, they will need special spacesuits.
  • We won't survive on the Moon unless we bring oxygen.
  • If scientists discover water on a planet, they will look for signs of life.
  • Unless the spacecraft has enough fuel, it won't reach its destination.

What tense is used after if/unless? What tense is used in the main clause? What does 'unless' mean?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Look at the Notice sentences again. Complete the rule with the words in the box.

Present Simplewill + base verbif...notfuture

Well done! You found the rule yourself. Now let's use it.

B Build sentences

Use the cues to write First Conditional sentences about space exploration. Look at the example first.

  1. astronauts travel to Mars they / need special spacesuits

    ✓ If astronauts travel to Mars, they will need special spacesuits.

  2. astronauts land on Mars they / collect rock samples
    Model answer

    If astronauts land on Mars, they will collect rock samples.

  3. we not / wear a spacesuit we / not survive in space
    Model answer

    If we don't wear a spacesuit, we won't survive in space.

  4. unless / the crew have enough oxygen they / not / complete the mission
    Model answer

    Unless the crew has enough oxygen, they won't complete the mission.

  5. the rocket launch on time it / reach orbit by Friday
    Model answer

    If the rocket launches on time, it will reach orbit by Friday.

  6. unless / scientists find a habitable planet humans / not / live there
    Model answer

    Unless scientists find a habitable planet, humans won't live there.

C Your turn

Write two First Conditional sentences about a future space mission or exploring another planet: one with if, one with unless.

If astronauts …Unless we …
Rule

We use the First Conditional (If + Present Simple, will + base verb) to talk about real, possible situations in the future and their results. Unless means 'if...not' and introduces a condition that must not happen for the result to be true.

TRFirst Conditional (Birinci Koşul Cümlesi), gelecekte gerçekleşmesi mümkün, gerçek durumları ve sonuçlarını anlatmak için kullanılır: If + Geniş Zaman, will + yalın fiil. Unless, 'eğer …olmazsa' anlamına gelir ve sonucun gerçekleşmesi için olmaması gereken bir koşulu tanıtır.

When?

We use it to talk about real future possibilities in space exploration — what will happen if (or unless) certain conditions are met.

TRUzay keşfiyle ilgili gelecekte gerçekleşmesi mümkün durumları anlatırken kullanılır — belirli koşullar gerçekleşirse (ya da gerçekleşmezse) ne olacağını anlatmak için.

3 · Form

Positive

  • If astronauts travel to Mars, they will need special spacesuits.
  • We will explore new planets if technology improves.

Negative

  • If we don't protect Earth's atmosphere, the climate will change.
  • Unless the crew has enough oxygen, they won't complete the mission.

Question

  • What will happen if scientists find water on Mars?

Short answers

  • They will look for signs of life there.
Mistakes
  • If astronauts will travel to Mars, they will need special spacesuits.If astronauts travel to Mars, they will need special spacesuits.

    Don't use 'will' in the if-clause — use the Present Simple.

    TRIf cümleciğinde 'will' kullanılmaz — Geniş Zaman kullanılır.

  • Unless we don't protect Earth's atmosphere, the climate will change.Unless we protect Earth's atmosphere, the climate will change.

    Unless already means 'if...not', so don't add another negative.

    TRUnless zaten 'eğer …olmazsa' anlamına geldiği için cümlede ikinci bir olumsuzluk eklenmez.

Compare

Zero Conditional her zaman doğru olan genel gerçekleri anlatır (Ice melts if the temperature rises — bu her zaman böyledir). First Conditional ise gelecekte gerçekleşmesi mümkün, özel bir durumu ve sonucunu anlatır (If we launch the rocket tomorrow, it will reach orbit — sadece bu durumda).

Our Solar System: A Family of Planets

Our solar system has one star, the Sun, and eight planets that revolve around it. The four planets closest to the Sun — Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars — are small and rocky. The four planets farther away — Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune — are much bigger gas giants.

Every planet orbits the Sun, but each one takes a different amount of time. Mercury orbits the Sun in just 88 days because it is close to the Sun. Neptune, the farthest planet, takes about 165 years!

Many planets also have satellites, or moons. Saturn has more than 80 moons, and its beautiful rings are made of ice and rock. Earth has only one moon, but it is very important — it affects our ocean tides.

If a planet is close to the Sun, its temperature is usually high. Venus, for example, is the hottest planet because its thick atmosphere traps heat. If a planet is far from the Sun, like Neptune, it is extremely cold.

Scientists use powerful telescopes and spacecraft to explore these planets. If we send astronauts to Mars one day, they will learn much more about the red planet and its surface.

Earth: Our Special Planet

Earth is the third planet from the Sun, and as far as we know, it is the only habitable planet in our solar system. But what makes Earth so special?

Earth has a thick atmosphere full of oxygen. If a planet doesn't have enough oxygen, humans and animals cannot breathe there. Earth's atmosphere also protects us from harmful rays from the Sun.

Unless a planet has liquid water, life cannot survive easily. Earth has huge oceans, rivers and lakes — about 70% of its surface is covered with water. Mars, in comparison, only has ice at its poles.

Earth's distance from the Sun is also important. It is not too close, like hot Venus, and not too far, like cold Neptune. Scientists call this the 'habitable zone'.

If astronauts explore other planets in the future, they will probably agree that Earth is still the best place for life. That's why it's so important to take care of our planet.

2 While listening

Transkripti göster

ReporterThank you for talking to us today! Can you tell us which planet is the biggest in our solar system?

ScientistOf course! Jupiter is the biggest planet. It's a huge gas giant — more than 300 Earths could fit inside it!

ReporterWow! And which planet is closest to the Sun?

ScientistThat's Mercury. If a planet is close to the Sun, like Mercury, its temperature changes a lot between day and night.

ReporterInteresting! Does every planet have a moon?

ScientistNo, it doesn't. Mercury and Venus don't have any moons, but Saturn has more than 80!

ReporterWhat happens if a spacecraft doesn't have enough fuel?

ScientistUnless a spacecraft has enough fuel, it won't reach its destination. That's why scientists plan missions very carefully.

ReporterOne last question — will humans live on another planet one day?

ScientistMaybe! If we find a habitable planet with water and oxygen, humans might live there in the future.

SpeakingPairs, then whole class10-12 dk

Guess the Planet

With a partner, take turns giving clues about a planet (size, distance from the Sun, temperature, moons) while your partner guesses which planet it is.

Useful language

This planet is…It orbits the Sun in…If you visit this planet, you will…Is it … ?

Adımlar

  1. Choose a planet and write 3 clues about it (without naming it).
  2. Give your first clue to your partner.
  3. Your partner guesses the planet; if they are wrong, give another clue.
  4. Swap roles and repeat with a different planet.
  5. Play again with 2 more planets each.
DestekGive students a fact card with key numbers (distance, moons, temperature) for each planet.
Hedef3 planets guessed correctly per pair.
İleriFast finishers add one Zero Conditional fact for each planet (e.g. 'If you stand here, you weigh less.').
SpeakingPairs10-12 dk

Mission to Mars: Astronaut Interview

One of you is a reporter and one is an astronaut who has just returned from a mission to Mars. Role-play a short interview about the mission, using the First Conditional to talk about future missions.

Useful language

What will happen if…?Unless we…, we won't…If we go back to Mars, we will…It was…

Adımlar

  1. Decide who is the reporter and who is the astronaut.
  2. The astronaut thinks of 3 facts about the Mars mission (what they saw, found, or did).
  3. The reporter prepares 3 questions, including at least one with if/unless.
  4. Act out the interview.
  5. Swap roles and do it again for a different planet.
DestekGive reporters a list of question starters (What did you…? What will happen if…?).
Hedef3 questions and answers per pair.
İleriFast finishers record their interview idea as notes for their solar system project script.
Writing60-80 words

Planet Fact File

Plan

  1. Choose your planet and research 4 key facts.
  2. Organise your facts: distance, size, temperature, moons.
  3. Write one Zero Conditional sentence about your planet.
  4. Check your grammar and punctuation.
