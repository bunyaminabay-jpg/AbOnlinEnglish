In Theme 10, you will explore Classroom Life and Learning with Technology: the tools we use in class, how apps and online platforms help us learn, and what future classrooms might look like. You will learn to talk about tools and processes with the passive voice (future tenses and modals), talk about doing things alone with reflexive pronouns, and link your ideas clearly with connecting words for cause, effect, contrast and time.

Passive voice: future tenses and modals

1 · Notice
  • New tablets will be given to every student next year.
  • Homework can be uploaded to the platform any time.
  • All devices must be charged before the lesson.
  • In future classrooms, some lessons will be taught by robot assistants too.

In these sentences, who or what does the action? What form of the verb comes after will be / can be / must be?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

will be + past participlemodal + be + past participlebyaction

B Build sentences

Use the cues to write passive sentences about classroom technology. Look at the example first.

  1. new laptops give students / next term

    ✓ New laptops will be given to students next term.

  2. the software must / update every month
    Model answer

    The software must be updated every month.

  3. homework can / upload to the platform
    Model answer

    Homework can be uploaded to the platform.

  4. the whiteboard should / clean after each lesson
    Model answer

    The whiteboard should be cleaned after each lesson.

  5. ? / all devices charge before class
    Model answer

    Will all devices be charged before class?

  6. classroom robots may / use in the next ten years
    Model answer

    Classroom robots may be used in the next ten years.

C Your turn

Write three sentences about the technology in your future classroom using will be / can be / must be + past participle.

In my future classroom, lessons will be…Homework can be…Devices must be…
Rule

We use the passive with future tenses and modals to focus on the action itself, not on who does it. Form: will be / modal (can, must, should, may) + be + past participle.

TRGelecek zamanda veya modal fiillerle edilgen çatı, eylemi yapan kişiden çok eylemin kendisini vurgulamak için kullanılır. Yapı: will be / modal (can, must, should, may) + be + fiilin 3. hâli (past participle).

When?

We use it when who does the action is unknown or unimportant, especially for rules, instructions, and future developments about technology.

TRKimin yaptığı önemli değilse ya da bilinmiyorsa, kural/plan/talimat anlatırken (özellikle teknoloji kuralları, kurallar, gelecekteki gelişmeler) kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • New tablets will be given to every student.
  • The software must be updated.

Negative

  • Personal devices won't be allowed in exams.
  • The files shouldn't be deleted.

Question

  • Will the tablets be collected at the end of the lesson?
  • Can the homework be uploaded late?

Short answers

  • Yes, it will. / No, it won't.
  • Yes, it can. / No, it can't.
Mistakes
  • The tablets will given to students.The tablets will be given to students.

    Don't forget 'be' after will: will be + past participle.

    TRwill'den sonra 'be' unutulmamalı: will be + V3.

  • Homework can uploaded any time.Homework can be uploaded any time.

    Modals also need 'be': modal + be + past participle.

    TRModal fiillerden sonra da 'be' gerekir: modal + be + V3.

Compare

Etken (active) cümlede eylemi yapan özne başta olur: 'The school will give new tablets.' Edilgen (passive) cümlede eylemi alan şey özne olur: 'New tablets will be given (by the school).' Kim yaptığı önemsizse ya da belliyse 'by' genelde atılır.

Reflexive pronouns

1 · Notice
  • Students can teach themselves new topics with online videos.
  • I fixed the connection problem myself.
  • The robot can't repair itself — a technician has to help.
  • We organised the whole technology project ourselves.

What do these -self / -selves words show? Who do they refer back to?

C Your turn

Write two sentences about things you did yourself with technology (for example, fixing a device, learning an app, or making a presentation).

I taught myself how to…We made … ourselves.
Rule

Reflexive pronouns show that the person doing the action and the person affected by it are the same: myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves.

TRDönüşlü zamirler (reflexive pronouns), eylemi yapan kişi ile eylemden etkilenen kişinin aynı olduğunu gösterir: myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves.

When?

We use them to emphasise that someone did something without help, or with certain verbs that need an object referring back to the subject (teach, enjoy, hurt, repair, etc.).

TRBir şeyi başkasının yardımı olmadan yaptığımızı vurgulamak için ya da 'kendine' anlamı taşıyan fiillerle (enjoy, teach, hurt, repair vb.) kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • I learned the app myself.
  • The device restarts itself automatically.

Negative

  • The robot doesn't repair itself.
  • They didn't organise the project themselves — a teacher helped.

Question

  • Did you set up the software yourself?

Short answers

  • Yes, I did it myself.
Mistakes
  • She teach herself English with an app.She teaches herself English with an app.

    Add -s/-es to the verb with he/she/it; the reflexive pronoun doesn't change the verb form.

    TRhe/she/it ile fiile -s/-es eklenmeli; dönüşlü zamir fiilin sonuna eklenmez.

  • We fixed the tablet ourself.We fixed the tablet ourselves.

    Use 'ourselves' with a plural subject like we, not 'ourself'.

    TRÇoğul özne (we) ile 'ourselves' kullanılır, 'ourself' değil.

Connecting words (cause, effect, contrast, time)

1 · Notice
  • Because the Wi-Fi was down, we couldn't use the tablets.
  • The projector broke, so we used the whiteboard instead.
  • Although technology helps us learn, it can also distract us.
  • After the teacher uploads the homework, students download it at home.

Which words give a reason (cause)? Which show a result (effect)? Which show a contrast? Which show time order?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Look at the Notice sentences again. Complete the rule with the words in the box.

because (cause)so (effect)although (contrast)after (time)

Well done! You found the rule yourself. Now let's use it.

B Build sentences

Join the two ideas with the connecting word given. Look at the example first.

  1. the battery died because I couldn't charge my tablet

    ✓ I couldn't charge my tablet because the battery died.

  2. the app crashed so we lost our project file
    Model answer

    The app crashed, so we lost our project file.

  3. technology is useful although it can be distracting
    Model answer

    Although technology is useful, it can be distracting.

  4. you download the app before you can join the online lesson
    Model answer

    You must download the app before you can join the online lesson.

  5. the robot finishes the task when it will send a message
    Model answer

    When the robot finishes the task, it will send a message.

C Your turn

Write two sentences about technology in your classroom using two different connecting words (one for cause/effect, one for contrast/time).

Because…Although……, so…After…
Rule

Connecting words join two ideas: because/so (cause-effect), although/but (contrast), after/before/when (time order). They make our sentences clearer and our writing more fluent.

TRBağlaçlar iki fikri birbirine bağlar: because/so (sebep-sonuç), although/but (zıtlık), after/before/when (zaman sırası). Cümlenin anlamını netleştirmek ve daha akıcı yazmak için kullanılır.

When?

We use them to explain the cause, effect, contrast or time order of events, especially in project presentations and written texts.

TRBir olayın nedenini, sonucunu, bir zıtlığı veya olayların sırasını açıklarken, özellikle proje sunumları ve yazılı metinlerde kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Because the Wi-Fi was slow, the video didn't load.
  • The tablets are new, so they work very fast.

Question

  • Why couldn't you finish the online quiz?
Mistakes
  • Because the projector broke. We used the whiteboard.Because the projector broke, we used the whiteboard.

    A 'because' clause at the start of a sentence is joined to the main clause with a comma, not a full stop.

    TR'Because' cümlesi başta kullanıldığında virgülle ana cümleye bağlanır, ayrı cümle olmaz.

  • Although technology helps us learn, but it can also distract us.Although technology helps us learn, it can also distract us.

    Don't use 'although' and 'but' together in the same sentence.

    TR'Although' ve 'but' aynı cümlede birlikte kullanılmaz.

Compare

Although (zıtlık: 'her ne kadar…') ile because (sebep: 'çünkü') anlamca birbirinin tersidir — 'Although it rained, we went out' ile 'Because it rained, we stayed home' farklı sonuçlar verir.

A Tour of a Smart Classroom

Our school opened a new smart classroom this year, and I was one of the first students to try it. When you walk in, the first thing you notice is the huge interactive whiteboard at the front. It isn't just a screen — you can touch it, write on it, and even play educational games on it.

Every student has a small tablet connected to the classroom's wireless network. Worksheets are never printed on paper anymore; instead, they are downloaded from the school's online platform at the start of the lesson. If a device runs out of battery, there is a charger under every desk.

During our science lesson, the teacher used the projector to show a 3D model of the solar system. It looked almost like a hologram floating above the desks! Although the equipment is expensive, our headteacher says it will be upgraded again next year with new, more innovative tools.

Of course, technology sometimes causes problems. Last week, the Wi-Fi went down, so we couldn't upload our homework on time. Because of this, our teacher taught us to always keep a backup copy. Students must also charge their tablets themselves every night — nobody does it for us!

I really enjoy learning in the smart classroom because lessons feel more interactive and modern. However, I still think a good teacher is more important than any device, no matter how advanced it is.

My Two Weeks with a Learning App

Two weeks ago, my English teacher recommended a new vocabulary app for our class project. At first, I found it a little confusing, but I taught myself how to use it in about ten minutes by watching a short video.

Every evening, I open the app on my phone and review ten new words. The app updates my progress automatically, so I can see exactly which words I need to practise again. Because the app tracks my mistakes, it gives me more practice with the words I find difficult.

Although I enjoy the app, it isn't perfect. Sometimes it needs an internet connection, so I can't use it on the school bus where there is no Wi-Fi. When this happens, I use paper flashcards instead — an old method that still works well!

For our technology project, our group decided to create a short guide for other students about using learning apps responsibly. We explained that apps are a great tool, but students shouldn't only rely on a screen; talking to real people is important too.

After two weeks, I can say the app really helped me learn new words faster. I will keep using it, but I will also keep talking, reading and writing in English every day — technology should support learning, not replace it.

A Guide to Using Classroom Technology Responsibly

Write a short guide for new students titled 'Using Our Classroom Technology Responsibly'. Include at least two rules with the passive voice (will be / must be / can be + past participle) and one sentence with a connecting word.

Plan

  1. Choose a title for your guide.
  2. List 3-4 rules using the passive voice with future tenses or modals.
  3. Add one sentence with a connecting word (because, so, although, after).
  4. Check your grammar and punctuation.

Our Ideal Technology Classroom — Project Script

Write the script for the performance project: 'Technological Classroom Tools, Learning with Technology, and Future Classrooms.' Describe the ideal use of technology in the classroom, the roles of students and teachers, and the tools used. Write at least two lines for each role (student and teacher), using at least two of this theme's grammar points.

Plan

  1. Decide on the roles (for example, Teacher, Student, and maybe a Robot Assistant) and where the scene happens.
  2. List the tools each role uses (interactive whiteboard, tablet, app, hologram, robot…).
  3. Write 8-10 lines of dialogue between the roles, showing how technology helps learning.
  4. Read it aloud with your group and time it — it should take about 1-2 minutes.
