C Your turn

Write two sentences about your family's achievements using the present perfect passive or past perfect passive.

In my family, ... has been ...Before ..., ... had been ...
Rule

The passive voice with perfect tenses focuses on the action or its result rather than who does it. Structure: have/has/had/will have + been + past participle.

TREdilgen çatı (passive voice), mükemmel zamanlarla (perfect tenses) birlikte kullanıldığında, eylemi yapan kişi yerine eylemin sonucuna odaklanılır. Yapı: have/has/had/will have + been + fiilin 3. hali (past participle).

When?

We use it when it is not important or not known who did the action, or when the result itself is the focus (applications, decisions, plans).

TRKim yaptığı önemli değilse ya da bilinmiyorsa, ya da sonucun kendisi önemliyse (başvurular, kararlar, planlar) kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Her application has been accepted.
  • The plan had been finished before the meeting.

Negative

  • The letter hasn't been sent yet.
  • The decision hadn't been made before we arrived.

Question

  • Has the project been completed?
  • Had the plan been approved before the deadline?

Short answers

  • Yes, it has. / No, it hasn't.
  • Yes, it had. / No, it hadn't.
Mistakes
  • Her application has accepted.Her application has been accepted.

    Don't forget 'been' in the perfect passive.

    TREdilgen için 'been' eksik bırakılmamalı.

  • The plan was been finished.The plan had been finished.

    Don't use 'was been' — use have/has/had + been.

    TR'was been' yanlıştır; have/had + been kullanılır.

Rule

The Present Perfect Progressive (have/has been + verb-ing) describes actions that started in the past and are still continuing, or that have just stopped with a visible result now. It emphasises the duration or continuity of the action.

TRPresent Perfect Progressive (have/has been + fiil-ing), geçmişte başlayıp devam eden ya da yakın zamanda biten ve sonucu şu an görülen eylemleri anlatır; eylemin sürekliliğine ya da süresine vurgu yapar.

When?

We use it to talk about ongoing efforts towards a goal, habits, or recent actions whose effect is still felt now.

TRBir hedefe doğru süren çabaları, alışkanlıkları ya da yakın zamanda biten ama etkisi hâlâ hissedilen eylemleri anlatırken kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • She has been working hard.
  • They have been saving money for a trip.

Negative

  • He hasn't been sleeping well.
  • We haven't been practising enough.

Question

  • Has she been studying for the exam?
  • Have you been planning this for long?

Short answers

  • Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't.
  • Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
Mistakes
  • My mother has working towards her promotion.My mother has been working towards her promotion.

    Don't forget 'been' before the -ing form.

    TR'been' eksik bırakılmamalı.

  • I have been study for three hours.I have been studying for three hours.

    Add -ing to the main verb.

    TRFiile -ing eklenmeli.

Compare

Present Perfect Simple (have done) genellikle bitmiş bir eylemin sonucuna odaklanır; Present Perfect Progressive (have been doing) ise eylemin ne kadar süredir devam ettiğine odaklanır: 'I have written three pages' (sonuç) ile 'I have been writing for an hour' (süreç) farklıdır.

C Your turn

Write three sentences about your family's abilities and achievements using different forms of 'able to'.

... was/were able to ...... will be able to ...... has/have been able to ...
Rule

'Able to' expresses ability and, unlike can/could, can be used in any tense: is/are able to (present), was/were able to (past — often preferred for a single specific achievement), will be able to (future), has/have been able to (an achievement up to now).

TR'Able to' yetenek anlatır ve can/could'un aksine tüm zamanlarda kullanılabilir: is/are able to (şimdi), was/were able to (geçmiş, genellikle tek seferlik bir başarı için tercih edilir), will be able to (gelecek), has/have been able to (şimdiye kadar başarılmış bir şey).

When?

We use it to talk about future or 'up to now' abilities (where can/could don't exist), or to emphasise a single specific achievement in the past.

TRGelecekteki ya da şimdiye kadarki yetenek/başarıları anlatırken ya da geçmişte tek bir olayda başarılmış bir şeyi vurgularken kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • She was able to pass the exam.
  • They will be able to travel next year.

Negative

  • He wasn't able to finish on time.
  • We won't be able to attend the ceremony.

Question

  • Were you able to solve the problem?
  • Will she be able to join us?

Short answers

  • Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't.
  • Yes, she will. / No, she won't.
Mistakes
  • She will can speak English fluently.She will be able to speak English fluently.

    Don't say 'will can' — use 'will be able to'.

    TR'Will can' yanlıştır; 'will be able to' kullanılır.

  • He could pass the exam yesterday.He was able to pass the exam yesterday.

    For a single past achievement, we usually prefer 'was/were able to', not 'could'.

    TRGeçmişteki tek bir başarı için genellikle 'was/were able to' tercih edilir, 'could' değil.

Compare

Could genellikle geçmişte genel bir yeteneği anlatır ('I could swim when I was five'); tek seferlik özel bir başarı için 'was/were able to' daha doğrudur ('I was able to finish the marathon last year').

C Your turn

Write three sentences about your family's plans and habits using verbs followed by the gerund or infinitive.

Rule

Some verbs are followed by to + verb (infinitive): hope to, want to, decide to, plan to. Other verbs are followed by verb + -ing (gerund): enjoy, avoid, finish, practise. You need to learn which verbs take which form.

TRBazı fiillerden sonra to + fiil (mastar/infinitive) gelir (hope to, want to, decide to, plan to); bazı fiillerden sonra ise fiil + -ing (gerund/ulaç) gelir (enjoy, avoid, finish, practise). Hangi fiilin hangi yapıyı aldığını ezberlemek gerekir.

When?

We use these verb patterns to talk about family goals, plans and habits.

TRAile hedeflerini, planlarını ve alışkanlıklarını anlatırken bu fiil kalıplarını kullanırız.

My name is Elif and my family has many different dreams for the future. For our school project, we have been thinking about each person's aspirations and how we support each other.

We all support each other's dreams. My father says that with persistence and a little help from your family, almost anything is possible.

ÖyküGrandma, can I ask you some questions for my English project?

GrandmaOf course, dear. What do you want to know?

ÖyküHave you always wanted to be a teacher?

GrandmaActually, when I was young, I was able to study art at university, but my family needed help at home, so I became a teacher instead.

ÖyküDo you regret that decision?

GrandmaNot really. I've been teaching for over thirty years, and I've helped hundreds of students. I'm proud of that.

ÖyküIs there anything you still hope to do?

GrandmaYes! I've been planning to paint again since I retired. I'm determined to finish a whole collection this year.

ÖyküThat's wonderful. I believe in you, Grandma!

SpeakingPairs10-12 dk

Interview: My Family's Dreams

Interview your partner about one family member's future aspirations and dreams, then present what you learned to another pair, using the grammar from this theme.

Useful language

What does your ... hope to do in the future?Has your ... been working towards ...?My ... is interested in ...My ... is determined to ...

Adımlar

  1. Think of one family member and their dream or ambition.
  2. Take turns interviewing each other using at least three questions.
  3. Take notes on your partner's answers.
  4. Prepare a short summary using 'able to', 'hope to' or 'has been -ing'.
  5. Share your summary with another pair and compare family dreams.
DestekGive a list of question starters for lower-level students.
Hedef3 interview questions + short summary.
İleriFast finishers add one more family member and compare two people's dreams.
SpeakingGroups of 3-412-15 dk

Family Project Role-Play (Performance Prep)

In your project group, act out a short scene where each person plays a family member describing their future aspirations and how the others support them, as preparation for the theme's performance task.

Useful language

I hope to become ...My family has been supporting me by ...I'm determined to ...We believe in you!

Adımlar

  1. Choose which family member each person will play.
  2. Decide on each character's dream and one skill they need.
  3. Write 2-3 lines for each character.
  4. Rehearse the scene together, using this theme's grammar points.
  5. Perform your scene for another group and give each other feedback.
DestekProvide sentence frames for each grammar point (able to, hope to, has been -ing).
Hedef3-4 characters, 2-3 lines each.
İleriFast finishers add a line where a family member gives advice.

My Family's Aspirations

Write a paragraph describing the future aspirations and dreams of two people in your family. Explain what skills they need and how the family supports them.

Plan

  1. Choose two family members and their dreams.
  2. Note down one skill each person needs.
  3. Note down how the family supports them.
  4. Check your grammar and punctuation.
