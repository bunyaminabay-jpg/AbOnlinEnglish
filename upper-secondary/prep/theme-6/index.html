In Theme 6, you will explore Life in the World: the continents, countries and nationalities of our planet, how to shop for the food you like or need, and the food culture of different regions. You will learn to talk about experiences with the Present Perfect, check facts with question tags, compare places and dishes with superlative adjectives, and talk about food with countable/uncountable nouns and quantifiers.

C Your turn

Write three true sentences about your own life experiences with food or travel. Use the Present Perfect and include ever, never, already or yet.

I have never …I have already …I haven't … yet
Rule

We use the Present Perfect (have/has + past participle) for life experiences from the past that are connected to now, when we don't say exactly when they happened. Negative: haven't/hasn't + past participle. Question: Have/Has + subject + past participle?

TRPresent Perfect (have/has + fiilin 3. hâli), geçmişte olan ama şu anla bağlantılı olan ya da hayatta ne zaman olduğu önemli olmayan deneyimleri anlatmak için kullanılır. Olumsuzda haven't/hasn't, soruda Have/Has başa gelir.

When?

We use it to talk about travel experiences, food we have tried, and things that have happened in our lives, when the exact time isn't important.

TRSeyahat deneyimlerini, denenen yemekleri ve hayatta yaşanan şeyleri, ne zaman olduğu önemli olmadan anlatırken kullanırız.

3 · Form

Positive

  • I have visited three continents.
  • She has tried Japanese food.

Negative

  • We haven't been to South America yet.
  • He hasn't eaten octopus.

Question

  • Have you ever eaten street food?
  • Has she visited Africa?

Short answers

  • Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
  • Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't.
Mistakes
  • I have visit many countries.I have visited many countries.

    Use the past participle after have/has, not the base verb.

    TRHave/has'ten sonra fiilin 3. hâli (past participle) kullanılır, yalın hâl değil.

  • I have eaten sushi yesterday.I ate sushi yesterday.

    Use the Past Simple, not the Present Perfect, with a finished time expression like yesterday.

    TRYesterday gibi belirli bir zaman ifadesiyle Present Perfect değil, Past Simple kullanılır.

Compare

Ever ve never cümlenin ortasında, have/has'ten hemen sonra gelir: 'I have never tried it.' Already olumlu cümlede fiilden önce, yet ise genellikle olumsuz cümlenin ve sorunun sonunda kullanılır.

Rule

A question tag is a short question added to the end of a Present Perfect sentence, usually to check information. If the main sentence is positive, the tag is negative; if the main sentence is negative, the tag is positive. The tag always uses the same auxiliary verb, have/has.

TRQuestion tag, Present Perfect ile kurulan cümlenin sonuna eklenen kısa bir sorudur ve genellikle onay almak için kullanılır. Ana cümle olumluysa etiket olumsuz, ana cümle olumsuzsa etiket olumlu olur. Etikette her zaman aynı yardımcı fiil (have/has) kullanılır.

When?

We use question tags to check information or keep a conversation going, especially when talking about experiences.

TRBir bilgiyi doğrulamak ya da sohbeti sürdürmek için, özellikle deneyimlerden bahsederken kullanırız.

C Your turn

Write three superlative sentences comparing countries, continents or dishes you know: the biggest, the most interesting and one more of your choice.

… is the biggest …… is the most interesting …
Rule

Superlative adjectives show which thing is the most/least of a quality among three or more things, and always use 'the'. Short adjectives take -est (long → longest); a single vowel + consonant doubles the consonant (big → biggest); adjectives ending in -y change to -iest (easy → easiest). Long adjectives use 'the most' (delicious → the most delicious). Good, bad and far are irregular: best, worst, furthest.

TRSuperlative sıfatlar, üç veya daha fazla şey arasında en üstün olanı belirtmek için kullanılır ve başına 'the' gelir. Kısa sıfatlara -est eklenir (long → longest); tek ünlü + ünsüzle biten sıfatlarda son ünsüz ikilenir (big → biggest); y ile bitenlerde y, i olur (easy → easiest). Uzun sıfatlarda 'the most' kullanılır (delicious → the most delicious). good/bad/far düzensizdir: best/worst/furthest.

When?

We use superlatives to compare countries, continents, cities and dishes, to say which one is the most/least of something.

TRÜlkeleri, kıtaları, şehirleri ve yemekleri karşılaştırırken, en üstün olanı belirtmek için kullanılır.

  • Asia is the biggest continent.
  • This is the most popular dish in the region.

Negative

  • This isn't the spiciest dish on the menu.

  • What is the most delicious dish you've tried?

Short answers

  • It's Türkiye. / The manti is.
Mistakes
  • Asia is the most big continent.Asia is the biggest continent.

    Use -est, not 'the most', with short adjectives.

    TRKısa sıfatlarda 'the most' değil, -est kullanılır.

  • This is the deliciousest dish.This is the most delicious dish.

    Use 'the most', not -est, with long adjectives.

    TRUzun sıfatlarda -est değil, 'the most' kullanılır.

Compare

Comparative (-er / more) iki şeyi karşılaştırır: 'This dish is spicier than that one.' Superlative (the -est / the most) üç veya daha fazla şey arasından en üstünü seçer: 'This is the spiciest dish on the menu.'

Rule

Countable nouns can be counted one by one; they have singular and plural forms and use a/an in the singular (an apple, two apples). Uncountable nouns cannot be counted one by one; they usually have no plural form and don't use a/an (rice, milk, cheese).

TRSayılabilir isimler (countable) tek tek sayılabilir; tekil ve çoğul hâlleri vardır ve tekilken a/an ile kullanılırlar (an apple, two apples). Sayılamayan isimler (uncountable) tek tek sayılamaz; genellikle çoğul hâlleri yoktur ve a/an ile kullanılmazlar (rice, milk, cheese).

When?

We need to know which food words are countable and which are uncountable when we talk about recipes, shopping or food in general.

TRBir yemek tarifi, market alışverişi ya da yiyecek konuşurken hangi kelimenin sayılabilir hangisinin sayılamayan olduğunu bilmek gerekir.

C Your turn

Write four sentences about the food in your kitchen at home, using some, any, a lot of, a little and a few.

We have some …We don't have any …There's a lot of …
Rule

Some is used with countable and uncountable nouns in positive sentences and offers (Would you like some…?). Any is used in negative sentences and questions. Much is used with uncountable nouns, and many with plural countable nouns, mainly in negatives and questions. A lot of works with both, usually in positive sentences. A little (a small amount, uncountable) and a few (a small number, countable) show small quantities.

TRSome, olumlu cümlelerde ve tekliflerde (Would you like some…?) hem sayılabilir hem sayılamayan isimlerle kullanılır. Any, olumsuz cümlelerde ve sorularda kullanılır. Much sayılamayan isimlerle, many sayılabilir çoğul isimlerle, özellikle olumsuz ve sorularda kullanılır. A lot of her ikisiyle de, genelde olumlu cümlelerde kullanılır. A little (az miktar, sayılamayan) ve a few (az sayı, sayılabilir) küçük miktarları belirtir.

When?

We use quantifiers to talk about amounts, especially when shopping or describing the ingredients in a recipe.

TRMiktar hakkında konuşurken, özellikle alışveriş yaparken ya da bir tarifin malzemelerini anlatırken kullanılır.

Seven Continents, One Small World

Our planet has seven continents: Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Australia, Europe, North America and South America. Each continent has its own countries, nationalities, languages and flags, and together they make our world an amazing, varied place.

Asia is the biggest continent, and it also has the largest population — more people live there than on any other continent. China and India alone have over a billion people each! Africa is the second biggest continent, with over fifty different countries and hundreds of official languages.

Some continents have a lot of countries close together, sharing borders and cultures. In Europe, for example, you can travel from one country to another and hear a different official language after just a few hours. Other continents, like Australia, are made up of just one country and a group of islands nearby.

Every country has a flag, a capital city and a nationality that people are proud of. If you have ever met someone from another continent, you have probably learned something new about their country's culture, food or traditions.

This theme is your chance to go continent-hopping without leaving the classroom — to learn about nationalities, borders and populations, and to discover how people from different parts of the world live, shop and eat.

A Taste of the World: Street Food and Traditions

Have you ever tried street food in another country? In many cities around the world, small stalls and carts sell some of the most delicious and famous dishes — often cheaper and tastier than in a restaurant!

In Thailand, street vendors sell spicy noodle dishes and fresh fruit. In Mexico, you can find tacos with many different fillings. In Türkiye, simit and köfte are popular street food, and every region has its own traditional dish that local people are proud of.

Food culture isn't only about street food, though. It's also about how people shop for ingredients. In some countries, families go to a big grocery store once a week; in others, they visit a small local market every day for fresh vegetables, fruit and bread.

More and more people today also have special food preferences. Some are vegetarian, some choose gluten-free or dairy-free products, and shops in most big cities now have a special section for these ingredients so that everyone can find something they enjoy.

Trying a country's traditional dishes and street food is one of the best ways to understand its culture. That's why, in this theme's project, you and your classmates will create a booklet about a culinary adventure — exploring continents, countries and the wonderful food cultures of the world.

Guess My Country

Think of a country. Your partner asks yes/no questions with Present Perfect and comparative/superlative language to guess it, e.g. 'Have you ever been there?' or 'Is it the biggest country on its continent?'

Useful language

Have you ever visited this country?Is it in Europe / Asia / Africa…?Is it the biggest / smallest country in its continent?Yes, it is. / No, it isn't. / Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.

Adımlar

  1. Think of a country and write its name secretly on a small piece of paper.
  2. Your partner asks up to eight yes/no questions to guess the country.
  3. Answer only Yes/No — don't give the country away too early!
  4. Swap roles and play again with a new country.
  5. Share your two favourite questions with the class.
DestekGive students a list of ready-made question starters before they begin.
HedefTwo rounds per pair, up to eight questions each.
İleriFast finishers add one superlative fact about their country (e.g. 'It has the longest coastline in South America').
SpeakingPairs12-15 min

Shopping for Traditional Food — Role-Play

With a partner, act out a shopping scene at a market or shop. One of you is a customer with a food preference (vegetarian, gluten-free or dairy-free) shopping for a traditional dish from another culture; the other is the shop assistant. This role-play is preparation for your culinary adventure booklet.

Useful language

I'm vegetarian / gluten-free / dairy-free. Do you have…?We have some fresh…/ We don't have any… left.How much / how many… do you need?Have you ever tried…? It's the most popular dish here.

Adımlar

  1. Choose a country and a traditional dish you want to shop for.
  2. Decide your character: Customer A (with a food preference) or Shop Assistant B.
  3. Act out the scene: greet, ask for ingredients, talk about quantities and preferences.
  4. Use at least two grammar points from this theme in your dialogue.
  5. Perform your scene for another pair and get feedback.
DestekGive lower-level pairs a shopping list template with ingredient names already on it.
HedefOne full shopping scene per pair, performed for another pair.
İleriFast pairs add a moment where the shop assistant recommends a traditional dish and describes it with a superlative.

The World in Superlatives

Write a short text comparing three countries or continents you have researched. Use at least three superlative adjectives and one sentence with the Present Perfect about an experience.

Plan

  3. Add one sentence about something you have or haven't experienced.

My Culinary Adventure Booklet Page

Write one page of your culinary adventure booklet (performance task): describe a country, its traditional dish, and a shopping trip for that dish, including a food preference (vegetarian, gluten-free or dairy-free) and quantities of ingredients.

Plan
