In Theme 11, you will explore Personal Life and Well-Being: the human body and common illnesses, what well-being and human nature mean to us, and how we can build healthy habits for the future. You will learn to imagine different situations with the second conditional, express wishes and regrets with wish and if only, and describe feelings and situations with -ing/-ed adjectives.

Second Conditional: Imaginary Present and Future Situations

1 · Notice

  • She would sleep better if she didn't drink coffee at night.

C Your turn

Write three imaginary sentences about your own well-being using the second conditional. Think about diet, sleep, exercise or stress.

If I …, I would …I would feel better if …If I had more time, I …
Rule

We use the second conditional to talk about imaginary or unlikely situations in the present or future. The if-clause uses the past simple, and the main clause uses would + base verb.

TRİkinci koşul cümlesi (second conditional), şimdiki zamanda ya da gelecekte gerçek olmayan ya da olası olmayan hayalî durumları anlatmak için kullanılır. If cümleciğinde geçmiş zaman (past simple), ana cümlede would + yalın fiil kullanılır.

When?

We use it to talk about situations that are not true now or are unlikely to happen, such as imagining healthier habits or different choices.

TRŞu anki gerçekliğe uymayan ya da gerçekleşme ihtimali düşük durumlar hakkında konuşurken (örn. sağlıklı alışkanlıklar, hayaller) kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • If I ate more vegetables, I would feel healthier.
  • She would sleep better if she exercised.

Negative

  • If I weren't so stressed, I would sleep better.
  • We wouldn't feel tired if we drank more water.

Question

  • What would you do if you caught a cold?
  • If you had more free time, would you exercise more?

Short answers

  • Yes, I would. / No, I wouldn't.
Mistakes
  • If I would have more time, I would relax.If I had more time, I would relax.

    Don't use 'would' in the if-clause; use the past simple.

    TRIf cümleciğinde 'would' kullanılmaz, past simple kullanılır.

  • If she was tired, she will rest.If she was tired, she would rest.

    Use 'would', not 'will', in the main clause.

    TRAna cümlede 'will' değil 'would' kullanılır.

Compare

Second conditional (hayalî, gerçek olmayan: If I ate healthier, I would feel better.) ile first conditional (gerçek, olası: If I eat healthier, I will feel better.) karıştırılmamalı.

  • If only I had eaten breakfast this morning — now I feel weak.
  • If only we had more time to relax after school.
  • If only he would stop staying up so late.

Do these sentences talk about the present, the past, or both? What feeling do they express?

B Build sentences

Rewrite the situations using If only. Look at the example first.

  1. I have a headache. (now)

    ✓ If only I didn't have a headache!

  2. I didn't sleep enough last night. (past)
    Model answer

    If only I had slept enough last night!

  3. We don't have enough free time. (now)
    Model answer

    If only we had more free time!

  4. I ate junk food before the match. (past)
    Model answer

    If only I hadn't eaten junk food before the match!

  5. He is always so stressed. (now)
    Model answer

C Your turn

Write two If only sentences: one about something you wish were different now, and one about something you regret from the past.

If only I …If only I had …
Rule

If only expresses a stronger wish or regret than 'I wish'. We use If only + past simple for a present situation we want to change, and If only + past perfect (had + past participle) for a past regret.

TRIf only, 'I wish'den daha güçlü bir pişmanlık ya da dilek ifade eder. Şimdiki bir durumdan memnun değilsek If only + past simple; geçmişte olan bir şeyden pişmansak If only + past perfect (had + V3) kullanılır.

When?

We use it when we regret a present situation or something we did or didn't do in the past, for example about our health habits.

TRŞu anki durumumuzdan ya da geçmişte yaptığımız/yapmadığımız bir şeyden pişmanlık duyduğumuzda kullanılır — örneğin sağlık alışkanlıkları hakkında.

3 · Form

Positive

  • If only I had more energy today.
  • If only I had gone to bed earlier last night.

Negative

  • If only I didn't feel so tired.
  • If only I hadn't eaten so much junk food.
Mistakes
  • If only I have more time.If only I had more time.

    Use the past simple after 'if only' for a present wish, not the present simple.

    TRIf only'dan sonra şimdiki durum için past simple kullanılır, present simple değil.

  • If only I ate breakfast this morning. (dün sabah pişmanlık)If only I had eaten breakfast this morning.

    Use the past perfect (had + past participle) for a past regret.

    TRGeçmişteki bir pişmanlık için past perfect (had + V3) kullanılır.

Compare

'I wish' ve 'If only' benzer anlamlıdır ama 'If only' daha güçlü, daha duygusal bir pişmanlık/dilek anlatır: 'I wish I had more time' (nötr) vs 'If only I had more time!' (güçlü pişmanlık).

Wish + past simple expresses a wish about a present situation that is not true. With I/he/she/it, 'were' is considered more correct ('was' is also common in everyday speech).

TRWish + past simple, şimdiki zamanda gerçek olmayan bir durum için dilek/pişmanlık anlatır. I/he/she/it ile 'were' kullanılması daha doğru kabul edilir ('was' da günlük konuşmada kullanılabilir).

When?

We use it when we are not happy with a present situation and wish it were different, for example about energy, stress, or habits.

TRŞu anki durumumuzdan memnun olmadığımızda ve onun farklı olmasını dilediğimizde kullanılır — örneğin enerji, stres ya da alışkanlıklar hakkında.

'Wish' ve 'if only' benzerdir; 'if only' daha güçlü bir duygu anlatır. Ayrıca 'I wish' + past simple şu anki durum için, 'I wish' + past perfect (had + V3) geçmişteki pişmanlık için kullanılır.

TRSağlık, stres ve iyi olma hâli hakkında konuşurken, hem durumları hem de hisleri anlatmak için kullanılır.

The Day I Caught the Flu

Last Monday, I woke up with a headache and a sore throat. I knew something wasn't right, so I told my mum I felt terrible. "If only I hadn't stayed up so late last week," I thought — maybe I wouldn't have caught a cold so easily.

My mum took my temperature and said I had a fever. She called the school and then took me to see Dr. Kaya, our family doctor. In the waiting room, I saw a nurse checking another patient's blood pressure.

Dr. Kaya listened to my symptoms and said, "You have the flu. If you rested and drank plenty of water, you would recover faster." She gave me some treatment and told me to stay in bed for a few days.

I wish I had taken better care of myself before I got sick — eating more vegetables and sleeping enough hours. Now I understand why it is so important to prevent illness, not just treat it.

After four days of rest, I finally felt better. The whole experience was exhausting, but it also taught me a useful lesson about listening to my body and looking after my well-being.

Small Habits, Big Changes

For our end-of-theme project, our class decided to create a Well-Being Challenge for the whole school. We wanted to show that small habits can make a big difference to how people feel.

First, we researched simple changes: drinking more water, walking for ten minutes after lessons, and going to bed at the same time every night. If more students tried these habits, they would probably feel less tired and stressed.

We also learned about mindfulness — taking a few quiet minutes to breathe and relax. One student said, "I wish I had discovered mindfulness earlier — it really helps me cope with stress before exams."

For the future, our group decided to set a goal: to build one new healthy habit every month. We know it takes time, but a sustainable lifestyle doesn't happen overnight.

In the end, we designed a short show about health and well-being, with characters like a doctor, a nurse and a patient, to present our ideas to the school in a fun and memorable way.

Find Someone Who… (Well-Being Habits)

Walk around the class and ask your classmates questions with the second conditional, wish and if only to find someone who matches each sentence. Write their name next to the sentence.

Useful language

If you had more free time, what would you do?Do you ever wish you slept more?If only I had…I would feel better if I…

Adımlar

  1. Look at your worksheet: 6 sentences about well-being (e.g. '…wishes they exercised more').
  2. Stand up and ask a classmate a question to check if the sentence is true for them.
  3. If it's true, write their name. If not, ask another classmate.
  4. Try to find a different name for each sentence.
  5. Sit down and share two interesting answers with the class.

Doctor and Patient Role-Play

With a partner, act out three short situations at a health room: one of you is a patient describing symptoms, and the other is a doctor or nurse giving advice.

Useful language

I have a…If you rested more, you would feel better.I wish I hadn't…You should…

Adımlar

  1. Read the three situation cards (a headache, a stomachache, feeling exhausted).
  2. Decide who is the Patient and who is the Doctor/Nurse.
  3. Act out each situation: describe the symptom and give advice using the second conditional or wish.
  4. Swap roles and do it again with new situations.
  5. Perform your favourite situation for another pair.
