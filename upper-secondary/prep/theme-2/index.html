C Your turn

Describe your own classroom. Say what there is, what there isn't, and ask one question with Are there…?

There is …There are …There isn't …Are there …?
Rule

We use There is/There are to say that something exists in a place. Use there is with singular nouns and there are with plural nouns. Negative: there isn't/aren't; question: Is there…?/Are there…?

TRThere is/are bir yerde bir şeyin var olduğunu anlatmak için kullanılır. Tekil isimlerle there is, çoğul isimlerle there are kullanılır. Olumsuzda isn't/aren't, soruda Is there/Are there kullanılır.

When?

We use it to describe a classroom and the things and numbers of things in it.

TRSınıfı, sınıftaki eşyaları ve sayılarını anlatırken kullanılır.

  • I can't see the whiteboard from the back row.

Which modal shows ability or permission? Which shows a rule that is necessary? Which shows something forbidden?

cancan'tmustmustn't

Well done! You found the rule yourself. Now let's use it.

B Build sentences

Use the cues to write classroom sentences with can, can't, must or mustn't. Look at the example first.

  1. you ask a question if you don't understand

    ✓ You can ask a question if you don't understand.

  2. we listen when the teacher is speaking
    Model answer

    We must listen when the teacher is speaking.

  3. you not / use your phone during the lesson
    Model answer

    You mustn't use your phone during the lesson.

  4. I not / see the whiteboard from the back row
    Model answer

    I can't see the whiteboard from the back row.

  5. students work in pairs for this activity
    Model answer

    Students can work in pairs for this activity.

  6. we not / talk loudly during a test
    Model answer

    We mustn't talk loudly during a test.

C Your turn

Write three sentences about your own classroom: one with can (ability/permission), one with must (a rule), and one with mustn't (forbidden).

In our classroom, I can …We must …We mustn't …
Rule

Can shows ability or permission (negative can't). Must shows a rule is necessary; mustn't shows something is forbidden. After can/can't and must/mustn't we use the base form of the verb.

TRCan yetenek ya da izni anlatır (olumsuzu can't). Must bir kuralın zorunlu olduğunu, mustn't ise bir şeyin yasak olduğunu anlatır. Can/can't ve must/mustn't'tan sonra fiilin yalın hâli kullanılır.

When?

We use these to talk about classroom rules, ability, and what is allowed or forbidden.

TRSınıf kurallarını, yeteneği ve izin/yasakları anlatırken kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • You can ask a question.
  • We must listen carefully.

Negative

  • I can't see the board.
  • You mustn't use your phone.

Question

  • Can I ask a question?
  • Must we finish this today?

Short answers

  • Yes, you can. / No, you can't.
  • Yes, we must. / No, we don't have to.
Mistakes
  • You mustn't to use your phone.You mustn't use your phone.

    Don't use 'to' after mustn't.

    TRMustn't'tan sonra 'to' gelmez.

  • I can't to see the board.I can't see the board.

    Don't use 'to' after can't; use the base verb.

    TRCan't'tan sonra 'to' gelmez, yalın fiil kullanılır.

Compare

Can't (bir şeyi yapamamak, görememek gibi bir yetenek/imkânsızlık) ile mustn't (yasak, yapmaman gerekiyor) karıştırılmamalı: 'I can't see the board' = görmüyorum, imkânsız. 'You mustn't talk' = konuşman yasak, kural.

Our Classroom Rules

My name is Elif and I'm in Year 10. Every morning, our teacher checks the timetable and tells us what we're going to study. There is a big whiteboard at the front of our classroom, and our teacher writes the plan for the lesson on it.

In our classroom, we must listen carefully to the teacher's instructions. If we don't understand something, we can ask a question — we just have to raise our hand first. We mustn't interrupt when someone else is speaking.

There are five rows of desks in our classroom, and there is a classroom monitor who helps the teacher every week. This week, it's my turn to be the monitor, so I hand out the worksheets and collect the homework.

For most activities, we work with a partner or in small groups. We have to cooperate and take turns, because everyone must share ideas and materials. Sometimes the teacher says 'Shall we work in pairs today?' and everyone gets excited.

I like our classroom because the rules are clear and fair. Don't run, don't shout, and always listen — these are simple instructions, but they help everyone learn together.

Planning Our Ideal Classroom

For our project this term, our class had to design an ideal classroom with ideal activities. First, we had a class discussion: 'Shall we choose our own roles, or shall the teacher choose them?' In the end, we voted, and everyone chose a role.

In our ideal classroom, there is a clear timetable on the wall, and there are enough chairs and books for every student. There isn't a lot of noise, because everyone respects the classroom rules.

Every student has a responsibility. There is a classroom monitor who checks the whiteboard is clean, a materials manager who gives out worksheets, and a timekeeper who watches the clock during group activities.

We must cooperate and take turns in every activity. Students mustn't interrupt each other, and everyone can ask for help without feeling embarrassed. We also revise new vocabulary together at the end of each lesson.

For the writing task, each group wrote a short script about their ideal roles and routines. Next, we're going to act out these roles in class and present our ideal classroom to the other students.
