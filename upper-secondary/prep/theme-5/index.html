In Theme 5, you will explore Life in the Neighbourhood and City: the attractions and recreational places we visit, the services that help us every day, and the ways we get around town. You will learn to talk about past visits with the Simple Past, compare places with comparative adjectives, link two past events with when, check information with question tags, talk about activities with gerunds and infinitives, and describe locations with prepositions of place and time.

Konular

  • Attractions and recreational places
  • Services in the neighbourhood and city
  • Transportation in the neighbourhood and city

Dil yapıları

  • Simple Past (was/were; regular and irregular verbs)
  • Comparative adjectives
  • Conjunction: when
  • Question tags (Simple Past)
  • Gerunds and infinitives
  • Prepositions of place and time; prepositional phrases

Anlayabileceğim

  • A text about visiting attractions and recreational places in a city
  • A text about a morning of using local services with family
  • A conversation asking for directions to a museum
  • A conversation comparing two ways of travelling around the city

Söyleyebileceğim

  • Talk about attractions and services in your own neighbourhood
  • Ask for and give directions using prepositions of place
  • Compare places and types of transport in your city
  • Talk about a real or imagined visit using the Simple Past

Yazabileceğim

  • A project report about attractions, services and transport in your city

C Your turn

Write about a real or imagined visit to an attraction or service in your neighbourhood. Say where you went, what it was like, and one thing you did there.

Last weekend, I went to...It was...I ... (irregular verb) ...There was / were...
Rule

We use the Simple Past for completed actions and states in the past. Regular verbs add -ed (visited, walked); irregular verbs have their own past form (go→went, have→had). The past of 'be' is was/were.

TRSimple Past; geçmişte tamamlanmış olayları ve durumları anlatmak için kullanılır. Düzenli fiillere -ed eklenir (visited, walked); düzensiz fiillerin ise kendine özgü bir geçmiş zaman biçimi vardır (go→went, have→had). Be fiilinin geçmiş hâli was/were'dir.

When?

We use it to talk about places we visited in the past, what we did there, and what the situation was like (weather, crowds, etc.).

TRGeçmişte ziyaret ettiğimiz yerleri, yaptığımız şeyleri ve o anki durumu (hava, kalabalık vb.) anlatırken kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • We visited the city museum last month.
  • The park was quiet yesterday.

Negative

  • The park wasn't crowded yesterday.
  • We didn't take the bus.

Question

  • Did you enjoy the museum tour?
  • Was the mall busy on Saturday?

Short answers

  • Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.
  • Yes, it was. / No, it wasn't.
Mistakes
  • We visited the museum and have a great time.We visited the museum and had a great time.

    The irregular past of 'have' is 'had', not 'have'.

    TR'have' fiilinin düzensiz geçmiş hâli 'had'dir.

  • There was many people at the market.There were many people at the market.

    Use 'were' with a plural subject like 'many people'.

    TRÇoğul özneyle 'were' kullanılır.

C Your turn

Compare two places in your own neighbourhood or city (for example, two parks, two shops, or two ways of travelling).

... is bigger than ...... is more ... than ...Is ... busier than ...?
Rule

For short (one-syllable) adjectives, we usually add -er (big→bigger). For adjectives ending in -y, we change y to i and add -er (busy→busier). For longer adjectives, we use more + adjective (relaxing→more relaxing). We use 'than' between the two things being compared.

TRSıfatları karşılaştırırken kısa sıfatlara (tek heceli) genellikle -er eklenir (big→bigger); -y ile bitenlerde y, i'ye döner ve -er eklenir (busy→busier). İki heceden uzun sıfatlarda more + sıfat kullanılır (relaxing→more relaxing). Karşılaştırılan iki şey arasında 'than' kullanılır.

When?

We use this to compare two places, services, or types of transport in our city — which is bigger, faster, more relaxing, etc.

TRŞehrimizdeki iki yeri, hizmeti ya da ulaşım şeklini karşılaştırırken kullanılır: hangisi daha büyük, daha hızlı, daha rahat vb.

  • The park is more relaxing than the mall.

Negative

  • The old museum wasn't more interesting than the new one.

  • Is the community centre busier than the library?

Short answers

  • Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.
Mistakes
  • The museum is more bigger than the library.The museum is bigger than the library.

    Don't use both -er and 'more' with a short adjective.

    TRKısa sıfatlarda hem -er hem 'more' birlikte kullanılmaz.

  • This park is relaxinger than that one.This park is more relaxing than that one.

    Don't add -er to longer adjectives; use 'more' instead.

    TRUzun sıfatlara -er eklenmez, 'more' kullanılır.

What does 'when' connect — two nouns or two actions/events? Can 'when' come at the start or in the middle of the sentence?

C Your turn

Write two sentences with 'when' about something that happened while you were visiting a place in your neighbourhood or city.

When I went to..., ...... when I was at...
Rule

'When' links two past events, usually showing that one happened while or as the other did. 'When' can start the sentence or come in the middle; if it starts the sentence, we use a comma before the main clause.

TR'When' iki geçmiş olayı birbirine bağlar; genellikle biri diğeri olurken gerçekleşir. 'When' cümlenin başında ya da ortasında yer alabilir; başta ise, ana cümleden önce virgül kullanılır.

When?

We use it to say that something else was happening or happened at the same time as a visit to a place or use of a service.

TRGeçmişte bir yeri ziyaret ederken ya da bir hizmetten yararlanırken aynı anda başka bir şeyin olduğunu anlatırken kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • When I visited the museum, it was raining.
  • The bus was full when we arrived.

Negative

  • When we got there, the shop wasn't open yet.

Question

  • What did you do when you arrived at the park?

Short answers

  • When did it happen? — When I arrived at the stop.
Mistakes
  • When I am arrive at the park, it started raining.When I arrived at the park, it started raining.

    Both verbs should be in the Simple Past when 'when' links two past events.

    TR'When' geçmiş olayları bağlarken iki fiil de Simple Past olmalıdır.

  • I saw my neighbour, when I was waiting at the bus stop she said hello.I saw my neighbour when I was waiting at the bus stop.

    No comma is needed when the 'when' clause comes after the main clause.

    TR'When' cümle ortasındaysa virgüle gerek yoktur.

Rule

A question tag depends on the main sentence: if the main sentence is positive, the tag is negative, and if it is negative, the tag is positive. In the Simple Past, we usually use did/didn't or was/were(n't).

TRQuestion tag, ana cümlenin türüne göre değişir: ana cümle olumluysa etiket olumsuz, ana cümle olumsuzsa etiket olumludur. Simple Past'te genellikle did/didn't ya da was/weren't kullanılır.

When?

We use it to check information or keep a conversation going when talking about something that happened in the past.

TRBir bilgiyi doğrulamak ya da bir konuşmayı sürdürmek için geçmişte olan bir şeyi tekrar sorarken kullanılır.

Write two sentences about activities you enjoy doing or plan to do in your neighbourhood or city, using a gerund and an infinitive.

Rule

Some verbs are followed by the -ing form (gerund): enjoy, love, like, mind, finish. Other verbs are followed by to + verb (infinitive): want, decide, plan, hope, need.

TRBazı fiillerden sonra -ing (gerund) hâli gelir: enjoy, love, like, mind, finish. Bazı fiillerden sonra ise 'to' + fiil (infinitive) hâli gelir: want, decide, plan, hope, need.

When?

We use this to talk about activities we enjoy doing, or things we plan or want to do in our city.

TRŞehrimizde yapmaktan hoşlandığımız etkinlikleri ya da yapmayı planladığımız şeyleri anlatırken kullanılır.

  • We decided to take the bus.

  • We didn't want to walk in the rain.

  • Did they decide to take the underground?

Mistakes
  • I enjoy to visit new parks.I enjoy visiting new parks.

    'Enjoy' is followed by the -ing form, not 'to'.

    TR'Enjoy' fiilinden sonra -ing hâli gelir, 'to' değil.

    TR'Decide' fiilinden sonra 'to' + fiil gelir.

Compare

Enjoy/love/like/mind gibi fiiller -ing alır; want/decide/plan/hope gibi fiiller 'to' + fiil alır. İkisini karıştırmamak gerekir.

C Your turn

Describe where three places are in your own neighbourhood, using at least three different prepositions of place.

Rule

Prepositions of place describe where something is: between, opposite, next to, near. Prepositions of time describe when something happens: at (a specific time), on (a day or street name), in (a month, season, or city).

TRYer belirten edatlar bir yerin nerede olduğunu anlatır: between (arasında), opposite (karşısında), next to (yanında), near (yakınında). Zaman belirten edatlar ise bir şeyin ne zaman olduğunu anlatır: at (belirli saat), on (gün/cadde adı), in (ay/mevsim/şehir).

When?

We use these to describe the location of places in our city and to say when a meeting or event happens.

TRŞehrimizdeki yerlerin konumunu tarif ederken ve bir buluşma ya da olayın zamanını söylerken kullanılır.

  • The library is in Elm Street.The library is on Elm Street.

    Use 'on' with street names, not 'in'.

    TRCadde adlarında 'on' kullanılır, 'in' değil.

  • We met in the bus stop at 9 o'clock.We met at the bus stop at 9 o'clock.

    Use 'at' for a specific point like a bus stop.

    TRBelirli bir nokta (durak) için 'at' kullanılır.

Compare

'Next to' ve 'near' benzer görünse de 'next to' bitişik konumu, 'near' ise genel bir yakınlığı anlatır: 'next to the supermarket' (hemen yanında) ile 'near the hospital' (hastaneye yakın bir yerde) karıştırılmamalıdır.

  4. After: Konuşma sorularını düşün ya da yaz.

1 Before reading

  • Look at the title. What attractions do you think the writer visited?
  • Guess: which place do you think the writer liked best — a park, a museum, or a mall?

A Great Day in the City Centre

My name is Aslı, and last Saturday I visited the city centre with my family. We wanted to see some of the most popular attractions and recreational places in our city.

First, we went to Riverside Park, a large green area next to the river. It was much quieter than the busy streets near our house, and there was a lovely playground for my little brother. We sat on the grass and had a picnic before we walked around the lake.

After the park, we visited the City History Museum. It was more interesting than I expected! There were old photos of our city from a hundred years ago, and a friendly guide explained how the neighbourhood changed over time. My favourite part was the model of the old market square.

In the afternoon, we went to Central Mall, the biggest shopping centre in our city. It was more crowded than the museum, but it had a cinema, a food court, and dozens of shops. We watched a film and ate dinner there before we went home.

It was a great day. The park was more relaxing than the mall, but the museum taught me the most about my own city. Next time, I want to visit the old castle on the hill — I've heard it's even more beautiful than the museum.

A Morning of Errands

On Saturday morning, Mr Kaya had a lot of errands to do in his neighbourhood. His daughter, Elif, decided to go with him and help.

Their first stop was the post office. Elif enjoyed posting a parcel to her cousin, and the clerk was very helpful. Next, they walked to the pharmacy to buy some medicine for Elif's grandmother. The pharmacy was smaller than the supermarket next door, but the pharmacist knew Elif's family well.

After that, Mr Kaya remembered that the library book was due back that day. They hurried to the library, which was busier than usual because of a children's story time. Elif loved choosing three new books before they left.

Their last stop was the community centre, where Elif's mother works as a volunteer. The centre offers free English classes, a food bank, and after-school clubs for children. Mr Kaya and Elif helped set up chairs for an evening meeting before they walked home.

By midday, Elif understood why her father always says that good services make a neighbourhood a better place to live. She started thinking about which services she wanted to include in her class project about their city.

Find Someone Who Visited These Places

Walk around the class and ask your classmates questions with the Simple Past (Did you ever go to...? / Did you visit...?) to find someone who matches each sentence. Write their name next to the sentence.

Useful language

Did you ever go to the new shopping mall?Did you visit a museum last year?Was it more interesting than you expected?Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.

Adımlar

  1. Look at your worksheet: 6 sentences about places and services in a city (e.g. '...visited a museum last year').
  2. Stand up and ask a classmate a question to check if the sentence is true for them.
  3. If it's true, write their name. If not, ask another classmate.
  4. Try to find a different name for each sentence.
  5. Sit down and share two interesting answers with the class.

Excuse Me — Asking for Directions

With a partner, act out three short situations in the city where one of you is a visitor asking for directions and the other gives directions using prepositions of place.
