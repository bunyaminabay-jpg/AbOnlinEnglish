In Theme 7, you will explore Life in Nature: the daily activities people do outdoors, the extreme weather conditions that can surprise us, and exciting adventures in nature. You will learn to describe what was happening with the Past Progressive, compare it with the Simple Past, talk about past abilities with could, and give advice with should.

Past Progressive: actions in progress in nature

1 · Notice
  • At 7 a.m., we were hiking along the trail.
  • While the sun was rising, the birds started singing.
  • It wasn't raining when we set up camp.
  • Were you camping near the river last weekend?

  2. the birds sing when the sun rose

C Your turn

Write about an outdoor activity you remember. Say what you were doing at a certain time, and one thing you weren't doing.

At … o'clock, I was …We were …I wasn't …It was/wasn't …
Rule

We use the Past Progressive (was/were + verb-ing) to talk about an action that was in progress at a specific moment in the past. It shows what was happening 'at that time'.

TRPast Progressive (was/were + fiil-ing), geçmişte belirli bir anda devam eden bir eylemi anlatmak için kullanılır. Genellikle 'o sırada' ya da 'o anda' ne olduğunu göstermek için kullanılır.

When?

We use it to describe what was happening in nature at a certain moment, for example what we were doing when the storm started.

TRDoğada belirli bir anda ne yaptığımızı anlatırken (örneğin, fırtına başladığında ne yapıyorduk) kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • We were hiking along the trail.
  • The birds were singing.

Negative

  • It wasn't raining.
  • We weren't sleeping.

Question

  • Were you camping near the river?
  • Was it raining when you left?

Short answers

  • Yes, we were. / No, we weren't.
  • Yes, it was. / No, it wasn't.
Mistakes
  • We were hike along the trail.We were hiking along the trail.

    Always add -ing to the verb in the Past Progressive.

    TRPast Progressive'de fiile mutlaka -ing eklenir.

  • She was sleep when the storm started.She was sleeping when the storm started.

    Use the -ing form after was/were, not the base verb.

    TRwas/were'den sonra fiilin -ing hali gelir, yalın hali değil.

Past Progressive vs Simple Past: interrupted actions

1 · Notice
  • We were walking along the trail when we saw a deer.
  • The rain started while we were setting up the tent.
  • I was reading my compass when my friend called me.
  • Lightning struck a tree while we were hiding under the rocks.

Which action was already in progress (longer)? Which action interrupted it (shorter, sudden)?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Look at the Notice sentences again. Complete the rule with the words in the box.

Past ProgressiveSimple Pastwhenwhile

Well done! You found the rule yourself. Now let's use it.

B Build sentences

Combine the two actions into one sentence with when or while. Look at the example first.

  1. we / walk along the trail we / see a deer

    ✓ We were walking along the trail when we saw a deer.

  2. the rain / start we / set up the tent
    Model answer

    The rain started while we were setting up the tent.

  3. I / read my compass my friend / call me
    Model answer

    I was reading my compass when my friend called me.

  4. lightning / strike a tree we / hide under the rocks
    Model answer

    Lightning struck a tree while we were hiding under the rocks.

  5. we / cross the river the storm / begin
    Model answer

    We were crossing the river when the storm began.

C Your turn

Write two sentences about something that happened while you were doing an outdoor activity. Use when or while.

I was … when …While we were …, …
Rule

When one action was in progress and a second, shorter action interrupted it, we use the Past Progressive for the longer action and the Simple Past for the shorter one. 'When' usually goes before the shorter action, and 'while' before the longer one.

TRBir eylem geçmişte devam ederken başka bir eylem onu kesintiye uğratırsa, uzun süren eylem için Past Progressive, kısa/ani eylem için Simple Past kullanılır. 'When' genellikle kısa eylemden önce, 'while' ise uzun eylemden önce gelir.

When?

We use it to say that something suddenly happened while we were doing an outdoor activity, for example seeing a deer while we were hiking.

TRDoğada bir aktivite sürerken aniden başka bir şeyin olduğunu anlatırken (örneğin yürüyüş yaparken bir geyik gördük) kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • We were walking when we saw a deer.
  • The storm started while we were camping.

Negative

  • We weren't sleeping when the storm started.
  • It wasn't raining when we left.

Question

  • What were you doing when the storm started?
  • Were you hiking when it began to rain?

Short answers

  • I was reading a map.
  • Yes, we were.
Mistakes
  • We walked along the trail when we were seeing a deer.We were walking along the trail when we saw a deer.

    The longer action needs the Past Progressive; the short, sudden action needs the Simple Past.

    TRUzun eylem Past Progressive, kısa/ani eylem Simple Past olmalı.

  • While we were setting up the tent, the rain was starting.While we were setting up the tent, the rain started.

    Use the Simple Past for the sudden, short action, not the Past Progressive.

    TRAni, kısa eylem için Simple Past kullanılır, Past Progressive değil.

Compare

Simple Past tek başına tamamlanmış, sıralı olayları anlatır (First we hiked, then we ate). Past Progressive + Simple Past birlikte kullanıldığında ise bir eylemin diğerini kesintiye uğrattığını gösterir (We were hiking when it started to rain).

C Your turn

Write two sentences about something you could or couldn't do outdoors when you were younger.

When I was younger, I could …I couldn't …
Rule

Could is used to talk about a general ability in the past (something we were able to do, for example as a child). The negative is couldn't, and the question is Could + subject + base verb.

TRCould, geçmişte genel bir yeteneği anlatmak için kullanılır (örneğin çocukken yapabildiğimiz şeyler). Olumsuzu couldn't, sorusu ise Could + özne + yalın fiil şeklindedir.

When?

We use it to talk about general abilities in nature in the past, like being able to climb, swim, or find the way.

TRDoğada geçmişte yapabildiğimiz ya da yapamadığımız genel şeyleri anlatırken kullanılır: tırmanabilmek, yüzebilmek, yön bulabilmek gibi.

3 · Form

Positive

  • My grandfather could climb mountains.
  • The guide could find the cave in the dark.

Negative

  • We couldn't see the trail.
  • I couldn't start a campfire.

Question

  • Could you swim across the river?
  • Could they find shelter in time?

Short answers

  • Yes, I could. / No, I couldn't.
Mistakes
  • We could to see the trail.We could see the trail.

    Don't use 'to' after could.

    TRCould'dan sonra 'to' gelmez.

  • She could swam very well as a child.She could swim very well as a child.

    Use the base verb after could, not the past form.

    TRCould'dan sonra fiilin yalın hali kullanılır.

Compare

Could genel bir yeteneği anlatır (I could swim when I was young). Belirli bir durumda tek seferlik bir başarıyı anlatmak için genellikle 'was/were able to' ya da 'managed to' tercih edilir (We were able to find shelter just in time).

  • You shouldn't hike alone in extreme weather.
  • Should we wear warm clothes for the trip?
  • If there's a storm, you should find shelter immediately.

How do we give advice or a recommendation? How do we say it's a bad idea?

shouldshouldn'tShouldadvice

Give advice for each situation using should or shouldn't. Look at the example first.

  1. you take a compass and a map on a hike

    ✓ You should take a compass and a map on a hike.

  2. you not / hike alone in extreme weather
    Model answer

    You shouldn't hike alone in extreme weather.

  3. ? / we wear warm clothes for the trip
    Model answer

    Should we wear warm clothes for the trip?

  4. hikers check the weather forecast before they leave

C Your turn

Write three pieces of advice for someone going on a nature adventure. Use should and shouldn't.

Before you go, you should …You shouldn't …
Rule

Should is used to give advice or a recommendation — it does not express obligation. The negative, shouldn't, shows that something is not a good idea.

TRShould, bir tavsiye ya da öneri vermek için kullanılır; zorunluluk anlatmaz. Olumsuzu shouldn't, bir şeyin iyi bir fikir olmadığını, yapılmamasının önerildiğini gösterir.

When?

We use it to give advice about nature trips and dangerous weather: what people should and shouldn't do.

TRDoğa gezileri ve tehlikeli hava koşulları için tavsiye verirken kullanılır: ne yapmalı, ne yapmamalı.

3 · Form

Positive

  • You should take a compass.
  • Hikers should check the forecast.

Negative

  • You shouldn't hike alone.
  • You shouldn't swim during a storm.

Question

  • Should we wear warm clothes?
  • Should I take extra water?

Short answers

  • Yes, you should. / No, you shouldn't.
Mistakes
  • You should to take a compass.You should take a compass.

    Don't use 'to' after should.

    TRShould'dan sonra 'to' gelmez.

  • You should hiking alone in a storm.You shouldn't hike alone in a storm.

    Negative advice uses shouldn't, and the verb stays in its base form.

    TRTavsiye olumsuzu shouldn't ile yapılır ve fiil yalın haldedir.

Compare

Should tavsiye verir (bir fikir, zorunlu değil). Must/have to ise zorunluluk anlatır. 'You should take water' = tavsiye; 'You must wear a life jacket' = kural/zorunluluk.

A Weekend Camping Trip

Last month, my family and I went camping near Lake Abant. We packed our backpacks and drove to the campsite early on Saturday morning. When we arrived, we set up camp near a small forest, close to a clear river.

At sunrise on the first day, we were already awake and ready for a hike. We followed a long trail through the trees and saw a lot of wildlife, including deer and colourful birds. My little sister could name almost every bird we saw!

In the afternoon, we made a campfire and cooked simple food. While we were cooking, my dad told stories about his own camping trips when he was young. He said he could survive for days with just a small backpack and a compass.

At night, the sky was full of stars, and we couldn't hear any city noise at all — only the wind in the trees. We were sitting around the campfire when we suddenly heard an owl.

The next morning, before we left, we checked our tent for any damage and cleaned up the campsite. It was a simple weekend, but it taught me how much I love spending time in nature.

Caught in a Storm on Uludağ

Two years ago, Kaan and his cousin Derin went on a hiking adventure on Uludağ. They were walking along a narrow trail near a cliff when the weather suddenly changed.

Dark clouds appeared quickly, and soon they heard thunder in the distance. 'We should find shelter now,' said Kaan, looking at the sky nervously. They couldn't see the trail clearly because of the heavy rain.

While they were looking for shelter, they found a small cave near a waterfall. They quickly went inside and waited for the storm to pass. Lightning struck a tree close to the cave, and they were both frightened.

Kaan remembered his father's advice: 'You should always carry a compass and extra water on a hike, and you shouldn't panic in a storm.' He used his compass to check their direction while they waited.

After almost an hour, the storm finally stopped. They were cold and wet, but they were safe. They walked back to the campsite carefully, using the compass to find the right path.

Kaan says this adventure taught him an important lesson: nature can be beautiful, but you should always be prepared for extreme weather.

Weather Warning! — Advice Role-Play

With a partner, act out two short situations: one hiker describes a sudden weather problem (storm, flood, or extreme heat), and the other gives advice using should/shouldn't.

Useful language

What should I do?You should… / You shouldn't…I was hiking when…We couldn't… because…

Adımlar

  1. Read the two situation cards (a sudden storm on a mountain; a flood near a campsite).
  2. Decide who is Hiker A (describes the problem) and who is Friend B (gives advice).
  3. Act out each situation using Past Progressive to describe what was happening and should/shouldn't for advice.
  4. Swap roles and do it again with new situations.
  5. Perform your favourite situation for another pair.
DestekProvide sentence starters on cards for lower-level students.
Hedef2 role-play situations per pair.
İleriStudents invent one extra weather situation of their own to role-play.

My Nature Journal Entry

Write a journal entry about a day of daily activities in nature (real or imagined). Say what you were doing at different times, and use at least two grammar points from this theme.

Plan

  1. Choose a place and a day (real or imaginary).
  2. List 4-5 things you were doing at different times of the day.
  3. Add one sentence about something unexpected that happened (use Past Progressive + Simple Past with when/while).
  4. Check your grammar and punctuation.

A Nature Adventure Script

Write a short scene (a dialogue with 6-8 lines) for the class performance assignment: a Nature Adventures project about daily activities, extreme weather, and adventures in nature. Include two characters (for example, two hikers, or a hiker and a guide) and use at least two of this theme's grammar points.

Plan

  1. Choose your two characters and the outdoor activity or adventure (hiking, camping, caving, canoeing).
  2. Decide what problem happens in the scene (e.g. a sudden storm, getting lost, needing advice).
  3. Write 6-8 lines of dialogue using Past Progressive, could, and/or should.
  4. Read it aloud with a partner and time it — it should take about 30 seconds.
