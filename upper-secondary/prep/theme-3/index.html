Rule

When?

We use it to talk about what someone looks like, their character, and their habits.

TRBir kişinin nasıl göründüğünü, karakterini ve alışkanlıklarını anlatırken kullanırız.

Simple Present vs Present Progressive

1 · Notice
  • Elif usually wears jeans, but today she is wearing a dress for the party.
  • My brother has short hair, but this month he is growing a beard.
  • I am trying on new shoes right now, but I usually buy comfortable trainers.
  • We don't usually wear uniform on Fridays, but today we are wearing it for a photo.

Which sentences describe habits and general facts? Which describe what's happening right now?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Look at the Notice sentences again. Complete the rule with the words in the box.

Simple PresentPresent Progressivehabitsnow

Well done! You found the rule yourself. Now let's use it.

B Build sentences

Choose the correct tense (Simple Present or Present Progressive) for each situation. Look at the example first.

  1. Deniz usually / wear trainers

    ✓ Deniz usually wears trainers.

  2. right now / Deniz wear smart shoes for the interview
    Model answer

    Right now, Deniz is wearing smart shoes for the interview.

  3. my mum usually / not like stripy clothes
    Model answer

    My mum usually doesn't like stripy clothes.

  4. at the moment / my mum try on a spotted dress
    Model answer

    At the moment, my mum is trying on a spotted dress.

  5. ? / you / usually get dressed quickly
    Model answer

    Do you usually get dressed quickly?

C Your turn

Write two sentences about yourself: one with the Simple Present about your usual style, and one with the Present Progressive about what you are wearing today.

I usually wear …Today, I am wearing …
Rule

The Simple Present describes habits, general facts and things that happen regularly (with usually, always, every day). The Present Progressive describes actions happening right now, at the moment of speaking (with now, at the moment, right now, look!).

TRSimple Present alışkanlıkları, genel gerçekleri ve düzenli olan şeyleri anlatır (usually, always, every day gibi zaman zarflarıyla). Present Progressive ise şu anda, konuşma anında olan eylemleri anlatır (now, at the moment, right now, look!).

When?

We use this contrast to separate someone's usual style or habits (Simple Present) from what they are wearing or doing right now (Present Progressive).

TRBir kişinin genel giyim tarzını/alışkanlığını (Simple Present) ile o anda ne giydiğini/yaptığını (Present Progressive) ayırt etmek için kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Deniz usually wears trainers. (habit)
  • Deniz is wearing smart shoes today. (now)

Negative

  • My mum doesn't usually wear stripy clothes.
  • She isn't wearing a coat right now.

Question

  • Do you usually get dressed quickly?
  • Is she trying on that dress now?

Short answers

  • Yes, I do.
  • Yes, she is.
Mistakes
  • Look! She wears a lovely dress.Look! She is wearing a lovely dress.

    'Look!' signals now, so use the Present Progressive.

    TR'Look!' şu anı gösterir, Present Progressive gerekir.

  • I am liking spotted clothes.I like spotted clothes.

    'Like' is a state verb — it doesn't usually take -ing.

    TR'Like' bir durum fiilidir (state verb), genellikle -ing almaz.

Compare

Simple Present (usually, every day) alışkanlık; Present Progressive (now, at the moment) şu an oluyor. 'Like', 'have' (sahiplik), 'want' gibi durum fiilleri genelde -ing almaz: 'I like it' doğru, 'I am liking it' yanlıştır.

C Your turn

Write three sentences using 's, a possessive pronoun, and Whose…? about clothes or things in your classroom.

That's ___'s …This … is mine / yours / …Whose … is this?
Rule

We add 's to a singular noun to show possession, and just ' to a plural noun ending in -s (the boys'). Possessive pronouns (mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs) replace a noun so we don't repeat it. We use Whose…? to ask who something belongs to.

TRBir şeyin kime ait olduğunu göstermek için tekil isimlere 's, çoğul -s ile biten isimlere ise sadece ' eklenir (the boys'). Possessive pronouns (mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs) isim tekrarını önler. Whose…? sorusu 'kimin' anlamına gelir ve sahiplik sorar.

When?

We use this to say or ask who an item of clothing or a belonging belongs to.

TRBir eşyanın (kıyafet, çanta vb.) kime ait olduğunu söylerken ya da sorarken kullanılır.

C Your turn

Write three sentences about a classmate with a question tag (about their appearance, clothes or personality), and check with a partner.

Rule

A question tag is a short question added to the end of a sentence, usually to check information. A positive sentence takes a negative tag, and a negative sentence takes a positive tag. The tag matches the auxiliary verb (do/does, am/is/are) and the subject.

TRQuestion tag, cümlenin sonuna eklenen ve genellikle bir şeyi doğrulamak için sorulan kısa bir sorudur. Olumlu cümleden sonra olumsuz tag, olumsuz cümleden sonra olumlu tag gelir. Tag, cümledeki yardımcı fiile (do/does, am/is/are) ve özneye göre değişir.

When?

We use question tags to check information or keep a conversation going, especially when talking about appearance, clothes and personality.

TRBir bilgiyi doğrulamak ya da sohbeti sürdürmek için, özellikle görünüş, kıyafet ve kişilik hakkında konuşurken kullanılır.

  • She isn't wearing a coat, isn't she?She isn't wearing a coat, is she?

    A negative sentence needs a positive tag.

    TROlumsuz cümleden sonra olumlu tag gelir.

My Best Friend, Zeynep

My name is Aslı and I want to tell you about my best friend, Zeynep. We are in the same class, and we get on well with each other. Zeynep is fourteen years old, and she has long curly brown hair and big brown eyes.

Zeynep isn't very tall, but she is quite slim and always looks stylish. She usually wears comfortable clothes, like jeans and a stripy jumper, but today she is wearing a spotted dress for a school photo.

As for her personality, Zeynep is very cheerful and generous. She always shares her lunch with me, and she never gets angry, even when things go wrong. She's also hard-working — she studies every evening after school.

Zeynep isn't shy at all. She loves talking to new people, and she gets on well with everyone in our class. The only thing is, she can be a little stubborn — when she decides something, she doesn't change her mind easily!

I'm really lucky to have a friend like Zeynep. She always makes me laugh, and I know I can trust her with anything.

How to Write a Great Fact File

A fact file is a short text that gives information about a person. At the end of Theme 3, you are going to interview a classmate and write a fact file about them, so let's look at a good example first.

This is Deniz's fact file, written by his classmate Mert: 'Deniz is fifteen years old. He is tall and has short straight hair and dark brown eyes. He usually wears casual clothes, like a hoodie and trainers, because he thinks they are comfortable.'

Mert continues: 'Deniz's personality is easy to describe — he is friendly, cheerful and very generous. He always helps his classmates with their homework, and he gets on well with everyone in our year. He isn't shy at all!'

Notice how Mert uses the Simple Present for facts (he is, he wears) and clear vocabulary for appearance and personality. He also organises the fact file into two parts: appearance first, then personality.

When you write your own fact file, remember to interview your classmate carefully, take notes, and use a mix of appearance and personality words. Don't forget to check your grammar before you present it to the class!

Guess Who?

Think of a classmate without saying their name. Answer your partner's yes/no questions about appearance and personality until they guess who it is.

Useful language

Does this person have …?Is this person tall / shy / cheerful?Yes, they do. / No, they don't.I think it's …!

Adımlar

  1. Silently choose a classmate in your mind — don't tell anyone yet.
  2. Your partner asks up to 8 yes/no questions about appearance and personality.
  3. Answer honestly with short answers (Yes, they do. / No, they aren't.).
  4. Your partner tries to guess the name.
  5. Swap roles and play again with a new person.

Practising the Interview
