This theme looks at personal life and well-being: talking about the body and common illnesses, thinking about healthy habits now and in the future, and describing fashion and personal style.

Konular

Rule

We use will for predictions and quick decisions, be going to for plans and intentions with evidence, the Present Progressive for fixed arrangements with a time and place, and the Simple Present for timetables and schedules.

TRTahminler ve anlık kararlar için will, kanıta dayalı planlar için be going to, belirli bir zaman ve yeri olan düzenlemeler için Present Progressive, tarife/programlara bağlı olaylar için Simple Present kullanılır.

When?

Use the form that matches how certain or fixed the future event is: opinion, plan, arrangement, or timetable.

TRGelecekteki olayın ne kadar kesin veya planlı olduğuna göre uygun zaman formu seçilir: görüş, plan, düzenleme veya tarife.

Rule

Can/could express ability (can = present, could = past). Should/shouldn't give advice or recommend the right thing to do.

TRCan/could yetenek belirtir (can = şimdiki zaman, could = geçmiş zaman). Should/shouldn't ise tavsiye vermek veya doğru olanı önermek için kullanılır.

When?

Use can/could when talking about physical or mental ability; use should/shouldn't when giving advice about health, habits, or behaviour.

TRFiziksel veya zihinsel yetenekten bahsederken can/could, sağlık, alışkanlık veya davranış hakkında tavsiye verirken should/shouldn't kullanılır.

Write 2-3 original sentences comparing two outfits, habits, or people using comparatives, as...as, or like/alike, and one sentence with an -ing or -ed adjective.

Rule

Comparative adjectives (+ than) compare two different things. As...as and same...as show two things are equal; like and alike show similarity. -ing adjectives describe what causes a feeling; -ed adjectives describe the person who feels it.

TRKarşılaştırmalı sıfatlar (+ than) iki farklı şeyi kıyaslar. As...as ve same...as iki şeyin eşit olduğunu; like ve alike ise benzerliği gösterir. -ing sıfatları bir duyguya neden olan şeyi, -ed sıfatları ise o duyguyu hisseden kişiyi tanımlar.

When?

Use these structures when comparing habits, clothes, or people, and when describing how something makes you feel.

TRAlışkanlıkları, kıyafetleri veya kişileri karşılaştırırken ve bir şeyin sizi nasıl hissettirdiğini anlatırken bu yapılar kullanılır.

Small Habits, Big Difference

Many teenagers believe that being healthy means going to the gym every day or following a strict diet. In fact, doctors say that small daily habits often make the biggest difference to how we feel. Simple things like drinking enough water, sleeping at regular times, and taking short breaks from screens can improve both our body and our mind.

Sleep is one of the most important habits for teenagers. Experts recommend eight to nine hours a night, but many students go to bed late because of homework or phones. As a result, they feel exhausted the next day and find it harder to concentrate in lessons. A simple change, like putting phones away thirty minutes before bed, can make sleep much better.

Movement matters too, but it doesn't have to mean intense exercise. A short walk after school, stretching in the morning, or even dancing to your favourite song can lift your mood and give you more energy. Doctors say that these small activities are often easier to keep up than a strict gym plan, because they fit naturally into a busy student's day.

Finally, well-being isn't only physical. Talking to friends and family, taking breaks when you feel stressed, and doing something you enjoy every day all help your mental health. In the future, more schools will probably teach students about these small habits, because a healthy routine now can shape a healthier life later.

Fashion: More Than Just Clothes

For many teenagers, fashion is not just about following trends — it's a way to show who they are. Some students prefer casual clothes like jeans and trainers because they feel more comfortable and confident in them. Others enjoy trying new styles and mixing colours, patterns, and accessories to create a look that feels unique.

Personal style can also change with mood and occasion. A student might wear relaxed, casual clothes for a normal school day but choose something more formal for a special event, like a presentation or a family celebration. Many young people say that what they wear can affect how they feel — a comfortable, well-matched outfit often makes them feel more relaxed and self-assured.

Fashion trends are also becoming more thoughtful. More brands are now making clothes that are as comfortable as they are stylish, and many young shoppers say they prefer simple, practical designs over expensive, uncomfortable ones. Social media has made it easier to discover new styles, but it has also encouraged some teenagers to buy too many clothes that they rarely wear.

In the future, experts predict that fashion will focus more on comfort, individuality, and responsible choices. Instead of buying lots of cheap clothes, more people will probably choose fewer items that they really love and that match their own personal taste. For today's teenagers, fashion is becoming less about copying others and more about expressing themselves.

  3. Student B asks a follow-up question and gives advice using should/shouldn't.
  4. Switch roles and repeat with a different illness.
  5. Perform your favourite version for another pair.
