This theme explores planets, space exploration, and the possibility of life beyond Earth, while introducing zero and first conditional structures to talk about scientific facts and future possibilities.

Konular

  • Planets and space
  • Life in the universe
  • Space explorations

Dil yapıları

  • Zero conditional
  • First conditional

  • I can understand predictions about the future of space travel.

Use the cues to build zero conditional sentences about space.

  4. if / a planet / be / too far from the Sun / it / be / very cold

  5. astronauts / feel / weightless / if / they / be / in space
    Model answer

    Astronauts feel weightless if they are in space.

  6. if / a satellite / lose / power / it / stop / sending signals
    Model answer

    If a satellite loses power, it stops sending signals.

  7. a comet / glow / if / it / get / close to the Sun
    Model answer

    A comet glows if it gets close to the Sun.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original zero conditional sentences about facts related to space or the universe.

If a planet ...Stars ... if ...If there is no oxygen, ...
Rule

Zero conditional describes general truths, scientific facts, and things that are always true. Both clauses use the present simple: If + present simple, ... present simple.

TRSıfır koşul cümleleri genel gerçekleri, bilimsel olguları ve her zaman doğru olan durumları anlatmak için kullanılır. Her iki cümlecikte de geniş zaman (present simple) kullanılır: If + geniş zaman, ... geniş zaman.

When?

We use zero conditional for facts, natural laws, and habitual results — not for imagined future situations.

TRSıfır koşul; gerçekler, doğa kanunları ve alışılmış sonuçlar için kullanılır; hayali gelecek durumlar için kullanılmaz.

3 · Form

Positive

  • If + subject + present simple, subject + present simple.
  • Example: If you heat ice, it melts.

Negative

  • If + subject + do/does not + verb, subject + do/does not + verb.
  • Example: If a planet does not have water, it does not support life.

Question

  • Does it ... if ...? / What happens if ...?
  • Example: What happens if a rocket runs out of fuel?

Short answers

  • Yes, it does. / No, it does not.
Mistakes
  • If you will heat water, it boils.If you heat water, it boils.

    Do not use 'will' in the if-clause of a zero conditional.

    TRSıfır koşulda 'if' cümleciğinde 'will' kullanılmaz.

  • If a star runs out of fuel, it will explode.If a star runs out of fuel, it explodes.

    Both clauses need the present simple, not 'will', for a general fact.

    TRGenel bir gerçek için her iki cümlecikte de geniş zaman kullanılmalı, 'will' değil.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original first conditional sentences making predictions about the future of space exploration.

If scientists ...If humans travel to Mars, ...If we do not ...
Rule

First conditional describes a real possibility in the future. It uses: If + present simple, ... will + base verb.

TRBirinci koşul cümleleri gelecekte gerçekleşmesi mümkün olan durumları anlatır. Yapısı: If + geniş zaman, ... will + fiil.

When?

We use first conditional to talk about likely future results of a possible condition, such as predictions about space travel.

TRBirinci koşul; olası bir durumun gelecekteki muhtemel sonucunu anlatmak için, örneğin uzay yolculuğuyla ilgili tahminlerde kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • If + subject + present simple, subject + will + base verb.
  • Example: If we build better rockets, we will reach Mars sooner.

Negative

  • If + subject + do/does not + verb, subject + will not + verb.
  • Example: If we do not invest in research, we will not discover new planets.

Question

  • Will ... if ...? / What will happen if ...?
  • Example: What will happen if the mission fails?

Short answers

  • Yes, it will. / No, it will not.
Mistakes
  • If scientists will find water, they will send a mission.If scientists find water, they will send a mission.

    Do not use 'will' in the if-clause of a first conditional.

    TRBirinci koşulda 'if' cümleciğinde 'will' kullanılmaz.

  • If we not protect the Earth, life will be harder.If we do not protect the Earth, life will be harder.

    Use 'do not/does not' for the negative present simple in the if-clause.

    TRIf cümleciğinde olumsuzluk için 'do not/does not' kullanılmalı.

Our Neighbours in Space

Our solar system has eight planets that orbit the Sun. Some, like Mercury and Venus, are close to the Sun and extremely hot. Others, like Neptune and Uranus, are far away and freezing cold. Earth sits in a special zone where the temperature is just right for liquid water, and that is one reason life exists here.

Mars is often called the 'Red Planet' because of the iron dust on its surface. Scientists are very interested in Mars because they believe it once had rivers and lakes. If future missions find clear signs of water underground, researchers will search even harder for signs of past or present life. Several robotic rovers have already explored the surface, sending back photos and data.

Jupiter, the largest planet, is a giant ball of gas with a famous storm called the Great Red Spot. It does not have a solid surface, so astronauts could never land on it. Saturn is easy to recognise because of its beautiful rings, made of ice and rock. Both planets have many moons, and some of these moons, like Europa and Enceladus, may have oceans hidden under thick ice.

Space agencies around the world continue to plan new missions. If governments and companies keep investing in space technology, humans will probably visit Mars within the next few decades. Until then, telescopes and robotic explorers remain our best way to learn about our fascinating neighbours in space.

Are We Alone in the Universe?

For centuries, people have wondered whether life exists beyond Earth. Scientists now use powerful telescopes to search for planets outside our solar system, called exoplanets. If a planet is the right distance from its star and has water, scientists consider it a good place to look for life.

So far, no one has found clear proof of alien life. However, researchers have discovered thousands of exoplanets, and some of them are similar in size to Earth. A project called SETI (Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence) listens for radio signals from space, hoping to detect a message from another civilisation. If SETI ever receives a real signal, it will be one of the most important discoveries in human history.

Some scientists focus closer to home. They study Mars, and the icy moons of Jupiter and Saturn, because simple forms of life, like bacteria, might survive there. If future missions bring back samples from these places, laboratories on Earth will test them carefully for any sign of life.

Many people find the question exciting rather than frightening. Finding even simple microbial life beyond Earth would completely change how humans see their place in the universe. Until that day comes, scientists continue to search, explore, and imagine what — or who — might be out there.

A Day in the Life of an Astronaut

1 Before listening

  • What do you imagine an astronaut's daily routine looks like?
  • What questions would you ask an astronaut about life in space?
0:00

2 While listening

Transkripti göster

InterviewerThanks for joining us today. Can you describe a normal day on the space station?

AstronautOf course! We usually wake up around six o'clock. If the schedule allows it, we start with about two hours of exercise, because muscles get weaker without gravity.

InterviewerThat's interesting. What happens after exercise?

AstronautWe check the equipment and do experiments. If something breaks, we have to fix it ourselves, because there is no repair shop up here!

InterviewerHow do you eat and sleep in space?

AstronautOur food comes in special packets. If we do not close them properly, food floats around the cabin! We sleep in small sleeping bags attached to the wall.

InterviewerWhat do you miss most from Earth?

AstronautHonestly, fresh air and rain. If I go outside on Earth and feel the wind, I always appreciate it more after a mission.

InterviewerOne last question — what happens if there is an emergency?

AstronautWe train for years for that. If an emergency happens, we follow strict procedures and stay calm. Training makes a huge difference.

3 After listening
