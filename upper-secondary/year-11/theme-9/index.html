In this theme, students describe the people, places and events in their lives and learn how to ask for and give clarification when something is not clear.

Konular

  • Describing events, places and people
  • Asking for and giving clarification

Dil yapıları

  • Relative Clauses (who, which, that, whose)
  • Relative adverbs (where, when, why)
  • Defining vs non-defining relative clauses

Anlayabileceğim

  • I can understand descriptions of people, places and events using relative clauses.
  • I can understand someone asking for clarification about something I said.
  • I can understand a short story about friendship.
  • I can understand a conversation in which two friends describe a mutual friend.
  • I can understand a description of a memorable place a speaker visited with friends.
  • I can understand the difference between defining and non-defining information about a person or place.
  • I can understand simple written texts about friendship and social events.

Söyleyebileceğim

  • I can describe a friend using relative clauses (who/which/that/whose).
  • I can describe a place where something happened, using 'where'.
  • I can describe the reason for an event, using 'why'.
  • I can ask someone to clarify or explain something they said.
  • I can give clarification when someone doesn't understand me.
  • I can talk about a memorable event with my friends.
  • I can compare two friends or two places using relative clauses.
  • I can describe qualities I value in a friend.

Yazabileceğim

  • I can write a description of a friend using relative clauses.
  • I can write a short paragraph about a memorable event with friends.
  • I can write sentences that add extra information about people or places.
  • I can write a short message asking for or giving clarification.

Relative Clauses — who/which/that/whose (defining) and where/when/why

1 · Notice
  • This is the friend who helped me move to my new house.
  • The café which we visited last weekend has closed down.
  • I still remember the day when we first met.
  • That's the boy whose sister is in my English class.

What do you notice about the words who, which, when and whose, and what kind of word do they follow?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Complete each sentence with the correct relative pronoun or adverb from the bank.

whowhichwhosewherewhy

Well done — you can now choose the correct relative word!

B Build sentences

Combine the two cues into one sentence with a relative clause, as in the model.

  1. girl / sits next to me / is friendly

    ✓ The girl who sits next to me is friendly.

  2. boy / lives next door / plays football with me
    Model answer

    The boy who lives next door plays football with me.

  3. park / we play basketball / is near my house
    Model answer

    The park where we play basketball is near my house.

  4. day / we became friends / I will never forget
    Model answer

    I will never forget the day when we became friends.

  5. girl / brother studies abroad / sits behind me
    Model answer

    The girl whose brother studies abroad sits behind me.

  6. reason / she was upset / was a misunderstanding
    Model answer

    The reason why she was upset was a misunderstanding.

  7. book / I borrowed from Elif / is very interesting
    Model answer

    The book which I borrowed from Elif is very interesting.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original sentences describing a friend, a place, or an event using who, which, that, whose, where, when or why.

My best friend is someone who...The place where we usually meet is...I'll never forget the day when...
Rule

We use defining relative clauses to give essential information that identifies exactly which person, thing, place, time or reason we are talking about, without which the sentence would not make full sense. We use who/that for people, which/that for things, whose to show possession, where for places, when for times, and why for reasons. Defining relative clauses are not separated by commas.

TRBelirten (defining) sıfat cümleciklerini, hakkında konuşulan kişi, şey, yer, zaman ya da sebebi tam olarak belirlemek için gereken, cümlenin anlamı için gerekli bilgiyi vermek amacıyla kullanırız; bu bilgi olmadan cümle eksik kalır. İnsanlar için who/that, nesneler için which/that, sahiplik için whose, yerler için where, zamanlar için when, sebepler için why kullanılır. Belirten sıfat cümlecikleri virgülle ayrılmaz.

When?

Use a defining relative clause whenever the listener needs the extra information to know exactly who or what you mean; if you removed the clause, the sentence would become unclear or too general.

TRDinleyicinin tam olarak kimi ya da neyi kastettiğinizi anlaması için ek bilgiye ihtiyacı olduğunda belirten sıfat cümleciği kullanılır; bu cümlecik çıkarılırsa cümle belirsiz ya da çok genel hale gelir.

3 · Form

Positive

  • The person who/that helps me is my friend.
  • The place where we meet is a café.
  • The reason why she left is unclear.

Negative

  • The person who doesn't help me isn't a real friend.
  • The place where we don't usually go is quiet.

Question

  • Is he the friend who called you yesterday?
  • Is this the café where you met your friend?

Short answers

  • Yes, he's the one who called.
  • Yes, that's the place where we met.
Mistakes
  • This is the girl which is my best friend.This is the girl who is my best friend.

    Use 'who' or 'that' for people, not 'which'.

    TRİnsanlar için 'which' değil, 'who' ya da 'that' kullanılır.

  • That's the café, where we always meet.That's the café where we always meet.

    Defining relative clauses do not take a comma before them.

    TRBelirten sıfat cümleciklerinden önce virgül kullanılmaz.

  • He is the boy who's bag is red.He is the boy whose bag is red.

    'Whose' shows possession; don't confuse it with the contraction 'who's' (who is).

    TR'Whose' sahiplik bildirir; 'who is' kısaltması olan 'who's' ile karıştırılmamalıdır.

  • I remember the day which we met.I remember the day when we met.

    Use 'when' for times, not 'which'.

    TRZamanlar için 'which' değil 'when' kullanılır.

who/which/that vs. where/when/why
who / which / that / whosewhere / when / why

Defining vs. Non-defining Relative Clauses

1 · Notice
  • The friend who lives next door lent me her notes. (no comma — essential)
  • My sister, who lives in Ankara, is visiting us this weekend. (comma — extra info)
  • The book that I borrowed was about friendship. (no comma — essential)
  • Kerem, whose father is a teacher, helped me with my homework. (comma — extra info)

What do you notice about the commas in the second and fourth sentences, and what happens to the meaning if you remove the clause?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Decide if each sentence needs commas (non-defining) or not (defining), then complete it correctly.

whowhichwhose,no comma

Great — you can now tell essential from extra information!

B Build sentences

Turn each pair of facts into one sentence, choosing defining or non-defining depending on whether the person/place is already named.

  1. My friend Cem / lives in Bursa (already named)

    ✓ My friend Cem, who lives in Bursa, called me yesterday.

  2. The girl / sits next to me in class (not named)
    Model answer

    The girl who sits next to me in class is very friendly.

  3. My teacher, Mr. Aydın / organised the trip (already named)
    Model answer

    My teacher, Mr. Aydın, who organised the trip, is very kind.

  4. The restaurant / we celebrated at (not named)
    Model answer

    The restaurant where we celebrated was full of our friends.

  5. İstanbul / has many old friends of mine (already named)
    Model answer

    İstanbul, where many of my old friends live, is a huge city.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 sentences about people or places you know, using both a defining and a non-defining relative clause correctly.

My friend ___, who ..., ...The place where ... is ...
Rule

A defining relative clause gives essential information without which the sentence loses its meaning, and it is not separated by commas. A non-defining relative clause adds extra, non-essential information about someone or something already identified (often a name or a unique place), and it is separated by commas. 'That' can replace who/which in defining clauses but not in non-defining clauses.

TRBelirten (defining) sıfat cümleciği, çıkarıldığında cümlenin anlamını kaybettiği temel bilgiyi verir ve virgülle ayrılmaz. Belirtmeyen (non-defining) sıfat cümleciği ise zaten tanımlanmış bir kişi ya da şey (genellikle bir isim ya da tek bir yer) hakkında ek, gereksiz olmayan ama zorunlu da olmayan bilgi verir ve virgülle ayrılır. 'That', belirten cümleciklerde who/which yerine kullanılabilir ama belirtmeyen cümleciklerde kullanılamaz.

When?

Choose non-defining when the person or place is already clearly identified (a name, 'my mother', a capital city) and you are only adding extra detail; choose defining when the clause is needed to say exactly who or what you mean.

TRKişi ya da yer zaten açıkça belirlenmişse (bir isim, 'annem', bir başkent gibi) ve sadece ek bir bilgi ekleniyorsa belirtmeyen cümlecik; tam olarak kimi ya da neyi kastettiğinizi belirtmek gerekiyorsa belirten cümlecik seçilir.

3 · Form

Positive

  • The boy who called me is my cousin. (defining)
  • Emre, who called me, is my cousin. (non-defining)

Negative

  • The boy who didn't call me is my cousin.
  • Emre, who didn't call me, is my cousin.

Question

  • Is the boy who called you your cousin?
  • Is Emre, who called you, your cousin?

Short answers

  • Yes, he is.
  • Yes, he is.
Mistakes
  • My mother who lives in Balıkesir called me.My mother, who lives in Balıkesir, called me.

    'My mother' is already unique, so the clause is non-defining and needs commas.

    TR'My mother' zaten tek kişidir, bu yüzden cümlecik belirtmeyen türdedir ve virgül gerekir.

  • The friend, that helped me, is very kind.The friend that helped me is very kind.

    'That' is only used in defining clauses, which never take commas.

    TR'That' yalnızca belirten cümleciklerde kullanılır ve bu cümlecikler asla virgül almaz.

  • My best friend Deniz who I trust most.My best friend Deniz, whom I trust most, always helps me.

    A non-defining clause needs commas and the main sentence still needs its own verb.

    TRBelirtmeyen cümlecik virgül gerektirir ve ana cümlenin de kendi yüklemi olmalıdır.

The Friend Who Never Gave Up on Me

Everyone has a friend who they will never forget, and for me, that friend is Ceren. We met in the seventh grade, on the first day when our new class was formed, and we have been close ever since. Ceren is the kind of person who notices when something is wrong, even before you say a word.

Two years ago, I was going through a difficult period at school, which affected my grades and my confidence. Ceren, who could see that I was struggling, started walking home with me every day just to talk. She never told me what to do; instead, she asked questions that helped me understand my own feelings better.

The place where we had our longest conversations was a small park near our school, which has a bench under an old tree. That bench is where we solved most of our problems, from small arguments with classmates to bigger worries about the future. It became a kind of second home for us.

What I admire most about Ceren is her loyalty. She is a friend whose honesty I can always trust, even when the truth is uncomfortable. Last year, when I made a decision that most people disagreed with, Ceren was the only one who asked me to explain my reasons before judging me.

Now that we are in different classes, we don't see each other every day, but the bond we built has not weakened. Whenever I need someone to talk to, Ceren is still the first person I call. She taught me that a real friend is someone who stays, not just when things are easy, but especially when they are not.

Our Ten-Year Reunion

Last month, my old classmates and I organised a reunion, which took place exactly ten years after we finished middle school. The event, which was held in a restaurant near our old school, brought together almost thirty former students, some of whom I hadn't seen since graduation.

The person who worked hardest to organise everything was Burak, a classmate whose energy for planning events never seems to disappear. He created a group chat where everyone could share memories, photos and, eventually, their availability for the big day.

When the evening finally arrived, it was strange to see faces that I remembered as children now belonging to adults with jobs, families and completely different lives. The first person I recognised was Selin, whose laugh had not changed at all in ten years. We talked for almost an hour about the summer when our whole class went camping together.

One moment that made everyone emotional was when we watched an old video, which someone had saved from our final week of school. In the video, our teacher, who had since retired, gave a short speech about friendship that none of us had fully understood at the age of fourteen.

By the end of the night, we had exchanged numbers, made plans for a smaller gathering, and remembered why some of these friendships, despite the years when we drifted apart, still mattered. As Burak said in his closing toast, a good friend is someone who, no matter how much time passes, can still make you feel like you're fifteen again.

Use English

Nasıl çalışılır?

  1. Useful language kutusundaki kalıpları oku.
  2. Konuşma görevini bir arkadaşınla yap ya da kendini kaydet.
  3. Yazma planını izleyerek taslağını yaz.
  4. Kontrol listesi ile yazını gözden geçir.
SpeakingPairs8-10 min

Describe Your Friend

Describe a close friend to your partner using at least three relative clauses (who/which/that/whose/where/when/why), while your partner listens and asks for clarification when needed.

Useful language

This is a friend who...We met at a place where...Could you clarify what you mean by...?In other words,...Sorry, could you explain that a bit more?

Adımlar

  1. Think of one close friend and note down three facts about them (personality, a shared place, a shared memory).
  2. Take turns describing your friend to your partner using relative clauses.
  3. The listener must ask at least one clarification question during the description.
  4. The speaker answers by rephrasing or explaining further.
  5. Switch roles and repeat.
DestekGive students a short list of sentence starters and relative pronouns to choose from before they speak.
HedefStudents describe their friend using at least three different relative pronouns/adverbs correctly.
İleriStudents add a non-defining relative clause and compare their friend to another person they know.
SpeakingGroups of 3-410 min

A Memorable Event with Friends

Take turns telling your group about a memorable event you shared with friends, explaining where it happened, when it happened, and why it was memorable, while the group asks clarifying questions.

Useful language

It happened at a place where...It was the year when...The reason why it was special was...Sorry, what do you mean by...?So, in other words, you're saying...

Adımlar

  1. Think individually of one memorable event with friends for one minute.
  2. In your group, take turns telling your story, using relative clauses to add detail.
  3. After each story, at least two group members must ask a clarification question.
  4. The speaker responds by giving clarification, rephrasing where necessary.
  5. As a group, decide whose story was the most memorable and why.
DestekProvide a simple story outline (what/where/when/why) for students who need extra structure.
HedefStudents tell a clear story using at least two relative clauses and respond appropriately to clarification questions.
İleriStudents are asked follow-up questions requiring them to use both defining and non-defining relative clauses in their answers.
Writing120-150 words

A Portrait of My Best Friend

Write a descriptive paragraph about your best friend, using at least four relative clauses to describe their personality, a place you share, and a memorable event together.

Plan

  1. Note down your friend's name and three personality traits.
  2. Think of a place that is special to your friendship and why.
