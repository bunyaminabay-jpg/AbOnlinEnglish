Theme 8 focuses on the language of sports: reporting sports news, interviewing athletes, and talking about games, training, and competitions, while building the grammar of Reported Speech.

Konular

  • Reporting news
  • Interviewing someone
  • Talking about sports

Dil yapıları

  • Reported Speech (statements, tense back-shift, pronoun/time changes)

Anlayabileceğim

  • I can understand a short sports news report on radio or TV.
  • I can understand an interview with an athlete or coach.
  • I can understand statements reported by someone else (e.g. 'He said he was tired').
  • I can understand a text about a sports event or championship.
  • I can follow a conversation about training routines and rules of a game.
  • I can understand quotes from players and coaches in a sports article.

Söyleyebileceğim

  • I can report what someone said using reported speech.
  • I can ask and answer interview questions about sports.
  • I can talk about my favourite sport and why I like it.
  • I can describe a match or competition I watched or played in.
  • I can give my opinion about fair play and sportsmanship.
  • I can compare two sports or two athletes.
  • I can talk about the benefits of doing sports regularly.

Yazabileceğim

  • I can write a short sports news report.
  • I can write an interview script with questions and answers.
  • I can rewrite direct speech from an interview as reported speech.
  • I can write a paragraph about a sports event I attended or watched.

Reported Speech — Statements & Tense Back-shift

1 · Notice
  • Direct: "I am tired," said the coach. → Reported: The coach said he was tired.
  • Direct: "We trained hard," she said. → Reported: She said they had trained hard.
  • Direct: "I will play tomorrow," he said. → Reported: He said he would play the next day.
  • Direct: "I can run fast," the athlete said. → Reported: The athlete said she could run fast.

What happens to the verb tense when a direct statement becomes reported speech?

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original sentences reporting something a coach, athlete, or commentator said about a sports event.

The coach said...The commentator explained that...The player admitted that...
Rule

When we report a statement, the reporting verb (said, told) is usually in the past, so the tense of the original statement moves one step back into the past: present simple → past simple, present continuous → past continuous, past simple → past perfect, will → would, can → could.

TRBir ifadeyi aktarırken, aktaran fiil (said, told) genellikle geçmiş zamanda olduğu için, orijinal cümlenin zamanı bir adım geriye kayar: geniş zaman → geçmiş zaman, şimdiki zaman → geçmiş zamanın hikayesi, geçmiş zaman → miş'li geçmiş (had + V3), will → would, can → could.

When?

We use reported speech to tell someone what another person said, without quoting their exact words — very common in sports news reports and interview summaries.

TRReported speech, başka birinin söylediklerini onun tam kelimelerini alıntılamadan aktarmak için kullanılır — spor haberlerinde ve röportaj özetlerinde çok sık görülür.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Subject + said/told (object) + (that) + clause with back-shifted tense

Negative

  • Subject + said + (that) + subject + did not/had not + verb

Question

  • Not used for statements — see reporting questions in a later theme.

Short answers

  • He said he was tired.
  • She told me she had trained hard.
Mistakes
  • He said he is tired.He said he was tired.

    The present tense must back-shift to past tense in reported speech.

    TRReported speech'te şimdiki/geniş zaman geçmiş zamana kaymalıdır.

  • She told that she won the race.She said that she had won the race.

    'Tell' needs an object (tell someone); use 'said' without an object, and back-shift the tense.

    TR'Tell' bir nesne alır (tell someone); nesnesiz kullanımda 'said' tercih edilir ve zaman geri kaydırılır.

Reported Speech — Pronouns, Time Expressions & Reporting Verbs

1 · Notice
  • Direct: "I will win tomorrow," she said. → Reported: She said she would win the next day.
  • Direct: "We played here last week," they said. → Reported: They said they had played there the week before.
  • Direct: "This is my best season," the athlete said. → Reported: The athlete said that was her best season.
  • Direct: "I'm training now," he said. → Reported: He said he was training then.

How do pronouns and time/place words change when direct speech becomes reported speech?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Complete the sentence with the correct pronoun or time expression.

the day beforeherthat daythere

Great work adjusting pronouns and time words!

B Build sentences

Build the reported sentence, choosing the correct reporting verb (said/told) and adjusting pronouns and time words.

  1. player tell reporter I / feel / great today

    ✓ The player told the reporter (that) he felt great that day.

  2. coach tell journalist my team / train / hard this week
    Model answer

    The coach told the journalist (that) his team was training hard that week.

  3. captain say we / meet / here tomorrow
    Model answer

    The captain said (that) they would meet there the next day.

  4. athlete tell fans I / retire / next year
    Model answer

    The athlete told the fans (that) she would retire the following year.

  5. referee explain the decision / be / correct
    Model answer

    The referee explained (that) the decision was correct.

  6. commentator add this match / be / the best of the season
    Model answer

    The commentator added (that) that match was the best of the season.

C Your turn

Imagine you interviewed an athlete yesterday. Write 2-3 sentences reporting what they said, using 'told' at least once and changing pronouns/time expressions correctly.

The athlete told me that...She explained that...He added that...
Rule

In reported speech, pronouns change to match the reporter's point of view (I → he/she, my → his/her, we → they), and time/place expressions shift away from the moment of speaking (today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the day before, here → there). We also choose the reporting verb carefully: 'say' does not take a personal object (say something), while 'tell' needs one (tell someone something).

TRReported speech'te zamirler, aktaran kişinin bakış açısına göre değişir (I → he/she, my → his/her, we → they); zaman/yer ifadeleri de konuşma anından uzaklaşacak şekilde değişir (today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the day before, here → there). Ayrıca aktaran fiil dikkatle seçilir: 'say' kişisel bir nesne almaz (say something), 'tell' ise bir nesneye ihtiyaç duyar (tell someone something).

When?

We use these changes whenever the reporting happens at a different time or place from the original statement — for example, a journalist reporting a coach's comments a day after they were made.

TRBu değişiklikler, aktarma işlemi orijinal ifadenin söylendiği zamandan veya yerden farklı bir zaman/yerde yapıldığında kullanılır — örneğin bir gazetecinin, bir antrenörün yorumlarını ertesi gün aktarması gibi.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Subject + said/told (object) + (that) + clause with adjusted pronouns & time/place words

Negative

  • Subject + said/told + (that) + subject + did not/had not + verb (adjusted pronouns & time/place words)

Question

  • Not covered in this point — see statement patterns above.

Short answers

  • He told me he was training then.
  • They said they had played there.
Mistakes
  • She said me she was tired.She told me she was tired.

    'Say' cannot take a personal object directly; use 'tell' when naming who was told.

    TR'Say' doğrudan kişisel bir nesne almaz; kime söylendiği belirtilecekse 'tell' kullanılır.

  • He said he will play here tomorrow.He said he would play there the next day.

    Both the modal (will→would) and the time/place words (here→there, tomorrow→the next day) must change.

    TRHem kip (will→would) hem de zaman/yer ifadeleri (here→there, tomorrow→the next day) değişmelidir.

A Historic Win for the City Team

Last Saturday, the City Basketball Team achieved a historic victory in the regional championship final, defeating their long-time rivals by a score of 78 to 74. The match, held at the packed City Sports Hall, kept thousands of supporters on the edge of their seats until the final minute.

After the game, the team captain spoke to reporters. She said she had never felt so proud of her teammates. She explained that the players had trained twice a day for the past month and that their hard work had finally paid off. She also added that the whole team had believed in victory even when they were losing by ten points in the third quarter.

The coach told journalists that the turning point of the match had come when a young substitute player scored three important baskets in a row. He said the young player had shown great courage under pressure and that he was extremely proud of the entire squad. He also mentioned that the team would begin preparing immediately for the national tournament next month.

Fans who attended the match praised both teams for their sportsmanship. One supporter said the atmosphere in the hall had been unforgettable, with both sets of fans cheering loudly throughout the game. Local newspapers described the victory as one of the most exciting sporting moments the city had seen in years, and many are already looking forward to the national tournament.

Interview: Life of a Young Athlete

Our school magazine recently interviewed Deniz Aksoy, a nineteen-year-old swimmer who recently qualified for the national team. In the interview, Deniz talked openly about training, sacrifice, and staying motivated.

When asked about her daily routine, Deniz explained that she woke up at five every morning to train before school. She said that balancing school work and swimming was the most difficult part of her life as a student athlete. She added that her family had always supported her, even when she felt like giving up.

The interviewer asked Deniz what advice she would give to young people who wanted to become athletes. She replied that discipline was more important than natural talent, and she said that anyone could improve with consistent practice. She also mentioned that she had failed to qualify for the national team twice before finally succeeding this year.

Deniz told the interviewer that her biggest goal was to represent her country at an international competition. She said she was not afraid of losing, because every competition taught her something new. Finally, she admitted that without the support of her coach and family, she would never have achieved her dream.

The interview ended with Deniz thanking her school for allowing her to combine her studies with intense training, and she encouraged other students to follow their passions, whatever they might be.

SpeakingPairs8-10 min

Athlete Interview Role-Play

One student plays a journalist and the other plays an athlete or coach after an important match. The journalist asks at least five questions; the athlete/coach answers in character. Afterwards, each pair reports three things their partner said to the class using reported speech.

Useful language

Can you tell us how you feel about...?What was the turning point of the match?I felt that...Our coach told us that...He/She said (that)...He/She told me (that)...

Adımlar

  1. In pairs, decide who is the journalist and who is the athlete/coach.
  2. The journalist prepares 5 interview questions about a recent match or competition.
  3. Role-play the interview, speaking naturally.
  4. Swap roles and repeat with a new topic.
  5. Each student reports 3 things their partner said to the class, using reported speech.
DestekGive weaker pairs a list of ready-made interview questions and sentence starters for reporting ('He said that...', 'She told me that...').
HedefStudents create their own questions and answers, then report using at least two different reporting verbs.
İleriStronger pairs add follow-up questions and report using a wider range of reporting verbs (admit, explain, claim, deny).
SpeakingGroups of 3-410-12 min

Sports Debate: Fair Play vs Winning at All Costs

In small groups, students discuss whether winning is more important than playing fairly, using examples from sports they know. Each group presents a short summary of their discussion to the class, including at least one reported statement from a group member.

Useful language

In my opinion...I think fair play is more important because...Some people believe that...One of my classmates said that...I disagree because...

Adımlar

  1. In groups, discuss: Is it acceptable to break the rules if it helps your team win?
  2. Give real or imagined examples of fair play and unfair play in sports.
  3. Agree on a short group opinion.
  4. Choose one group member to summarise the discussion for the class, quoting a group member's opinion in reported speech.
DestekProvide a simple opinion frame: 'I think... because...' and a short vocabulary list (fair, unfair, rule, cheat, respect).
HedefStudents give reasons and at least one real or invented example to support their opinion.
İleriStronger groups compare fair play rules across two different sports and report contrasting opinions from group members.
Writing120-150 words

Write a Sports News Report

Write a short sports news report about an imaginary match or competition. Include a headline, the result, at least two reported statements from a player or coach, and a closing sentence about what happens next.

Plan

  1. Choose a sport and decide the result of the match/competition.
  2. Write a short, catchy headline.
  3. Note down 2-3 quotes a player or coach might have said.
  4. Turn those quotes into reported speech for your report.
  5. Add a final sentence about future plans (next match, tournament, etc.).

Interview Script and Summary

Write a short interview script (5 questions and answers) with an imaginary athlete or coach about a recent competition. Then write a short paragraph (4-6 sentences) summarising the interview using reported speech.

Plan

  1. Choose your interviewee (athlete, coach) and the topic (a recent win, a big goal, an injury, etc.).
  2. Write 5 interview questions.
  3. Write natural, original answers for the interviewee.

I can…

Ödev — seviyeni seç

Destek
  • Match 8 sports vocabulary words from this theme to their Turkish meanings.
  • Fill in 5 gap-fill sentences changing direct speech to reported speech, using a given word bank.
  • Copy and complete 4 sentences: "I am tired," she said. → She said she ___ tired.
  • Listen again to Listening 1 and write down 3 facts you hear.
Standart
  • Rewrite 6 direct sentences from a short sports dialogue into reported speech.
  • Write a 100-word sports news report about an imaginary match, including 2 reported statements.
  • Write 5 original interview questions for an athlete and short model answers.
  • Complete 6 mixed practice items (gap-fill, MCQ, rewrite) on Reported Speech.
İleri
  • Write a 150-word interview summary using at least 4 different reporting verbs (said, told, explained, admitted).
  • Find or imagine 3 real quotes from athletes and rewrite them all in reported speech, noting any tricky changes.
  • Write a short dialogue between a journalist and two athletes with contrasting opinions, then summarise it in reported speech.
  • Research (in your own words, not copied) one famous sports event and write a short reported-speech news paragraph about it.
