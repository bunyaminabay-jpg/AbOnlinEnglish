This theme explores hobbies, pastimes, and personal skills, teaching students to express likes, dislikes, and preferences, and to talk about what they can and could do at different points in their lives.

Konular

  • Expressing likes, dislikes and interests
  • Expressing preferences
  • Talking about present and past abilities

Dil yapıları

  • Verbs of liking/disliking (enjoy, love, hate, can't stand)
  • Adjective + preposition (fond of, keen on, interested in, crazy about)
  • Can / could (ability, present & past)

Anlayabileceğim

  • I can understand people talking about their hobbies and free-time activities.
  • I can understand someone explaining why they like or dislike an activity.
  • I can understand a description of a person's skills and abilities.
  • I can understand someone comparing two hobbies and saying which they prefer.
  • I can understand a short story about learning a new skill.
  • I can understand simple instructions for a hobby or craft.
  • I can understand people talking about abilities they had as children.

Söyleyebileceğim

  • I can talk about my hobbies and free-time interests.
  • I can say what I enjoy, love, hate, or can't stand doing.
  • I can describe my preferences between two activities.
  • I can say what I am good at and what I find difficult.
  • I can talk about skills I have now and skills I had as a child.
  • I can ask a classmate about their hobbies and abilities.
  • I can give reasons for my likes and dislikes.
  • I can take part in a short conversation about weekend activities.

Yazabileceğim

  • I can write a paragraph describing my hobbies and interests.
  • I can write sentences comparing two activities I like.
  • I can write about a skill I learned and how I learned it.
  • I can write a short profile of a person's hobbies and talents.

Rule

After enjoy, love, hate, and can't stand, we use the -ing form of the verb (the gerund), not the infinitive.

TREnjoy, love, hate ve can't stand fiillerinden sonra fiilin -ing hâli (gerund) kullanılır, to'lu mastar kullanılmaz.

When?

We use these verbs to talk about general feelings and interests, especially when describing hobbies and free-time activities.

TRBu fiiller, özellikle hobiler ve boş zaman etkinlikleri anlatılırken genel duygu ve ilgi alanlarını ifade etmek için kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • I enjoy painting.
  • She loves collecting stamps.

Negative

  • I don't enjoy painting.
  • She doesn't love collecting stamps.

Question

  • Do you enjoy painting?
  • Does she love collecting stamps?

Short answers

  • Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
  • Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.
Mistakes
  • I enjoy to paint.I enjoy painting.

    Enjoy is never followed by to + infinitive.

    TREnjoy fiilinden sonra asla to'lu mastar kullanılmaz.

  • She hate play chess.She hates playing chess.

    Remember the third-person -s on hate and the -ing form after it.

    TRÜçüncü tekil şahısta hate fiiline -s eklenir ve ardından -ing hâli gelir.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original sentences about a skill you can do now and a skill you could (or couldn't) do as a child.

I can...When I was young, I could...
Rule

We use can + base verb for present ability and could + base verb for past ability. Both are modal verbs and never take -s or 'to'.

TRŞimdiki zamandaki yetenek için can + fiilin yalın hâli, geçmişteki yetenek için could + fiilin yalın hâli kullanılır. İkisi de yardımcı fiildir ve -s ya da 'to' almaz.

When?

We use can/could to talk about general abilities and skills, not about a single completed action in the past.

TRCan/could, tek bir tamamlanmış geçmiş eylem için değil, genel yetenek ve becerileri anlatmak için kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • I can swim.
  • She could swim when she was six.

Negative

  • I can't swim.
  • She couldn't swim when she was three.

Question

  • Can you swim?
  • Could she swim at six?

Short answers

  • Yes, I can. / No, I can't.
  • Yes, she could. / No, she couldn't.
Mistakes
  • She cans play the piano.She can play the piano.

    Modal verbs like can never take an -s in the third person.

    TRCan gibi yardımcı fiiller üçüncü tekil şahısta -s almaz.

  • I could to ride a bike when I was five.I could ride a bike when I was five.

    Could is followed directly by the base verb, without 'to'.

    TRCould'dan sonra 'to' kullanılmadan doğrudan fiilin yalın hâli gelir.

Compare
can vs could
can (present ability)could (past ability)
Ability nowcan + verbI can play chess well.could + verbI could play chess when I was eight.
Negativecan't + verbI can't draw very well.couldn't + verbI couldn't draw at all as a small child.

The Weekend Woodcarver

Ahmet works as an accountant during the week, but every Saturday morning he becomes a completely different person. He puts on an old apron, opens his small garden shed, and picks up a piece of wood and a set of carving tools. Ahmet is passionate about woodcarving, and he has been practising this hobby for almost six years.

It all started when his grandfather gave him a small wooden bird he had carved himself. Ahmet was fascinated and decided he wanted to learn the same skill. At first, he was a complete novice and found the work slow and difficult. He couldn't even carve a simple spoon without cutting his fingers. However, he was fond of the quiet, careful process, and he refused to give up.

Ahmet joined a local craft club, where more experienced members were happy to share tips with beginners. He practised for at least two hours every weekend, and slowly his skills improved. Now he can carve detailed animal figures, small boxes, and even simple furniture. His favourite pieces are wooden birds, just like the one his grandfather gave him.

Ahmet says that woodcarving helps him relax after a stressful week at work. "I enjoy focusing on one small task and seeing something beautiful appear from a plain piece of wood," he explains. He is now keen on entering his work in a regional craft competition next spring, something he could never have imagined doing when he first picked up a carving knife.

Twins, Different Talents

Elif and Deniz are twin sisters, but their hobbies could hardly be more different. Elif is crazy about rock climbing. Every weekend, she travels to a climbing centre outside the city, where she can now climb walls that would have seemed impossible to her two years ago. As a child, she couldn't even climb the small tree in their garden without help, so her family is amazed at how far she has come.

Deniz, on the other hand, is interested in something much quieter: chess. She loves sitting for hours studying old games and planning her next move. She joined the school chess club in Year 9 and quickly became one of its strongest players. Unlike her sister, Deniz has always preferred activities that let her think carefully rather than move quickly.

Their parents say that the girls have always had opposite personalities, even though they look almost identical. Elif enjoys taking risks and testing her body's limits, while Deniz prefers solving problems calmly in her mind. Interestingly, both girls agree on one thing: they can't stand giving up on a challenge, whether it's a difficult climbing route or a tricky chess puzzle.

Last month, the twins decided to try something new together: a beginner's pottery class. Neither of them had any experience, and at first their bowls looked more like flat plates. However, after a few lessons, both sisters could shape a simple, even bowl, and they agreed it was fun to be complete beginners together for once.

Can You? Could You?

In small groups, students find out what abilities each group member has now and had as a child, then present the most surprising ability to the class.

Useful language

Can you...?Could you... when you were younger?I can... but I couldn't... when I was little.That's amazing!

Adımlar

  1. In groups, ask each other about three present abilities and three past abilities.
  2. Note down each person's most impressive or surprising answer.
  3. Agree on the one ability from the group that surprised everyone most.
  4. Present that ability to the class in a short sentence, e.g. 'Ayşe could ride a horse when she was five.'

I can…

Ödev — seviyeni seç

Destek
  • Match 8 hobby vocabulary words to their Turkish meanings.
  • Complete 5 gap-fill sentences using enjoy, love, hate, or can't stand + -ing.
  • Copy and complete 4 sentences with can or could about a family member's abilities.
  • Draw a picture of your favourite hobby and label it with 3 vocabulary words.
Standart
  • Write 6 sentences about your own hobbies using verbs of liking/disliking.
  • Write 4 sentences using fond of, keen on, interested in, and crazy about.
  • Write a short paragraph (80-100 words) about a skill you can do now.
  • Complete the theme's practice questions for grammar point g3 (can/could).
İleri
  • Interview a family member about their hobbies and write a short report (100-120 words).
  • Write a paragraph comparing two hobbies you could try, using preference structures.
  • Research a hobby you have never tried and write 5 sentences about why you might enjoy it.
  • Write a short dialogue between two friends deciding on a new hobby to try together.
