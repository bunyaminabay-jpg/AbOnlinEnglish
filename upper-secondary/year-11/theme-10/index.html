This theme explores values and norms — the shared ideas about right and wrong behaviour that guide people's lives — and how to express opinions, exchange ideas, and make comments about them politely.

Konular

  • Expressing opinions
  • Exchanging ideas
  • Making comments

Dil yapıları

  • Correlative conjunctions (both...and, either...or, neither...nor, not only...but also)

Anlayabileceğim

  • I can understand people expressing opinions about values such as honesty and kindness
  • I can understand a discussion in which speakers agree or disagree about a value
  • I can understand someone describing a custom or norm from their own culture
  • I can understand a speaker explaining why a value matters to them
  • I can understand spoken comments made in response to someone else's opinion
  • I can understand written texts comparing customs from different places

Söyleyebileceğim

  • I can express my opinion about which value matters most
  • I can exchange ideas with a partner about values and customs
  • I can make a polite comment on someone else's opinion
  • I can agree or disagree with an opinion politely
  • I can compare two customs or norms and explain the value behind each one
  • I can describe a value or custom from my own family or community

Yazabileceğim

  • I can write sentences using correlative conjunctions to link ideas about values
  • I can write a short paragraph giving my opinion about an important value
  • I can write a paragraph comparing two customs or norms
  • I can write a short comment responding to someone else's opinion

Correlative Conjunctions — both...and, either...or, neither...nor, not only...but also

1 · Notice
  • She is both honest and kind to everyone she meets.
  • You can either agree with his opinion or explain why you disagree.
  • He is neither rude nor selfish; he always thinks of others.
  • Good manners are not only about words but also about actions.

What do you notice about the words in bold pairs — how many parts do they have, and what kind of ideas do they connect?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Complete the sentences with the correct correlative conjunction pair from the box.

both...andeither...orneither...nornot only...but also

Well done! You're using correlative conjunctions like a fluent speaker.

B Build sentences

Combine the two cues into one sentence using the conjunction pair shown.

  1. honest kind (both...and)

    ✓ She is both honest and kind.

  2. fair patient (both...and)
    Model answer

    The teacher is both fair and patient with her students.

  3. apologise explain your mistake (either...or)
    Model answer

    You can either apologise or explain your mistake.

  4. rude disrespectful (neither...nor)
    Model answer

    A good friend is neither rude nor disrespectful.

  5. polite genuinely caring (not only...but also)
    Model answer

    He is not only polite but also genuinely caring.

  6. listen carefully respond thoughtfully (both...and)
    Model answer

    Good listeners both listen carefully and respond thoughtfully.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original sentences about values you consider important, using both...and, either...or, neither...nor, or not only...but also.

A good friend is both...In my opinion, we should either... or...I believe people should not only... but also...
Rule

Correlative conjunctions are pairs of words (both...and, either...or, neither...nor, not only...but also) that connect two equal grammatical elements — words, phrases, or clauses — within a sentence. The two connected elements should have the same grammatical form (parallel structure).

TRCorrelative conjunctions (both...and, either...or, neither...nor, not only...but also), bir cümle içinde iki eş değerli unsuru (kelime, öbek ya da cümlecik) birbirine bağlayan çift bağlaçlardır. Bağlanan iki unsur aynı dilbilgisel yapıda olmalıdır (paralel yapı).

When?

We use both...and to add two positive ideas equally; either...or to show a choice between two options; neither...nor to negate two ideas together; and not only...but also to emphasise an additional, often stronger, point.

TRİki olumlu fikri eşit şekilde eklemek için both...and; iki seçenek arasında tercih belirtmek için either...or; iki fikri birlikte olumsuz yapmak için neither...nor; ek ve genellikle daha güçlü bir noktayı vurgulamak için not only...but also kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Both + A + and + B
  • Not only + A + but also + B

Negative

  • Neither + A + nor + B

Question

  • Is/Are + subject + both + A + and + B?
  • Does/Do + subject + either + A + or + B?

Short answers

  • Neither do I.
  • So does she. (in response to a both...and statement)
Mistakes
  • She is both honest and she is kind.She is both honest and kind.

    The two parts joined by both...and must have the same grammatical form — here, two adjectives, not an adjective and a clause.

    TRBoth...and ile bağlanan iki unsur aynı dilbilgisel yapıda olmalıdır; burada bir sıfat yerine cümlecik kullanılmamalıdır.

  • He neither likes tea or coffee.He likes neither tea nor coffee.

    Neither must be paired with nor, not or.

    TRNeither, 'or' ile değil 'nor' ile eşleştirilmelidir.

  • Not only she is kind but also generous.She is not only kind but also generous.

    When 'not only' begins a clause with a full subject and verb, subject-verb inversion is needed; it is simpler to place 'not only' after the subject instead.

    TR'Not only', özne ve yüklemle başlayan bir cümlenin başında kullanıldığında devrik yapı gerekir; genelde özneden sonra kullanmak daha basittir.

  • Either you apologise either you leave.Either you apologise or you leave.

    Either must be paired with or, not repeated.

    TREither tekrar edilmez; 'or' ile eşleştirilmelidir.

Compare
Choosing the right correlative conjunction pair
Positive ideaNegative / choice idea
Adding two positive/equal ideasboth...andShe is both fair and kind.neither...norShe is neither unfair nor unkind.
Presenting a choice or emphasiseither...orYou can either speak up or stay silent.not only...but alsoYou should not only speak up but also listen.

Good Manners Around the World

Every culture in the world has its own set of unwritten rules about polite behaviour. These rules, often called social norms or etiquette, guide how people greet each other, eat together, and show respect. Although the details are different, most cultures share the same basic values: honesty, kindness, and respect for others.

In many Western countries, a firm handshake and direct eye contact are seen as signs of confidence and respect. In parts of East Asia, a slight bow is more common, and it is considered impolite to shake hands too firmly. In some Middle Eastern and South Asian cultures, taking off your shoes before entering someone's home is both a custom and a sign of courtesy toward the host.

Table manners also vary widely. In some countries, finishing every bite on your plate shows gratitude to the cook. In others, leaving a little food behind is a polite way of showing that you have had enough. Neither habit is right or wrong on its own — both are simply different expressions of the same value: respect.

Learning about another culture's customs is not just useful for travellers. It also teaches tolerance and open-mindedness. When we understand why people behave the way they do, we are less likely to judge them unfairly. A truly polite person is not only someone who follows their own culture's rules but also someone who respects other people's norms, even when those norms are unfamiliar.

So the next time you meet someone from a different background, try to notice — and appreciate — both the differences and the shared values underneath them.

What Makes a Good Person?

If you ask ten different people what makes a good person, you will probably get ten different answers. Still, certain values appear again and again in these answers: honesty, kindness, respect, and responsibility. Many people believe that a good person is not only kind to their friends but also fair to strangers.

Honesty is often mentioned first. Telling the truth, even when it is uncomfortable, builds trust between people. A student who admits a mistake honestly, instead of blaming someone else, earns more respect in the long run than one who hides the truth. Integrity — doing the right thing even when no one is watching — is closely connected to honesty.

Kindness and empathy are also seen as essential. A kind person notices when someone is struggling and offers help without expecting anything in return. Empathy allows us to imagine how another person feels, which makes it easier to be patient and understanding, especially with people whose experiences are very different from our own.

Responsibility matters too. Good citizens take responsibility for their actions, both at home and in their community. They understand that their choices — whether it's keeping a promise or recycling their waste — affect other people. Being responsible is not always exciting, but it is one of the clearest signs of maturity.

In the end, there is probably no single answer to what makes a good person. It depends on either the culture you grew up in or the personal experiences that shaped you. What most people can agree on, however, is that values like honesty, kindness, and responsibility make communities stronger — wherever you happen to live.

BThat's a tough one. I'd say honesty, actually. Without honesty, you can't really trust anyone, even a kind person.

AI see your point, but I think kindness matters more. You can be both honest and quite harsh at the same time, and that can really hurt people.

BTrue, but a kind lie is still a lie. If a friend asks for your honest opinion about their project and you just say something nice to avoid hurting them, are you really helping them?

Which Value Matters Most?

In pairs, look at a list of values (honesty, kindness, respect, responsibility, tolerance) and discuss which one matters most in friendship, and which matters most in society, giving reasons.

Useful language

In my opinion, ... matters most because...I both agree and disagree with that...Either ... or ... is more important, in my view...That's an interesting point, but have you considered...?

Adımlar

  1. Look at the list of five values together.
  2. Individually, rank the values from most to least important for a good friend.
  3. Compare your rankings with your partner and explain your reasons.
  4. Discuss whether your ranking would change for 'a good citizen' instead of 'a good friend'.
  5. Agree on one shared opinion to share with the class.
DestekGive students a simplified list of just three values and sentence starters on the board.
HedefStudents give at least two supported reasons for their ranking.
İleriStudents challenge their partner's ranking with a counter-example.
SpeakingGroups of 3-410 min

Comparing Customs

In groups, each student describes one custom or tradition from their own family or community that reflects an important value. The group then compares the customs and discusses the shared values behind them.

Useful language

In my family, we both... and...This custom shows respect because...Neither of our customs is more correct than the other...That reminds me of...

Adımlar

  1. Individually, think of one custom from your own family or community.
  2. Take turns describing your custom and the value behind it to the group.
  3. Ask each speaker at least one follow-up question.
  4. As a group, identify two values that appear in more than one custom.
  5. Prepare a short summary to share with the class.
DestekProvide example customs (e.g. a birthday tradition, a holiday meal) students can choose from if they prefer not to share personal details.
HedefStudents describe their custom clearly and identify the value behind it.
İleriGroups compare how similar values are expressed differently across their customs.
Writing120-150 words

My Most Important Value

Write a paragraph giving your opinion on the value you think is most important for a good friend, using correlative conjunctions to support your opinion.

Plan

  1. Choose one value (e.g. honesty, kindness, respect, loyalty).
  2. State your opinion clearly in the first sentence.
  3. Give two reasons, using both...and or not only...but also.
  4. End with one sentence about why this value matters to you personally.

Writing150-180 words

Comparing Two Customs

Write a short paragraph comparing two customs or norms (from your own experience, family, or general knowledge) and explain what value each one shows.

Plan

  1. Choose two customs to compare.
  2. Briefly describe each custom in one or two sentences.
  3. Explain the value behind each custom.
  4. Use either...or or neither...nor to compare or contrast them.
  5. End with a sentence about what the two customs have in common.
