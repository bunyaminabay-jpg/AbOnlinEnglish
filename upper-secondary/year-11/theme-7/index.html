The Passive Voice (Present Simple, Past Simple, Present/Past Continuous, Future, and Modals)

1 · Notice
  • The old fortress is visited by thousands of tourists every year.
  • The underground city was discovered in the 1960s.
  • The monument is being restored at the moment.
  • New information boards will be added next year.
  • This site must be protected for future generations.

What do you notice about the position of the subject and the verb 'be' + past participle in each sentence?

Rule

The passive voice is formed with the correct tense of the verb 'be' + the past participle of the main verb. We use it when the action itself, or the thing the action happens to, is more important than who performs it. The form changes according to tense: present simple (is/are + p.p.), past simple (was/were + p.p.), present continuous (is/are being + p.p.), past continuous (was/were being + p.p.), future simple (will be + p.p.), and with modals (modal + be + p.p.).

TREdilgen çatı (passive voice), doğru zamandaki 'be' fiili + ana fiilin geçmiş zaman ortacı (past participle) ile oluşturulur. Eylemi kimin yaptığından çok, eylemin kendisi veya eylemin yapıldığı şey önemliyse kullanılır. Zamana göre biçimi değişir: geniş zaman (is/are + p.p.), geçmiş zaman (was/were + p.p.), şimdiki zamanın sürekli hali (is/are being + p.p.), geçmiş zamanın sürekli hali (was/were being + p.p.), gelecek zaman (will be + p.p.) ve kiplerle birlikte (modal + be + p.p.).

When?

We use the passive voice when describing historic facts (who built something is unknown or unimportant), when talking about processes happening to a place now (restoration, repair), when describing future plans for a site, and when giving rules or recommendations about protecting a place.

TREdilgen çatıyı; tarihi bir gerçeği anlatırken (bir şeyi kimin yaptığı bilinmiyorsa veya önemli değilse), bir yere şu anda uygulanan bir süreçten (restorasyon, onarım) bahsederken, bir yer için gelecek planlarından söz ederken ve bir yeri korumaya dair kural ya da tavsiye verirken kullanırız.

3 · Form

Positive

  • The castle is built on a hill. (present simple)
  • The temple was built in ancient times. (past simple)
  • The road is being repaired this week. (present continuous)
  • The site was being excavated when the war began. (past continuous)
  • A new sign will be placed at the entrance. (future simple)
  • This monument must be respected by all visitors. (modal)

Negative

  • The museum is not opened on Mondays.
  • The gate was not damaged during the storm.
  • The hall is not being used at the moment.
  • The statue will not be moved to another city.
  • Photographs must not be taken inside the tomb.

Question

  • Is the palace visited by many tourists?
  • Was the bridge built by local engineers?
  • Is the site currently being restored?
  • Will the exhibition be opened next month?
  • Should this area be protected?

Short answers

  • Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.
  • Yes, it was. / No, it wasn't.
  • Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.
  • Yes, it will. / No, it won't.
  • Yes, it should. / No, it shouldn't.
Mistakes
  • The castle built in the 14th century.The castle was built in the 14th century.

    The auxiliary verb 'be' must not be left out of the passive form.

    TREdilgen yapıda 'be' yardımcı fiili asla atlanmamalıdır.

  • The museum is visit by many people.The museum is visited by many people.

    The main verb must be in the past participle form, not the base form.

    TRAna fiil, yalın hâlde değil, geçmiş zaman ortacı (past participle) hâlinde olmalıdır.

  • The ruins were discover last year.The ruins were discovered last year.

    The past participle of regular verbs needs the -ed ending.

    TRDüzenli fiillerin past participle hâli -ed ekini almalıdır.

  • This area must protected.This area must be protected.

    A modal verb needs 'be' before the past participle in the passive.

    TRModal fiilden sonra edilgen yapıda mutlaka 'be' gelmelidir.

An Underground City

In the region of Cappadocia, in central Türkiye, several underground cities can be found carved into the soft volcanic rock. One of the largest of these was discovered almost by accident in 1963, when a local resident knocked down a wall in his house and found a hidden tunnel behind it. Since then, many chambers, corridors and rooms have been uncovered by archaeologists.

It is believed that the underground city was first used thousands of years ago as a place of shelter. Over time, rooms were carved deeper and deeper into the rock, and the settlement was gradually expanded until it could hold thousands of people along with their animals and food supplies. Stables, kitchens, storage rooms and even small churches were built several levels below the ground.

Large round stones were used as doors, and these could be rolled across the entrances from the inside to block invaders. Fresh air was supplied through a clever system of ventilation shafts that also reached the surface, and wells were dug to provide clean water even during long periods underground.

Today, only a small part of the underground city is open to visitors, since new tunnels and rooms are still being explored. It has become one of the most popular attractions in the region, and it is visited by travellers from all over the world who want to see how people once lived beneath the earth.

A City Built on Water

Istanbul is one of the few cities in the world that is built on two continents, with the Bosphorus strait dividing its European and Asian sides. For thousands of years, the city has been ruled by different empires, and each civilisation has left behind its own buildings, walls and monuments.

The old city walls, parts of which can still be seen today, were originally constructed to protect the city from land and sea attacks. Massive gates were built into the walls, and guards were once stationed at each one to check everyone entering the city. Although much of the wall has been damaged over the centuries, sections of it have been carefully restored so that visitors can imagine how strong the defences once were.

One of the city's most famous buildings began its life as a church, was later converted into a mosque, and for many years was used as a museum before being reopened as a mosque again. Its enormous dome, which amazed visitors when it was completed, is still considered an outstanding achievement of engineering.

Today, bridges and tunnels connect the two sides of the city, and thousands of commuters cross the strait every single day. Ferries are still used by many people, partly because they are convenient and partly because the crossing offers wonderful views of the city's skyline. New museums and cultural centres are being opened all the time, which shows that this ancient city continues to change while its history is carefully preserved.

2 While listening

Transkripti göster

GuideWelcome, everyone! This fortress was originally built in the fifteenth century to protect the harbour below.

VisitorThat's amazing. Was it ever attacked?

GuideYes, it was attacked several times, but it was never fully captured because of its strong walls.

VisitorHow many towers does it have?

GuideThere are twelve towers in total, and three of them have recently been restored.

VisitorCan we go up to the top tower?

GuideUnfortunately not today — the top tower is currently being repaired, so it's closed to visitors.

VisitorThat's a shame. Is anything else being done to improve the site?

GuideYes, a new visitor centre will be opened here next year, with more information about the fortress's history.

VisitorSounds great. One more question — is photography allowed inside?

The Story of a Natural Wonder

1 Before listening

  • Can you think of a place where unusual rock or land shapes were formed naturally?
  • How do you think such shapes might be created over time?
Ses kaydı yakında

2 While listening

Transkripti göster

NarratorThis valley is famous for its unusual, cone-shaped rock formations, which are often called 'fairy chimneys' by visitors.

NarratorThe formations were created over millions of years by volcanic eruptions, which covered the whole area in soft ash and rock.

NarratorLater, this soft rock was shaped by wind and rain, and a harder layer of stone on top protected the columns underneath from being worn away completely.

NarratorAs a result, tall, cone-shaped towers were left standing, some of them more than thirty metres high.

NarratorToday, the valley is visited by thousands of tourists every year, and hot air balloon rides are offered so that visitors can see the strange landscape from above.

NarratorNew paths and viewing points have been added recently, but the natural formations themselves are strictly protected and must not be damaged.

Describe a Landmark

Choose a real or imaginary Turkish landmark. Describe it to your partner using at least four passive voice sentences (when it was built, what it is used for, how it is protected, and any future plans).

Useful language

It was built in...It is visited by...It is currently being...It will be...It must be protected because...

Adımlar

  1. Choose a landmark and note down four to five key facts.
  2. Turn each fact into a passive voice sentence.
  3. Take turns describing your landmark to your partner.
  4. Your partner listens and asks one follow-up question for more detail.

Ask for More Detailed Information

One student plays a tour guide describing a historic site; the others ask follow-up questions to get more detailed information, such as dates, numbers, or future plans.

Useful language

Could you tell me more about...?What exactly is being done to...?When was it...?How many...are there?Is anything planned for...?

Adımlar

  1. The 'guide' prepares three or four basic facts about a site.
  2. The guide gives a short introduction to the group.
  3. The other students ask at least three follow-up questions.
  4. The guide answers using the passive voice where possible.
  5. Switch roles so everyone has a turn as the guide.
DestekProvide a list of question starters for students who need help forming follow-up questions.
HedefGroups complete the full role-play with each member taking a turn as guide.

Describe a Historic Site
