Be going to (plans and predictions)

1 · Notice
  • I am going to study engineering after school.
  • Look at those clouds — it is going to rain soon.
  • They are not going to open a new branch this year.
  • Are you going to apply for that internship?

What do you notice about the structure before the main verb, and when is it used — for a plan or for something we can already see evidence of?

studyworknot staytravelbecome

Well done — you can already spot plans and evidence-based predictions!

B Build sentences

Build full sentences from the cues using be going to.

    ✓ I am going to become a vet.

  2. she not work in an office

  4. the company hire more staff
    Model answer

  5. he not become a lawyer
    Model answer

    He is not going to become a lawyer.

  6. they open a new office

    They are going to open a new office.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original sentences about your own future job plans using be going to.

After I finish school, I am going to...I am not going to...
Rule

We use be going to for plans and intentions decided before the moment of speaking, and for predictions based on present evidence.

TRKonuşma anından önce karar verilmiş planlar ve niyetler için, ayrıca şu anki kanıtlara dayanan tahminler için be going to kullanırız.

When?

Use it for a personal plan ('I am going to become a doctor') or a prediction you can already see signs of ('Look at those clouds, it is going to rain').

TRKişisel bir plan için ('Doktor olacağım') veya şu anda belirtisini gördüğümüz bir tahmin için kullanılır ('Şu bulutlara bak, yağmur yağacak').

3 · Form

Positive

  • Subject + am/is/are + going to + base verb
  • She is going to work abroad.

Negative

  • Subject + am/is/are + not + going to + base verb
  • He is not going to change jobs.

Question

  • Am/Is/Are + subject + going to + base verb?
  • Are they going to hire new staff?

Short answers

  • Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
  • Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.
Mistakes
  • I go to become a doctor.I am going to become a doctor.

    The auxiliary be must appear before going to.

    TRBe going to yapısından önce yardımcı fiil (am/is/are) mutlaka bulunmalıdır.

  • She is going become a nurse.She is going to become a nurse.

    The word to must not be dropped from going to.

    TRGoing to yapısındaki 'to' düşürülmemelidir.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original sentences: one offer, one promise, and one prediction about future jobs, all using will/won't.

I'll...I promise I will...I think... will...
Rule

We use will for instant decisions made at the moment of speaking, offers, promises, and general predictions or opinions about the future.

TRKonuşma anında verilen ani kararlar, teklifler, sözler ve gelecekle ilgili genel tahmin/görüşler için will kullanırız.

When?

Use will when you decide something right now ('The bag is heavy, I'll carry it'), promise something, or give an opinion about the future without present evidence.

TRBir şeye o an karar verdiğinizde ('Çanta ağır, ben taşırım'), söz verdiğinizde veya şu anki bir kanıt olmadan gelecek hakkında görüş bildirdiğinizde will kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Subject + will + base verb
  • I will call the office tomorrow.

Negative

  • Subject + won't + base verb
  • She won't miss the deadline.

Question

  • Will + subject + base verb?
  • Will you be free on Monday?

Short answers

  • Yes, I will. / No, I won't.
Mistakes
  • I will to call you later.I will call you later.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original sentences about arrangements already fixed in your diary using present continuous.

On Saturday I am...Next week I'm...
Rule

We use the present continuous to talk about future arrangements that are already fixed, usually with a specific time or place and often involving another person.

TRGenellikle belirli bir saat veya yerle ve çoğunlukla başka bir kişiyle önceden planlanmış gelecekteki düzenlemeler için şimdiki zamanın hikayesini değil, present continuous'u kullanırız.

When?

Use it for a diary-type plan that is already organised, such as a meeting, appointment, or trip with a set time.

TRZaten organize edilmiş, belirli bir saati olan bir toplantı, randevu veya seyahat gibi ajanda tipi bir plan için kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing
  • I am starting my new job on Monday.

Negative

  • Subject + am/is/are + not + verb-ing
  • We aren't meeting until next week.

Question

  • Am/Is/Are + subject + verb-ing?
  • Are you seeing the manager today?

Short answers

  • Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
Mistakes
  • I meeting the manager tomorrow.I am meeting the manager tomorrow.

    The auxiliary be must not be dropped before verb-ing.

    TR-ing fiilinden önceki yardımcı fiil (am/is/are) atlanmamalıdır.

  • She is meeting the manager usually on Fridays.She usually meets the manager on Fridays.

    Present continuous is for a specific fixed future plan, not a repeated habit.

    TRPresent continuous, tekrarlanan bir alışkanlık için değil, belirli ve sabit bir gelecek planı için kullanılır.

Jobs That Didn't Exist Ten Years Ago

Ten years ago, nobody had heard of a 'drone pilot' or a 'social media manager.' Today these are real careers that thousands of people do every day. Technology is changing so fast that schools cannot always prepare students for jobs that don't exist yet.

Experts say that in the next ten years, we are going to see even more new careers. For example, some companies are already training 'AI trainers,' people who teach computer programs to understand human language better. Other new roles include 'sustainability consultants,' who help businesses reduce waste, and 'virtual event planners,' who organise online conferences and parties.

Not every new job needs a university degree. Many require practical skills, creativity, and the ability to learn quickly. A student who is going to succeed in this changing job market will probably need to update their skills again and again throughout their career.

So what does this mean for students today? It means staying curious, being open to unfamiliar careers, and not worrying too much if a job doesn't have a name yet. The most important skill of all might be the willingness to keep learning.

Preparing for a Job Interview

Getting ready for a job interview can feel stressful, but a little preparation makes a big difference. Career coaches say the biggest mistake candidates make is not researching the company beforehand. Before any interview, it's a good idea to learn about what the company does and why you want to work there.

On the day of the interview, arriving early is essential. Being even five minutes late can create a bad impression before you say a single word. Most experts suggest arriving at least ten minutes before the appointment, then using that time to relax and review your notes.

During the interview, employers often ask questions like 'Where do you see yourself in five years?' This question isn't about giving a perfect answer; it's about showing that you have thought about your future and that you are going to work hard to reach your goals.

Finally, at the end of the interview, it's polite to thank the interviewer for their time and ask when you will hear back about the decision. A short follow-up message the next day, thanking them again, can leave a positive final impression.

My Future Career Plan

Tell your partner about your future job plans, including what you are going to do, why, and what qualifications you will need.

Useful language

I'm going to...My plan is to...I think I will need...One day I hope to...

Adımlar

  2. Take turns describing your plan to your partner using be going to.
  3. Ask your partner at least two follow-up questions about their plan.
  4. Report one interesting thing about your partner's plan to the class.

Phone Role-Play: Making an Appointment

One student calls a company to make a job interview appointment; the other plays the receptionist, offering times and confirming details.

Useful language

I'd like to make an appointment...Are you available on...?Could we reschedule?I'll confirm that for you.

  2. Student A calls to book an appointment; Student B checks a made-up diary and offers two times.
  3. Agree on a time and Student B confirms the details out loud.
  4. Switch roles and repeat with a new scenario, such as rescheduling an appointment.
