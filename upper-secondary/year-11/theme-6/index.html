What Will I Learn?

Nasıl çalışılır?

  1. Bu temada neyi öğreneceğini oku.

In this theme, students learn how to express regret and criticism about past actions and how to show different degrees of certainty about past events, using modal perfect structures in real-life situations about relationships and personal decisions.

Konular

  • Expressing degrees of certainty in the past
  • Expressing criticism for the events in the past

Dil yapıları

  • Should have / could have (criticism)
  • Might have / must have / couldn't have (certainty in the past)

Anlayabileceğim

  • I can understand a story about a past mistake or missed chance
  • I can understand someone criticising a decision that was made in the past
  • I can understand a speaker guessing what probably happened in the past
  • I can understand the difference between a strong guess and a certain conclusion about the past
  • I can understand an apology that explains what should have been done differently
  • I can understand a dialogue where two people disagree about what really happened
  • I can understand a short text about learning from past regrets

Söyleyebileceğim

  • I can criticise a past action using should have / shouldn't have
  • I can express regret about something I did or didn't do
  • I can make a guess about a past event using might have / must have
  • I can say that something was impossible using couldn't have
  • I can compare my opinion with someone else's about a past decision
  • I can give advice about what someone could have done differently
  • I can talk about opening up emotionally to friends and family

Yazabileceğim

  • I can write a short reflection about a personal regret using should have / could have
  • I can write a paragraph explaining what probably happened in a situation, using modal perfects
  • I can write advice to a friend about a past mistake

Should have / Could have (criticism and regret about the past)

1 · Notice
  • You should have told me you were upset instead of staying silent.
  • I could have called her back, but I was too proud to admit I was wrong.
  • They shouldn't have ignored his messages for a whole week.
  • We could have talked about it calmly, but we both got angry instead.

What do you notice about the verb form after should/could + have? Do these sentences describe the past or the present?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Complete the sentences with should have, shouldn't have or could have and the verb in brackets.

should haveshouldn't havecould have

Nice work — you're getting the hang of criticising and expressing regret about the past!

B Build sentences

Build full sentences from the cues, using should have / shouldn't have / could have.

  1. you not / shout at your brother

    ✓ You shouldn't have shouted at your brother.

  2. I tell her the truth earlier
    Model answer

    I should have told her the truth earlier.

  3. they not / cancel the plan at the last minute
    Model answer

    They shouldn't have cancelled the plan at the last minute.

  4. he call and explain what happened
    Model answer

    He could have called and explained what happened.

  5. we not / assume the worst about him
    Model answer

    We shouldn't have assumed the worst about him.

  6. she ask before borrowing my jacket
    Model answer

    She should have asked before borrowing my jacket.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original sentences about a real or imaginary situation where you or someone else should have, shouldn't have, or could have done something differently.

Looking back, I should have...My friend shouldn't have...We could have...
Rule

We use should have + past participle to criticise a past action that did not happen but would have been the right thing to do. We use shouldn't have + past participle to criticise something that did happen but was the wrong thing to do. We use could have + past participle to talk about a possible action in the past that was not taken.

TRGeçmişte yapılmayan ama yapılması gereken bir davranışı eleştirmek için should have + fiil3 kullanılır. Geçmişte yapılan ama yapılmaması gereken bir davranışı eleştirmek için shouldn't have + fiil3 kullanılır. Geçmişte mümkün olduğu hâlde yapılmayan bir eylemden bahsetmek için ise could have + fiil3 kullanılır.

When?

Use this structure when giving advice about the past, expressing regret, or criticising a decision that cannot be changed anymore.

TRGeçmişle ilgili tavsiye verirken, pişmanlık ifade ederken veya artık değiştirilemeyecek bir kararı eleştirirken bu yapı kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Subject + should/could + have + past participle
  • You should have listened to her.

Negative

  • Subject + shouldn't/couldn't + have + past participle
  • You shouldn't have said that.

Question

  • Should/Could + subject + have + past participle?
  • Should I have called him first?

Short answers

  • Yes, you should have. / No, you shouldn't have.
Mistakes
  • You should have went to the doctor.You should have gone to the doctor.

    After have, we use the past participle (gone), not the past simple (went).

    TRhave'den sonra past simple değil, past participle (gone) kullanılır.

  • I should to have called her.I should have called her.

    There is no 'to' after should; should is always followed directly by have + past participle.

    TRshould'dan sonra 'to' kullanılmaz; doğrudan have + fiil3 gelir.

Compare
should have vs. could have
should havecould have
MeaningCriticises a past action as wrong or missingYou should have told me earlier.Points out a possible but unused optionYou could have told me earlier, but it wasn't necessary.

Might have / Must have / Couldn't have (degrees of certainty about the past)

1 · Notice
  • She might have forgotten about the meeting — I'm not sure.
  • He must have been really upset if he left without saying goodbye.
  • They couldn't have seen the message; their phone was off all day.
  • You must have misunderstood what she meant.

Which sentence expresses a strong belief, which expresses an impossible conclusion, and which expresses a weak guess?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Complete with might have, must have or couldn't have and the verb in brackets.

might havemust havecouldn't have

Great — you're reading the clues like a detective now!

B Build sentences

Build full sentences from the cues to show certainty about the past.

  1. he must / forget his keys

    ✓ He must have forgotten his keys.

  2. she might / hear the news already
    Model answer

    She might have heard the news already.

  3. they couldn't / know about the surprise
    Model answer

    They couldn't have known about the surprise.

  4. it must / be a difficult decision for him
    Model answer

    It must have been a difficult decision for him.

  5. he might / lose his phone somewhere
    Model answer

    He might have lost his phone somewhere.

  6. she couldn't / say that; it's not like her
    Model answer

    She couldn't have said that; it's not like her.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original sentences guessing what might have, must have, or couldn't have happened in a mysterious or emotional situation.

He must have...She might have...They couldn't have...
Rule

We use must have + past participle when we are almost certain about a past event based on evidence. We use might have + past participle when we are not sure and are only guessing at a possibility. We use couldn't have + past participle when we are certain something was impossible in the past.

TRGeçmişte olan bir olay hakkında kanıtlara dayanarak neredeyse emin olduğumuzda must have + fiil3 kullanılır. Emin olmadığımız, sadece bir olasılığı tahmin ettiğimiz durumlarda might have + fiil3 kullanılır. Geçmişte bir şeyin imkânsız olduğundan emin olduğumuzda ise couldn't have + fiil3 kullanılır.

When?

Use this structure when speculating about a past event, explaining evidence, or ruling out an impossible explanation.

TRGeçmişteki bir olay hakkında tahminde bulunurken, kanıtları açıklarken veya imkânsız bir açıklamayı elerken bu yapı kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Subject + must/might + have + past participle
  • He must have left early.

Negative

  • Subject + might not/couldn't + have + past participle
  • She couldn't have known that.

Question

  • Rarely used in questions; guesses are usually expressed as statements: 'Do you think she might have forgotten?'

Short answers

  • She might have. / No, she couldn't have.
Mistakes
  • He must have went home.He must have gone home.

    After have, we always use the past participle (gone), never the past simple (went).

    TRhave'den sonra her zaman past participle (gone) kullanılır, past simple (went) değil.

  • She can't have knew about it.She couldn't have known about it.

    The negative certainty form is couldn't have, not can't have known + past simple.

    TRGeçmişteki imkânsızlık için couldn't have + fiil3 kullanılır, can't have knew gibi karışık bir yapı değil.

Compare
must have vs. might have vs. couldn't have
must have (almost certain)might have (uncertain guess)
Certainty levelStrong belief based on clear evidenceThe ground is wet — it must have rained.A weak guess, one of several possibilitiesIt might have rained, but I'm not sure.

The Letter I Never Sent

For almost a year, Deniz and her best friend Ela hadn't spoken. It all started with a small misunderstanding that neither of them had bothered to clear up. Deniz often thought about what she should have done differently. She should have called Ela the same evening instead of waiting for an apology that never came. Looking back, she realised she could have simply asked what had happened instead of assuming the worst.

One evening, Deniz decided to write a letter. She wrote about how she had bottled up her feelings for months, afraid that opening up would make her seem weak. She admitted that she shouldn't have ignored Ela's messages at the beginning, even though she was hurt. She also wrote that Ela must have felt confused when the replies suddenly stopped; there was no other explanation that made sense.

As she wrote, Deniz thought about all the small clues she had missed. Ela had seemed distant at school, and her friends said she looked tired and quiet. Deniz guessed that Ela might have been dealing with something difficult at home, something she couldn't have known about at the time. She felt a wave of guilt. She should have asked instead of staying silent.

In the end, Deniz never sent the letter by post. Instead, she read it aloud to Ela in person, her voice shaking. Ela listened quietly and then said she, too, wished she could have handled things differently. Neither of them had been a bad friend; they had simply been too proud to be vulnerable. By the end of their conversation, the heavy burden both girls had carried for months finally felt lighter.

Who Forgot the Cake?

When the Aydın family arrived at their grandmother's 70th birthday party, everyone realised the cake was missing. Panic spread quickly through the kitchen. Someone must have forgotten to pick it up from the bakery, but nobody wanted to admit it. Uncle Kemal insisted he couldn't have forgotten, since he had written the reminder himself on a sticky note stuck to the fridge.

Cousin Aslı suggested that their younger cousin Mert might have taken the sticky note down while cleaning the kitchen that morning. Mert denied it firmly, saying he hadn't touched anything near the fridge. Still, his sister remembered seeing him near the fridge earlier, so he might have moved the note without even noticing.

Meanwhile, Grandma sat calmly in her armchair, smiling. She said it didn't really matter who should have picked up the cake. What mattered more, she explained, was that everyone had come together, worried and a little chaotic, just to make her day special. She reminded them that a missing cake couldn't have ruined a celebration built on love and family.

In the end, they discovered that the bakery itself must have made the mistake — they had accidentally given the cake to another family with a similar name. Everyone laughed with relief. Uncle Kemal joked that he should have called to confirm the order the night before, just to be safe next time. They ordered pizza instead and turned the missing cake into the best story of the year.

KaanI noticed you left, but I didn't understand why. I thought maybe you just weren't feeling well.

BuseNo, it wasn't that. You made a joke about my presentation in front of everyone, and it really hurt. You shouldn't have said that in front of the whole class.

SpeakingGroups of 3-410 min

Detectives of the Past

In groups, look at a short mystery situation (e.g. a broken window, a missing notebook, a surprise party ruined) and discuss what might have, must have, or couldn't have happened, giving reasons for your guesses.

Useful language

It must have been...It might have been...It couldn't have been... because...That would explain why...I'm not so sure about that because...

Adımlar

  1. In groups, choose one mystery situation from the teacher's list.
  2. Brainstorm at least three possible explanations using might have.
  3. Agree on the explanation you think is most likely (must have) and one you can rule out (couldn't have).
  4. Present your group's reasoning to the class in two minutes.
DestekGive weaker students a simple sentence frame: 'I think ___ must have happened because ___.'
HedefStudents produce at least three modal perfect sentences with clear reasoning.
İleriAsk stronger groups to challenge another group's explanation with a counter-argument using couldn't have.
SpeakingPairs8 min

I Wish I Had Said Something

In pairs, take turns describing a real or invented situation where you feel you should have or could have acted differently, while your partner gives gentle advice or agrees with your reflection.

Useful language

I should have...I could have...Looking back, I shouldn't have...In your place, I might have...It's understandable that you...

Adımlar

  1. Think of a small past situation (a missed chance, a small conflict, an unfinished task).
  2. Share it with your partner using should have / could have to express what you would do differently now.
  3. Your partner listens and responds with a supportive comment or their own similar experience.
  4. Swap roles and repeat.
DestekProvide a bank of situations (forgetting a friend's message, not helping with something, avoiding a conversation) for students who need ideas.
HedefBoth partners use at least two modal perfect structures accurately.
İleriAsk stronger students to give their partner specific advice for the future using different structures, e.g. 'Next time, you could try...'
Writing120-150 words

A Letter I Never Sent

Write a short letter to someone (a friend, family member, or classmate) explaining a past situation you regret and what you should have or could have done differently.

Plan

  1. Decide who the letter is to and what the situation was.
  2. List 2-3 things you regret or should have done.
  3. Think about how the situation made you feel and what you learned.
  4. Plan a closing line that shows how you feel now.

The Mystery of the Missing Gift

Write a short paragraph speculating about what happened to a missing item (e.g. a birthday gift, a phone, a set of keys), using might have, must have, and couldn't have to explain different theories.

Plan

  1. Decide what item is missing and where it was last seen.
  2. Brainstorm at least three possible explanations.
  3. Order your ideas from least to most likely.
  4. End with the explanation you believe is most probable.
