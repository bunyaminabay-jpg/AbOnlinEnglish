This theme looks at hard times in people's lives — things that happened in the past, things that were happening at the same time, and habits people used to have — through stories of ordinary people facing and overcoming difficulties.

Konular

  • Describing events happening at the same time in the past
  • Explaining people's habits in the past

Dil yapıları

  • Past Simple
  • Past Continuous
  • Past Continuous with while
  • Used to

Anlayabileceğim

  • I can understand short biographical texts about people facing hard times.
  • I can understand a story about two things happening at the same time in the past.
  • I can understand people describing habits or routines they used to have.
  • I can understand a dialogue about a difficult period in someone's life.
  • I can understand the difference between a finished past action and an action in progress in the past.
  • I can understand key vocabulary related to hardship and overcoming challenges.
  • I can understand a listening text about someone describing a childhood memory.

Söyleyebileceğim

  • I can talk about difficult events from my own past.
  • I can describe what I was doing when something else happened.
  • I can talk about habits I used to have but don't have anymore.
  • I can ask and answer questions about past experiences.
  • I can describe how someone overcame a hardship.
  • I can compare my past habits with my present ones.
  • I can give advice to someone going through a hard time.
  • I can retell a short story about hard times in the past tense.

Yazabileceğim

  • I can write a short biographical paragraph about a person overcoming a challenge.
  • I can write sentences using the past simple and the past continuous correctly.
  • I can write about habits I used to have using 'used to'.
  • I can write a diary entry about a difficult day in the past.
  • I can write a short story combining past simple and past continuous.

Rule

We use the past simple for finished actions or situations at a definite time in the past. Regular verbs add -ed; many common verbs are irregular.

TRGeçmişte belirli bir zamanda tamamlanmış eylemler veya durumlar için past simple kullanılır. Düzenli fiillere -ed eklenir; birçok yaygın fiil ise düzensizdir.

When?

Use it for a single finished action, a series of finished actions, or a past state that is now over.

TRTek bir tamamlanmış eylem, art arda gelen tamamlanmış eylemler veya artık bitmiş bir geçmiş durum için kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Subject + V2 (regular: verb + -ed)
  • She worked hard.
  • They moved to a new city.

Negative

  • Subject + didn't + base verb
  • She didn't give up.
  • They didn't have much money.

Question

  • Did + subject + base verb...?
  • Did she work hard?
  • Did they move to a new city?

Short answers

  • Yes, she did. / No, she didn't.
  • Yes, they did. / No, they didn't.
Mistakes
  • She not worked hard.She didn't work hard.

    Negative past simple needs 'didn't' + base verb, not 'not' + past form.

    TROlumsuz past simple'da 'didn't' + fiilin yalın hali kullanılır, 'not' + geçmiş zaman biçimi kullanılmaz.

  • Did she moved to the city?Did she move to the city?

    After 'Did', use the base form of the verb, not the past form.

    TR'Did' sorusundan sonra fiilin yalın hali kullanılır, geçmiş zaman biçimi kullanılmaz.

  • He goed to school every day.He went to school every day.

    'Go' is an irregular verb: go → went, not 'goed'.

    TR'Go' düzensiz bir fiildir: go → went, 'goed' değil.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original sentences using the past continuous (with 'while' at least once) to describe two things happening at the same time during a hard period.

While I was ..., my family was ...At that time, she was ...
Rule

We use the past continuous (was/were + verb-ing) for an action in progress at a specific moment in the past, or for two actions happening at the same time, often linked with 'while'.

TRPast continuous (was/were + fiil-ing), geçmişte belirli bir anda devam eden bir eylem için veya genellikle 'while' ile bağlanan, aynı anda gerçekleşen iki eylem için kullanılır.

When?

Use it for background actions in progress, actions interrupted by another action (with past simple), or two parallel actions with 'while'.

TRDevam etmekte olan arka plan eylemleri, başka bir eylem tarafından kesilen eylemler (past simple ile birlikte) veya 'while' ile ifade edilen iki paralel eylem için kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Subject + was/were + verb-ing
  • She was studying at night.
  • They were working long hours.

Negative

  • Subject + wasn't/weren't + verb-ing
  • He wasn't earning much.
  • They weren't sleeping well.

Question

  • Was/Were + subject + verb-ing...?
  • Was she studying?
  • Were they working long hours?

Short answers

  • Yes, she was. / No, she wasn't.
  • Yes, they were. / No, they weren't.
Mistakes
  • She was study when the phone rang.She was studying when the phone rang.

    The main verb must take the -ing ending in the past continuous.

    TRPast continuous'ta ana fiil -ing eki almalıdır.

  • While she was studying, her mother cooked.While she was studying, her mother was cooking.

    When two actions happen at the same time, both are usually in the past continuous.

    TRİki eylem aynı anda gerçekleştiğinde, genellikle her ikisi de past continuous'ta olur.

  • They was working hard.They were working hard.

    Use 'were' with 'they', not 'was'.

    TR'They' ile 'was' değil 'were' kullanılır.

Compare
Past Simple vs Past Continuous
Past SimplePast Continuous
Usea finished action / a series of eventsHe lost his job.an action in progress at a past momentHe was looking for a new job.
With 'while'the shorter, interrupting actionThe phone rang while she was studying.the longer background actionWhile she was studying, the phone rang.

Write 2-3 original sentences with 'used to' about habits or a way of life someone had in the past that is different now.

Rule

We use 'used to' + base verb to talk about repeated past habits or past states that are no longer true.

TR'Used to' + fiilin yalın hali, artık doğru olmayan tekrarlanan geçmiş alışkanlıkları veya geçmiş durumları anlatmak için kullanılır.

When?

Use it to contrast a past habit/state with the present situation. Do not use it for a single, one-time action.

TRGeçmişteki bir alışkanlığı/durumu şimdiki durumla karşılaştırmak için kullanılır. Tek seferlik bir eylem için kullanılmaz.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Subject + used to + base verb
  • He used to walk to school.
  • They used to live in a village.

Negative

  • Subject + didn't use to + base verb
  • She didn't use to eat meat.
  • We didn't use to have a car.

Question

  • Did + subject + use to + base verb...?
  • Did he use to walk to school?
  • Did they use to live in a village?

Short answers

  • Yes, he did. / No, he didn't.
  • Yes, they did. / No, they didn't.
Mistakes
  • He use to walk to school.He used to walk to school.

    In positive statements, the form is always 'used to', with -d.

    TROlumlu cümlelerde form her zaman -d ile 'used to' şeklindedir.

  • She didn't used to eat meat.She didn't use to eat meat.

    After 'didn't', use 'use to' without -d, because 'didn't' already shows the past.

    TR'Didn't' zaten geçmiş zamanı gösterdiği için sonrasında -d'siz 'use to' kullanılır.

  • I used to go to the cinema yesterday.I went to the cinema yesterday.

    'Used to' is for repeated past habits, not a single past action with a specific time word.

    TR'Used to' tekrarlanan geçmiş alışkanlıklar içindir, belirli bir zaman ifadesiyle tek seferlik bir eylem için kullanılmaz.

The Girl Who Didn't Stop

Elif grew up in a small town where her family didn't have much money. Her father worked long hours at a local workshop, and her mother sold vegetables at the market. When Elif was ten years old, her father lost his job, and things became even harder. The family moved into a smaller house, and Elif started helping her mother at the market every morning before school.

While her classmates were playing after school, Elif was usually working or studying by candlelight, because the electricity at home was often cut off. She used to dream about becoming a teacher one day, but nobody in her family had ever finished university. Many people told her it wasn't realistic for a girl from her background.

Elif didn't listen to them. She studied every night, even when she was exhausted, and she never missed a day of school. One teacher noticed how determined she was and began giving her extra books to read. Slowly, Elif's grades improved, and after years of hard work, she won a scholarship to study education at a university in another city.

Today, Elif is a teacher at a school not far from the town where she grew up. She often tells her students that hard times don't have to decide who you become. 'I used to think poverty would stop me,' she says, 'but it actually taught me how to keep going.' Her story has since inspired several of her own students to keep studying, no matter how difficult their situation is.

From the Repair Shop to the Stage

Kerem's father used to run a small bicycle repair shop, and Kerem started helping him there when he was only eleven. While other children in the neighbourhood were going to after-school clubs, Kerem was fixing tyres and cleaning chains. The family needed the extra income, so there wasn't much time left for hobbies.

One rainy afternoon, while Kerem was repairing an old guitar that a customer had left behind, he started playing a few notes just to test the strings. He had never had lessons, but something about the sound stayed with him. From that day, whenever the shop was quiet, he practised on that same guitar, teaching himself simple songs from an old book his uncle had given him.

Life didn't get easier quickly. Kerem still worked at the shop every afternoon, and money was often tight at home. But he used to save a little money from small jobs to buy guitar strings, and he never stopped practising, even during the hardest months when the shop had almost no customers.

Years later, a local café owner heard Kerem playing outside the repair shop and asked him to perform one evening. That first small concert led to others, and slowly Kerem built a name for himself as a musician in his city. He still visits the repair shop sometimes, not to fix bicycles, but to remind himself where his journey began.

My Family's Story

Useful language

  2. Prepare 4-5 questions to ask your partner about their story, using past simple and past continuous.
  3. Take turns interviewing each other and taking short notes.
  4. Report your partner's story back to the class in 3-4 sentences.
