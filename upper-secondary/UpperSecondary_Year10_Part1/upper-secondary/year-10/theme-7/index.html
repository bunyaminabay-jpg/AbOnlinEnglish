This theme looks at the natural resources our planet gives us, the global problems that come from using them badly, and the everyday and large-scale actions that can protect them for the future.

Konular

  • Natural resources
  • Global problems related to natural resources
  • Protecting natural resources

Dil yapıları

  • Defining relative clauses (that, which, who)
  • Connecting words: so (cause and effect)

Anlayabileceğim

  • I can understand short texts about natural resources and how people use them.
  • I can understand a text describing a global environmental problem.
  • I can understand people talking about ways to protect nature.
  • I can follow a dialogue about recycling or saving energy at home.
  • I can understand a text that explains cause and effect with 'so'.
  • I can understand descriptions that use relative clauses to give extra information.
  • I can follow a short talk about renewable energy sources.
  • I can understand an opinion text about climate change.

Söyleyebileceğim

  • I can talk about natural resources like water, forests, and minerals.
  • I can describe global problems such as pollution and deforestation.
  • I can suggest ways to protect natural resources.
  • I can give reasons for environmental problems using 'so'.
  • I can describe people, places, and things using relative clauses.
  • I can discuss the causes and effects of climate change.
  • I can express my opinion about environmental protection.
  • I can compare habits that harm and habits that help the environment.

Yazabileceğim

  • I can write a short paragraph describing a natural resource and its uses.
  • I can write about a global environmental problem and its causes.
  • I can write a short proposal suggesting ways to protect nature.
  • I can write sentences using relative clauses and 'so' correctly.

Defining relative clauses (that, which, who)

1 · Notice
  • Solar power is an energy source that never runs out.
  • The scientist who studies climate change gave a talk at our school.
  • Forests, which produce oxygen, are being cut down quickly.
  • People who waste water often don't think about the future.

What do you notice about the words 'that', 'which', and 'who' in these sentences? What kind of information do they add?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Complete the sentences with 'that', 'which', or 'who'.

thatwhichwho

Well done! You can now identify the right relative pronoun.

B Build sentences

Use the cues to build full sentences with a defining relative clause.

  1. oil / non-renewable resource / comes from the ground

    ✓ Oil is a non-renewable resource that comes from the ground.

  2. farmers / people / grow our food
    Model answer

    Farmers are people who grow our food.

  3. plastic / material / takes hundreds of years to break down
    Model answer

    Plastic is a material that takes hundreds of years to break down.

  4. the Amazon / forest / produces a lot of the world's oxygen
    Model answer

    The Amazon is a forest which produces a lot of the world's oxygen.

  5. a biologist / scientist / studies living things
    Model answer

    A biologist is a scientist who studies living things.

  6. recycling bins / containers / help us sort our waste
    Model answer

    Recycling bins are containers that help us sort our waste.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original sentences of your own using defining relative clauses (that/which/who) about nature or the environment.

A natural resource that...People who...An animal which...
Rule

We use defining relative clauses to give essential information that identifies exactly who or what we are talking about. Use 'who' for people, 'which' for things, and 'that' for both people and things. No comma is used before the clause.

TRTanımlayıcı ilgi cümlecikleri, kimden veya neden bahsettiğimizi tam olarak belirten gerekli bilgiyi verir. İnsanlar için 'who', nesneler için 'which', hem insanlar hem nesneler için 'that' kullanılır. Cümlecikten önce virgül kullanılmaz.

When?

Use a defining relative clause when the information in the clause is necessary to understand which person or thing you mean.

TRCümlecikteki bilgi, hangi kişi veya nesneden bahsettiğimizi anlamak için gerekliyse tanımlayıcı ilgi cümleciği kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • The resource that/which we use most is water.
  • People who protect nature are important.

Negative

  • This isn't the kind of pollution that we can ignore.
  • There aren't many rivers which are still clean.

Question

  • Is this the forest that was damaged by the fire?
  • Who is the person who started this project?

Short answers

  • Not applicable in short-answer form; the clause is part of a longer sentence.
Mistakes
  • The man which works in recycling is my neighbour.The man who works in recycling is my neighbour.

    Use 'who' (not 'which') for people.

    TRİnsanlar için 'which' değil 'who' kullanılır.

  • Solar power, that is renewable, is getting cheaper.Solar power, which is renewable, is getting cheaper.

    'That' is not normally used after a comma (non-defining clauses use 'which').

    TR'That' normalde virgülden sonra kullanılmaz (virgüllü cümleciklerde 'which' kullanılır).

Connecting words: so (cause and effect)

1 · Notice
  • The lake dried up, so the farmers could not water their crops.
  • We recycle our waste, so less rubbish goes to landfill.
  • The city has strict pollution rules, so the air is cleaner now.
  • There was a long drought, so water became very expensive.

What does 'so' connect in each sentence? Which part is the cause and which part is the effect?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Complete the sentences with 'so' and choose the correct effect.

so

Great work! You understand how 'so' links cause and effect.

B Build sentences

Combine the cause and effect using 'so'.

  1. it didn't rain for months / the crops died

    ✓ It didn't rain for months, so the crops died.

  2. the city built a recycling centre / less waste goes to landfill
    Model answer

    The city built a recycling centre, so less waste goes to landfill.

  3. the ice is melting / sea levels are rising
    Model answer

    The ice is melting, so sea levels are rising.

  4. we turned off the lights / we saved energy
    Model answer

    We turned off the lights, so we saved energy.

  5. hunting increased / the species became endangered
    Model answer

    Hunting increased, so the species became endangered.

  6. the government banned plastic bags / the beaches are cleaner
    Model answer

    The government banned plastic bags, so the beaches are cleaner.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original sentences using 'so' to show a cause and its effect on the environment.

There was a lot of pollution, so...People started recycling more, so...The weather changed, so...
Rule

We use 'so' to connect a cause (the reason) with its effect (the result). The cause comes first, followed by a comma, then 'so' and the effect.

TR'So' bir nedeni (sebebi) sonucuyla (etkisiyle) bağlamak için kullanılır. Önce neden gelir, sonra virgül, sonra 'so' ve etki gelir.

When?

Use 'so' when you want to show that one event or situation directly causes another.

TRBir olayın veya durumun doğrudan başka bir olaya neden olduğunu göstermek istediğinizde 'so' kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • The river was polluted, so the government closed the beach.
  • We saved water, so our bills were lower.

Negative

  • We didn't recycle enough, so waste kept increasing.
  • They didn't protect the forest, so animals lost their habitat.

Question

  • Not typically used to form questions; 'so' links two statements.
  • Why did they build solar farms, so that they could reduce emissions? (less common form)

Short answers

  • Not applicable; 'so' joins two clauses rather than giving a short answer.
Mistakes
  • The air was dirty, because people stopped going outside.The air was dirty, so people stopped going outside.

    Use 'so' for a result, not 'because' (which gives a reason before the result).

    TRSonuç için 'because' değil 'so' kullanılır ('because' nedeni sonuçtan önce verir).

  • It was raining so we brought umbrellas so we didn't get wet.It was raining, so we brought umbrellas.

    Avoid using 'so' twice in one sentence; keep one clear cause-effect link.

    TRBir cümlede 'so' iki kez kullanılmamalı; tek ve net bir neden-sonuç bağlantısı kurulmalı.

so vs. because
so (result)because (reason)
Order of ideascause, so effectIt rained a lot, so the river flooded.effect because causeThe river flooded because it rained a lot.

The World's Water Problem

Water is one of the most important natural resources on Earth, but it is not endless. Although almost seventy percent of the planet is covered with water, only a small part of it is fresh water that people, animals, and plants can use. Most fresh water is locked in ice or deep underground, so the amount that is easily available is actually quite limited.

In many parts of the world, people are already facing serious water shortages. Long periods of drought, growing populations, and wasteful habits are putting pressure on rivers and lakes that used to provide plenty of clean water. In some regions, farmers cannot grow enough food because there simply isn't enough water for their fields, so families are forced to move to cities to find other work.

Factories and farms are also responsible for a lot of water pollution. Chemicals and waste that are dumped into rivers make the water unsafe for drinking, swimming, or fishing. This pollution harms the ecosystems that depend on clean water, and it can take many years for a damaged river to recover.

Fortunately, there are simple actions that can help. Fixing leaking pipes, taking shorter showers, and collecting rainwater are habits that reduce water waste at home. On a larger scale, governments and companies that invest in better water treatment and recycling systems can protect this resource for future generations. Every person who saves water, even a small amount, makes a difference.

Saving the Rainforest

Rainforests, which cover only a small part of the Earth's land, are home to more than half of the world's plant and animal species. They also produce a large amount of the oxygen we breathe and absorb huge amounts of carbon dioxide, so they play a key role in controlling the planet's climate.

Despite their importance, rainforests are disappearing quickly. Every year, large areas of forest are cut down for wood, farmland, and mining. This process, which is called deforestation, destroys the habitats of countless animals and forces some species closer to extinction. When trees that store carbon are burned or removed, that carbon is released into the atmosphere, so deforestation also makes climate change worse.

People who live near rainforests often depend on them for food, medicine, and their way of life. When the forest disappears, these communities lose resources that they have used for generations. This shows that protecting rainforests is not only about saving animals and plants, but also about supporting the people who call these forests home.

There are hopeful signs, though. Organisations that plant new trees, governments that create protected forest areas, and companies that choose sustainable wood are all part of the solution. Consumers who check where their products come from can also help by choosing goods that do not contribute to deforestation. Small choices, repeated by millions of people, can add up to a big difference.

Protecting Our Natural Resources

In your group, discuss three natural resources in your region and agree on the two most important actions your community could take to protect each one.

Useful language

I think we should...One resource that is at risk is...In my opinion, the best solution is...I agree/disagree because...We could also consider...

Adımlar

  1. Choose three natural resources that are important in your region.
  2. Discuss why each resource is important and what problems it faces.
  3. Agree on two protection actions for each resource.
  4. Prepare a short summary to present to the class.
DestekGive students a list of resource and problem words (water, forest, soil, pollution, shortage) to use as prompts.
HedefStudents discuss freely, using relative clauses and 'so' where natural.
İleriAsk stronger groups to rank all six actions from most to least urgent and justify the order.
SpeakingGroups of 410-12 min

Role-Play: Town Council Meeting

Role-play a town council meeting where members debate a new factory that could bring jobs but may harm a nearby river. Decide together whether to approve it.

Useful language

The factory that we are discussing...This decision will affect people who...If we approve this, so...I'm concerned about...A fair compromise would be...

Adımlar

  1. Assign roles: council leader, factory representative, environmental expert, local resident.
  2. Each role prepares two or three points to support their position.
  3. Hold the meeting and let each member speak.
  4. Vote as a group and explain your final decision.
DestekProvide role cards with key phrases and vocabulary for each character.
HedefStudents argue their assigned position and reach a group decision.
İleriAsk stronger students to include a compromise solution that considers both jobs and the environment.
