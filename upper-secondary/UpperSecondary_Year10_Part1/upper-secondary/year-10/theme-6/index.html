Write 2-3 original sentences about your own life experiences using the present perfect with 'since', 'for', 'already' or 'just'.

I have never...I have already...I have lived here for/since...
Rule

We form the present perfect with have/has + past participle. We use it for life experiences (without saying exactly when), for actions that started in the past and continue now (with 'for' + a period of time or 'since' + a point in time), and for very recent actions ('just') or actions completed before now ('already').

TRPresent perfect, have/has + fiilin 3. hali ile yapılır. Ne zaman olduğunu söylemeden yaşam deneyimlerini anlatmak için, geçmişte başlayıp hâlâ devam eden durumlar için ('for' + süre veya 'since' + zaman noktası ile) ve çok yakın zamanda olmuş ('just') veya şu andan önce tamamlanmış ('already') eylemler için kullanılır.

When?

Use it to talk about travel and life experiences ('Have you ever...?'), how long something has continued ('How long have you lived here?'), and recent completed actions ('I have just finished my homework.').

TRSeyahat ve yaşam deneyimlerinden bahsederken ('Hiç ... yaptın mı?'), bir şeyin ne kadar süredir devam ettiğini sorarken ('Burada ne kadar süredir yaşıyorsun?') ve yakın zamanda tamamlanmış eylemlerden bahsederken ('Ödevimi az önce bitirdim.') kullanılır.

Mistakes
  • I have went to three countries.I have gone to three countries.

    Use the past participle (gone), not the past simple form (went), after have/has.

    TRHave/has'ten sonra geçmiş zaman hali (went) değil, geçmiş ortaç (gone) kullanılır.

  • She has live in Ankara since five years.She has lived in Ankara for five years.

    Use 'for' with a period of time (five years) and 'since' with a point in time (2019).

    TRSüre belirtirken (beş yıl) 'for', zaman noktası belirtirken (2019) 'since' kullanılır.

  • I have already ate breakfast.I have already eaten breakfast.

    The past participle after 'have' is 'eaten', not 'ate'.

    TR'Have'den sonra gelen geçmiş ortaç 'eaten'dır, 'ate' değil.

Write 2-3 original sentences about the food in your own kitchen using some, any, much or many.

There is/are some...We don't have much/many...How much/many...?
Rule

Countable nouns (an egg, two eggs, apples) have singular and plural forms and are used with 'many'. Uncountable nouns (rice, milk, sugar) have no plural form and are used with 'much'. 'Some' is usually used in positive sentences (and offers/requests), and 'any' is usually used in negative sentences and questions, with both countable and uncountable nouns.

TRSayılabilen isimler (bir yumurta, iki yumurta, elmalar) tekil ve çoğul biçimlere sahiptir ve 'many' ile kullanılır. Sayılamayan isimlerin (pirinç, süt, şeker) çoğul biçimi yoktur ve 'much' ile kullanılır. 'Some' genellikle olumlu cümlelerde (ve teklif/istek cümlelerinde), 'any' ise genellikle olumsuz cümlelerde ve sorularda, hem sayılabilen hem sayılamayan isimlerle kullanılır.

When?

Use these words when shopping for food, talking about quantities in recipes, or asking what is available in the fridge or at the market.

TRYemek alışverişi yaparken, tariflerdeki miktarlardan bahsederken veya buzdolabında/pazarda nelerin bulunduğunu sorarken bu kelimeler kullanılır.

Rule

A question tag is a short question added to the end of a statement to check information or keep a conversation going. If the main sentence is positive, the tag is usually negative; if the main sentence is negative, the tag is usually positive. The tag repeats the auxiliary/modal verb of the main sentence (or uses do/does/did for the present simple/past simple).

TRSoru eki, bilgiyi kontrol etmek veya sohbeti sürdürmek için bir ifadenin sonuna eklenen kısa bir sorudur. Ana cümle olumluysa eki genellikle olumsuz, ana cümle olumsuzsa eki genellikle olumludur. Ek, ana cümledeki yardımcı/yapı fiili tekrarlar (ya da geniş/geçmiş zamanda do/does/did kullanılır).

When?

Use question tags in everyday conversation to confirm something you think is true, or to invite the other person to agree or respond.

TRGünlük konuşmada doğru olduğunu düşündüğünüz bir şeyi teyit etmek veya karşınızdaki kişiyi hemfikir olmaya/cevap vermeye davet etmek için soru ekleri kullanılır.

A World of Continents and Cultures

The world is divided into seven continents: Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe and Australia. Each continent is home to many countries, and each country has its own nationality, language and traditions. Turkey, for example, is located on two continents, Europe and Asia, which makes its culture a fascinating mix of East and West.

People from different countries often have very different daily habits. In some cultures, families eat their main meal together in the evening, while in others, lunch is the most important meal of the day. Greetings can also vary a lot: in some countries people shake hands, in others they bow, and in others they kiss on both cheeks. Learning about these customs helps travellers show respect when they visit a new place.

Nationality is not only about a passport; it is also about shared history, food and celebrations. Many young people today have friends from several different nationalities because of the internet and international schools. This is a wonderful opportunity to learn about other cultures without leaving home. By asking questions and listening carefully, students can discover how people in other parts of the world live, eat and celebrate special occasions.

Understanding other cultures also helps us understand our own culture better. When we compare our traditions with those of other countries, we notice what makes our own nationality and way of life special. This is why many schools now encourage students to learn about world cultures alongside their own history and language.

Food Around the World

Food is one of the best ways to learn about a country's culture. In Italy, families often gather for long meals that include pasta, fresh bread and vegetables grown nearby. In Japan, rice, fish and vegetables are the base of most meals, and food is usually presented in small, beautifully arranged portions. In Mexico, corn, beans and chilli peppers appear in almost every traditional dish, giving Mexican cuisine its famous spicy flavour.

In Turkey, meals often start with a variety of small dishes called mezes, followed by grilled meat or fish and ending with sweet desserts like baklava. Bread is a staple food and is served with almost every meal. Many Turkish families still follow the tradition of eating together and sharing food from the same plates, which shows the importance of community and hospitality in Turkish culture.

Shopping for food is also different from country to country. In some places, people buy fresh ingredients every day from local markets, while in others, they prefer to buy packaged food from large supermarkets once a week. Some people follow special diets for health or personal reasons, such as being vegetarian or avoiding certain ingredients, and shops around the world are increasingly offering more choices to meet these needs.

Trying new food is a simple but powerful way to connect with another culture. Even if you cannot travel to a country, tasting its traditional dishes can teach you about its history, geography and values. This is one reason why food culture is such a popular topic when people talk about life in different parts of the world.

Have you ever been to...?I've never...I've already...You've tried..., haven't you?That's..., isn't it?

Adımlar

  1. Prepare five present-perfect questions about travel, food or meeting people of different nationalities.
  2. Take turns asking and answering your questions with your partner.
  3. Add a question tag to at least two of your statements during the conversation, e.g. 'You've never eaten sushi, have you?'
  4. Report one interesting thing you learned about your partner to the class.
DestekGive students a list of ready-made present perfect questions (e.g. Have you ever tried...? Have you ever visited...?) that they can choose from.
HedefStudents write and ask their own five questions using the target structures with minimal teacher support.
İleriStudents also ask a follow-up 'How long...?' question with 'since' or 'for' and add a question tag of their own.

Shopping Role-Play: At the Food Market

In groups, act out a role-play in which a customer buys food from a market seller, practising countable/uncountable nouns and talking about personal food preferences.

Useful language

Do you have any...?How much/many... do you need?I'd like some...I don't eat... because I'm vegetarian.That's... in total.

Adımlar

  1. In your group, decide who is the seller and who are the customers.
  2. The seller makes a short list of food items for sale (some countable, some uncountable).
  3. Customers ask about quantity, price and their own food preferences (e.g. being vegetarian, not liking spicy food).
  4. Act out the shopping conversation for the class, using at least four question forms with much/many/some/any.
DestekProvide a simple shopping dialogue frame with gaps for students to fill in with vocabulary and quantities.
HedefStudents build their own short dialogue from the useful language list with teacher monitoring.
İleriStudents extend the role-play by adding a disagreement about price or a special request related to a dietary need.
Writing120-150 words

My Country and Its Food Culture

Write a paragraph describing your country (or a country you find interesting), its people, and one or two traditional dishes that represent its food culture.

Plan

  1. Choose the country you will write about.
  2. List 3-4 facts about its location, nationality and culture.
  3. List 1-2 traditional dishes and a short description of each.
  4. Organise your ideas into an opening sentence, two or three detail sentences, and a closing sentence.

A Shopping List and My Food Preferences

Write a short shopping list for a healthy dinner, then write a few sentences explaining your personal food preferences and anything you don't eat or need to avoid.

Plan

  1. Make a shopping list with both countable and uncountable food items.
  2. Note the quantity you need for each item (some, a little, two, etc.).
  3. Write 2-3 sentences about what you like, dislike or don't eat, and why.
  4. Check that you have used some, any, much and many correctly.

Kullanabileceğin kelime ve kalıplar

groceryquantityportiondietvegetarianfreshpackagedFor dinner, I need some/a few...I don't need much/many...I usually eat..., but I don't eat... because...I prefer fresh food to packaged food.
