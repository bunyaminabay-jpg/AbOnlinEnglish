In Theme 1, you will meet students from different countries, learn about their nationalities and languages, and find out about capital cities, tourist attractions and national days around the world. You will learn to introduce yourself and others with 'to be' (am/is/are), and to talk about possibility, opportunity and ability with 'can'.

Konular

  • Students of different countries, nationalities, and languages
  • Capitals of their countries, and tourist attractions
  • Activities in their capitals and countries
  • National days and celebrations

Dil yapıları

  • To be (present): nationality, country, capital
  • Can: possibility, opportunity, language ability

Anlayabileceğim

  • A pen-pal blog post about students from different countries

  • A video call between two exchange students
  • A short talk about planning an 'International School' presentation

To be (am/is/are): nationality and origin

1 · Notice
  • I am Turkish and I am from Balıkesir.
  • Kasia is Polish. She is from Warsaw.
  • We are students of different nationalities, but we aren't from the same country.
  • Is Zoltán Hungarian? — Yes, he is. / No, he isn't.

Which form of 'be' goes with I, with he/she, and with we/they? How do we make it negative and a question?

C Your turn

Write four sentences about yourself and one classmate: your nationality, where you are from, and one thing you are not.

I am...I am from...My classmate is...We aren't...
Rule

We use 'to be' (am/is/are) in the present to talk about identity, nationality and where someone is from. Use am with I, is with he/she/it, and are with we/you/they. Negative: add 'not' (isn't, aren't). Question: put the 'be' verb before the subject.

TR'To be' (am/is/are) şimdiki zamanda kimlik, milliyet ve nereli olduğumuzu anlatmak için kullanılır. I ile am, he/she/it ile is, we/you/they ile are kullanılır. Olumsuzda 'not' eklenir (isn't, aren't); soruda be fiili özneden önce gelir.

When?

We use this to introduce ourselves, our classmates and exchange students, and to say our nationality, country and where we are from.

TRKendimizi, sınıf arkadaşlarımızı ve değişim öğrencilerini tanıtırken; milliyet, ülke ve nereli olduğumuzu söylerken kullanırız.

3 · Form

Positive

  • I am Turkish.
  • She is from Warsaw.

Negative

  • We aren't from the same country.
  • He isn't Polish.

Question

  • Is Zoltán Hungarian?
  • Are you an exchange student?

Short answers

  • Yes, he is. / No, he isn't.
  • Yes, we are. / No, we aren't.
Mistakes
  • She are Polish.She is Polish.

    Use 'is' with she/he/it, not 'are'.

    TRShe/he/it ile 'is' kullanılır, 'are' değil.

  • I is from Türkiye.I am from Türkiye.

    Always use 'am' with I.

    TRI ile her zaman 'am' kullanılır.

Can: possibility, opportunity and ability

1 · Notice
  • In Istanbul, you can visit lots of famous mosques and palaces.
  • Kasia can speak Polish, English and a little German.
  • You can't see the northern lights in Türkiye — it's too far south.
  • Can you visit the capital by train? — Yes, you can.

Which sentences talk about what is possible to do in a place? Which talk about a language/skill ability? How do we make 'can' negative and a question?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Look at the Notice sentences again. Complete the rule with the words in the box.

cancan'tCanbase verb

Well done! You found the rule yourself. Now let's use it.

B Build sentences

Use the cues to write sentences with can/can't about countries and abilities. Look at the example first.

  1. in Istanbul visit famous mosques and palaces

    ✓ In Istanbul, you can visit famous mosques and palaces.

  2. Kasia speak Polish, English and a little German
    Model answer

    Kasia can speak Polish, English and a little German.

  3. you not / see the northern lights in Türkiye
    Model answer

    You can't see the northern lights in Türkiye.

  4. ? / you visit the capital by train
    Model answer

    Can you visit the capital by train?

  5. tourists climb the old castle tower
    Model answer

    Tourists can climb the old castle tower.

  6. ? / we try the national dish at the festival
    Model answer

    Can we try the national dish at the festival?

C Your turn

Write three sentences with can/can't about your own country: one about a tourist attraction, one about a language ability, and one negative sentence.

In my country, you can...I can speak...You can't...
Rule

We use 'can' to talk about possibility or opportunity in a place, and about ability (including language skills). Negative: can't. Question: Can + subject + base verb? Always use the base verb after can/can't.

TR'Can' bir yerde neyin mümkün olduğunu (imkan/fırsat) ve bir yeteneği anlatmak için kullanılır. Olumsuzda 'can't', soruda 'can' özneden önce gelir. Can'dan sonra fiilin yalın hâli kullanılır.

When?

We use this to say what you can see or do in a country or city, and to talk about language skills and other abilities.

TRBir ülkede/şehirde nelerin görülüp yapılabileceğini, ve bir dili/beceriyi ne kadar iyi bildiğimizi anlatırken kullanırız.

3 · Form

Positive

  • You can visit the old town on foot.
  • She can speak three languages.

Negative

  • You can't see the sea from the capital.
  • He can't speak Hungarian yet.

Question

  • Can you visit the castle at night?
  • Can she speak Azerbaijani?

Short answers

  • Yes, you can. / No, you can't.
  • Yes, she can. / No, she can't.
Mistakes
  • She can to speak three languages.She can speak three languages.

    Don't use 'to' after can.

    TRCan'dan sonra 'to' gelmez.

  • She cans speak Polish.She can speak Polish.

    Can never takes -s, even with he/she/it.

    TRCan, he/she/it ile de değişmez, -s almaz.

My New Pen Pal from Poland

Hi, everyone! My name is Elif, and I'm a student at a school in Balıkesir, Türkiye. This month, our class started an international pen-pal project, and I'm so excited — my pen pal is Kasia, and she's from Poland!

Kasia is Polish, and she is from Warsaw, the capital city of Poland. In her first email, she told me that Warsaw's official language is Polish, but she can also speak good English and a little German. I told her that I am Turkish, and my nearest big city is İzmir.

Kasia says that in Warsaw, you can visit the Old Town, with its colourful buildings and the famous Royal Castle. You can also see a huge monument called the Palace of Culture and Science from almost anywhere in the city! She sent me a photo of the castle — it looks amazing.

I wrote back and told her about my own country. I explained that Ankara is the capital of Türkiye, but I live near İstanbul, where you can visit incredible mosques, palaces and the famous Grand Bazaar. I also told her about our national day, the 29th of October, when we celebrate the founding of the Turkish Republic.

Kasia and I are already planning to be video-call friends. We aren't in the same country, but with a pen pal like Kasia, the world feels a little smaller — and a lot more interesting!

International Day at Our School

Every spring, our school celebrates International Day, and it's one of my favourite events of the year. Students from different classes prepare a small stand about a country — its flag, capital city, nationality, and one famous tourist attraction.

This year, my group chose Azerbaijan. We learned that the capital of Azerbaijan is Baku, and that Azerbaijani is the official language there. We found out that in Baku, you can see the famous Flame Towers, which light up in different colours at night. We also made a poster with the flag and some facts about the population.

Other groups presented Hungary and Poland. The Hungarian group explained that Budapest is the capital of Hungary, and that you can go sightseeing along the Danube River. The Polish group talked about Warsaw's Old Town and even brought a small national dish to try — everyone loved it!

In the afternoon, each group gave a short presentation about their country. We answered questions like 'Can you tell us about a national day in this country?' and 'What can tourists do there?' It was a great chance to practise speaking and to learn about the world at the same time.

At the end of the day, our teacher said that International Day helps us understand that although we come from different countries and speak different languages, we can still learn from each other and be good friends.

Guess My Country

Think of a country. Walk around the class and answer your classmates' yes/no questions with 'to be' and 'can' so they can guess your country. Then guess theirs.

Useful language

Is it in Europe?Can you see the sea from the capital?Are people from this country called...?Yes, it is. / No, it isn't. / Yes, you can. / No, you can't.

Adımlar

  1. Secretly choose a country you know something about.
  2. Find a partner and let them ask up to 5 yes/no questions with 'is/are' or 'can'.
  3. Answer honestly with short answers.
  4. Swap roles, then find a new partner.
  5. After 4-5 rounds, share one interesting guess with the class.

International School — Interview a Classmate

Interview a partner about their country, nationality, capital, a tourist attraction and a national day (real or invented, for creative-writing purposes). Take notes, then prepare to present your partner to the class as part of the 'International School' presentation.

Useful language

What's your nationality?What's the capital of your country?What can tourists do or see there?This is [name] from [country]. He/She is... You can visit...

Adımlar

  1. In pairs, take turns asking the interview questions and noting the answers.
  2. Check spelling of country/capital names together.
  3. Prepare 4-5 sentences introducing your partner and their country.
  4. Practise your short presentation with your partner.
  5. Present your partner to a small group or the whole class.

My Country Fact File

A Postcard from a Tourist Attraction
