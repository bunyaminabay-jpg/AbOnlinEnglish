In Theme 4, you will meet families whose members have different jobs, work routines and workplaces. You will learn how to talk about what people do every day at work, where they work, and what their daily work activities are. You will practise the Simple Present tense for jobs and work activities, and the prepositions of place 'at', 'in' and 'on' for talking about workplaces.

Konular

  • Family members' jobs, work routines
  • Family members' work activities and workplaces

Dil yapıları

  • Simple Present (+, –, ?): jobs, work activities
  • Prepositions of place: at, in, on

Anlayabileceğim

  • A blog post about a student's family members and their different jobs
  • A text about a school 'Career Day' where students present a family member's job
  • A phone call between two friends talking about their parents' jobs

Söyleyebileceğim

Simple Present: jobs and work activities (+, –, ?)

1 · Notice
  • My aunt works as a bank clerk in the city centre.
  • My uncle doesn't work at weekends — the shopping centre is closed on Sundays.
  • Every day, my mother checks medicine and serves customers at the pharmacy.
  • Does your cousin work in a shopping centre? — Yes, she does.

What do we add to the verb with he/she/it? How do we make the negative and the question in the Simple Present?

C Your turn

Write four sentences about a family member's job: what they do, where they work, and one thing they don't do at work.

He/She works as...He/She works at/in...Every day, he/she...He/She doesn't...
Rule

We use the Simple Present to talk about regular, habitual actions like family members' jobs and work routines. Add -s/-es to the verb with he/she/it. Negative: doesn't + base verb. Question: Does + subject + base verb?

TRŞimdiki zaman (Simple Present), aile üyelerinin meslekleri ve iş rutinleri gibi düzenli, alışılmış eylemleri anlatmak için kullanılır. He/she/it ile fiile -s/-es eklenir. Olumsuzda 'doesn't' + yalın fiil, soruda 'Does' özneden önce gelir ve fiil yalın hâlde kalır.

When?

We use this to say what job a family member has, what they do at work, and to describe their work routine.

TRAile üyelerinin ne iş yaptığını, işte ne yaptıklarını ve iş rutinlerini anlatırken kullanırız.

Prepositions of place: at, in, on (workplaces)

1 · Notice
  • My mother works at the pharmacy near our house.
  • My father works in a big office in the city centre.
  • There's a photo of my uncle on the wall of the bank.
  • Does your cousin work at a shop in Balıkesir?

Which preposition do we use for a specific point or building? Which one do we use for a bigger, enclosed space? Which one do we use for a surface?

C Your turn

Write three sentences with at/in/on about where your family members work.

My... works at...My... works in...There's a... on...
Rule

Use 'at' for a specific point or building (at the bank), 'in' for a bigger, enclosed space (in the office, in Balıkesir), and 'on' for a surface (on the wall).

TR'At' belirli bir nokta veya bina için (at the bank), 'in' daha büyük, kapalı bir alan için (in the office, in Balıkesir), 'on' ise bir yüzey için (on the wall) kullanılır.

When?

We use this to say where a family member works or where something is located.

TRBir aile üyesinin nerede çalıştığını veya bir şeyin nerede olduğunu anlatırken kullanırız.

3 · Form

Positive

  • She works at the pharmacy.
  • He works in an office.

Negative

  • She doesn't work at the bank.
  • He doesn't work in a warehouse.

Question

  • Does she work at the shop?
  • Does he work in a shopping centre?

Short answers

  • Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.
  • Yes, he does. / No, he doesn't.
Mistakes
  • She works on the bank.She works at the bank.

    Use 'at' for a specific building or point, not 'on'.

    TRBelirli bir bina/nokta için 'at' kullanılır, 'on' değil.

  • He works at Balıkesir.He works in Balıkesir.

    Use 'in' for a bigger area like a city, not 'at'.

    TRBir şehir gibi büyük bir alan için 'in' kullanılır.

My Family, Different Jobs

Hi, everyone! My name is Deniz, and today I want to tell you about the different jobs in my family. We're not a family of teachers or doctors — we all do very different things!

My mother is a pharmacist, and she works at a small pharmacy in the town centre. Every day, she checks medicine and serves customers who need advice. She doesn't work on Sundays, so that's our family day together.

My father is an electrician. He doesn't work in an office — he visits different houses and fixes the lights and wires. His work routine changes every day, because every house has a different problem!

My aunt works as a bank clerk at a branch in the city centre. She serves customers, checks their information, and helps them with their money. My uncle, on the other hand, works at a big shopping centre nearby, where he's a security guard. He checks IDs and makes sure everyone is safe.

My cousin is still a student, but she works as a shop assistant at the weekend. She serves customers in a small clothes shop and says she loves talking to people all day.

Every family has different jobs and different work routines, but we all sit down together on Sunday and talk about our week. I think that's the best part!

Career Day at Our School

Every autumn, our school organises a Career Day, and it's always one of the most interesting days of the year. Each student chooses one family member's job and prepares a short poster and presentation about it.

This year, I chose my aunt's job. She's a flight attendant, so I explained that she works on planes and travels to different cities every week. I made a poster with a photo of an airport and some facts about her work routine.

My classmate Ela presented her father's job. He's a mechanic, and he works in a garage near our neighbourhood. Ela explained that he fixes cars every day and sometimes works late in the evening when there's a lot of work.

Another classmate, Kerem, talked about his mother, who works as an accountant in an office in the city centre. He said she doesn't work with cars or customers — she works with numbers and computers all day.

In the afternoon, each student gave a short presentation and answered questions like 'Does your family member work at the weekend?' and 'What does a typical work day look like for them?' It was a great chance to practise speaking and learn about different jobs.

At the end of the day, our teacher said that Career Day helps us understand that every job is important, whether it's fixing cars, serving customers, or helping people with their money.

Guess the Job

Think of a job for an invented family member. Walk around the class and answer your classmates' yes/no questions with the Simple Present and at/in/on so they can guess the job. Then guess theirs.

Useful language

Does he/she work in an office?Does he/she work at a shopping centre?Does he/she serve customers?Yes, he/she does. / No, he/she doesn't.

Adımlar

  1. Secretly choose a job and a workplace for your invented family member.
  2. Find a partner and let them ask up to 5 yes/no questions with 'does'.
  3. Answer honestly with short answers.
  4. Swap roles, then find a new partner.
  5. After 4-5 rounds, share one interesting guess with the class.
DestekGive students a list of sample questions before they start.
Hedef5 rounds of guessing with a partner.
İleriFast finishers add one extra clue using 'at/in/on' about the workplace.

Career Day — Interview a Classmate

Interview a partner about a family member's job, workplace and work routine (real or invented, for creative-writing purposes). Take notes, then prepare to present your partner's family member as part of the 'Career Day' presentation.

Useful language

What does your... do?Where does he/she work?What does he/she do every day?This is [name]'s [family member]. He/She works as... He/She works at/in...

Adımlar

  1. In pairs, take turns asking the interview questions and noting the answers.
  2. Check the job title and workplace name together.
  3. Prepare 4-5 sentences introducing your partner's family member.
  4. Practise your short presentation with your partner.
  5. Present your partner's family member to a small group or the whole class.

A Day at My Family Member's Workplace
