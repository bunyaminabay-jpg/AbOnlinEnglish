Clauses of Reason and Result

1 · Notice
  • Many families choose homeschooling because it lets children learn at their own pace.
  • Since the exam covers so much material, students often revise for weeks beforehand.
  • The lecture was so boring that half the class stopped taking notes.
  • The school introduced such a flexible timetable that attendance improved almost overnight.

In which sentences does the underlined word introduce a REASON (why something happens), and in which introduces a RESULT (what happens because of it)?

C Your turn

Write four sentences explaining reasons and results connected to your own education — why you chose something, or what a result of your effort was.

I chose… because…Since…, I decided to…The class was so… that…It was such a… that…
Rule

Reason connectors (because, since, as, due to) explain WHY something happens. 'So + adjective/adverb + that' and 'such + (a/an) + adjective + noun + that' structures describe the RESULT of something. 'Due to' is followed by a noun/noun phrase (due to low attendance), while the others are followed by a clause.

TRSebep bildiren bağlaçlar (because, since, as, due to) bir olayın NEDENİNİ açıklar. 'So + sıfat/zarf + that' ve 'such + (a/an) + sıfat + isim + that' yapıları ise bir olayın SONUCUNU bildirir. 'Due to' isim/isim öbeğiyle kullanılır (due to low attendance), diğerleri bir cümle alır.

When?

We use because/since/as/due to to explain the reason behind an educational choice or outcome; we use so...that/such...that to show how intense something was and what result it led to.

TRBir eğitim tercihinin ya da başarının nedenini açıklarken because/since/as/due to; bir durumun ne kadar yoğun olduğunu ve bunun ne gibi bir sonuca yol açtığını anlatırken so...that/such...that kullanırız.

3 · Form

Positive

  • She switched schools because the new one offered better labs.
  • The course was so intensive that students needed extra breaks.

Negative

  • He didn't drop out, even though the material was difficult.
  • It wasn't such a hard exam that everyone failed.

Question

  • Why did you choose this programme?
  • Was the workshop so useful that you'd recommend it?

Short answers

  • Because it matched my interests.
  • Yes, it was. / No, not really.
Mistakes
  • She chose this school due to it has smaller classes.She chose this school due to its smaller classes. / She chose this school because it has smaller classes.

    'Due to' is followed by a noun phrase, not a clause; use 'because' before a clause.

    TR'Due to' bir isim öbeği alır, cümle alamaz; cümle için 'because' kullanılmalıdır.

  • The lesson was so interesting lesson that nobody left early.The lesson was so interesting that nobody left early.

    In the 'so' structure, the noun is not repeated: so + adjective + that.

    TR'So' yapısında isim tekrar edilmez: so + sıfat + that.

  • It was such interesting lesson that we asked for more.It was such an interesting lesson that we asked for more.

    In the 'such' structure, don't forget 'a/an' before a singular countable noun.

    TR'Such' yapısında tekil sayılabilir isimden önce 'a/an' unutulmamalıdır.

Present Tense for General Truths + Modal Verbs of Deduction (Body Language)

1 · Notice
  • Crossed arms often signal that a person feels defensive or uncomfortable.
  • She's avoiding eye contact, so she might be nervous about the interview.
  • He must be confident — look at that relaxed posture and steady eye contact.
  • They can't be telling the truth; their facial expressions don't match their words at all.

Which sentence states a general truth about body language, and which sentences make a GUESS about someone's feelings based on evidence?

    Model answer

C Your turn

Describe a person's body language in a situation you imagine (an interview, a presentation, a first date) and write four sentences interpreting what it might mean.

Her posture suggests…He might be… because…That must mean…They can't be… since…
Rule

Use the Present Simple to state the general meaning of body language (Crossed arms often signal discomfort). Use modal verbs to guess how someone feels based on evidence: 'might/could be' (weak possibility), 'must be' (strong positive guess), 'can't be' (strong negative guess/impossible).

TRBeden dilinin genel anlamını açıklarken Present Simple kullanılır (Crossed arms often signal discomfort). Birinin ne hissettiğini KANITA dayanarak tahmin ederken modal fiiller kullanılır: 'might/could be' (zayıf ihtimal), 'must be' (güçlü olumlu tahmin), 'can't be' (güçlü olumsuz tahmin/imkânsız).

When?

We use the Present Simple to state a general rule about body language, and modal verbs of deduction to guess how a specific person is feeling right now, based on evidence.

TRBeden dilinin genel kuralını anlatırken Present Simple; belirli bir kişinin o an ne hissettiğini kanıta dayanarak tahmin ederken modal fiilleri kullanırız.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Steady eye contact usually suggests honesty.
  • She must be excited — she can't stop smiling.

Negative

  • Crossed arms don't always mean someone is angry.
  • He can't be relaxed; his shoulders are so tense.

Question

  • What does a raised eyebrow usually mean?
  • Could that gesture mean something different in another culture?

Short answers

  • It usually means surprise or doubt.
  • Yes, it could. / No, it can't.
Mistakes
  • She must being nervous because she's biting her nails.She must be nervous because she's biting her nails.

    After a modal verb, use the base form of the verb: must be, not must being.

    TRModal fiilden sonra fiilin yalın (base) hâli kullanılır: must be, not must being.

  • He can't be interested, he's smiling and leaning forward.He can't be uninterested — he's smiling and leaning forward. / He must be interested, since he's smiling and leaning forward.

    When the evidence points to a positive feeling, use 'must be' rather than 'can't be'.

    TRKanıt olumlu bir duyguyu işaret ediyorsa, 'can't be' yerine 'must be' kullanılmalıdır.

  • Crossed arms is signalling discomfort in general.Crossed arms generally signal discomfort.

    A general truth is expressed with the Present Simple, not the Present Progressive.

    TRGenel bir gerçek anlatılırken Present Progressive değil Present Simple kullanılır.

One Size Doesn't Fit All

Walk into a classroom in one country and you might see rows of silent students copying notes from a board. Walk into a classroom elsewhere, and you might find children sitting in a circle, designing their own project for the week. Neither approach is automatically wrong; each simply grew out of different beliefs about how learning works best.

Traditional, exam-focused systems often exist because they can be measured easily and applied to large numbers of students at once. Since a single national test can be marked quickly and compared fairly, many governments rely on it, even though critics argue it encourages rote learning rather than real understanding. Such systems can be so exam-driven that students learn to answer questions without ever asking their own.

Student-centred methods, on the other hand, grew popular due to research suggesting that motivation and self-discipline improve when learners have real choices. A student who picks their own topic is often so engaged that they keep researching long after the lesson ends. The challenge is that this approach demands well-trained teachers and smaller classes — resources not every school can afford.

The Conversation Nobody Speaks

Long before a person finishes a sentence, their body has usually already said something. Researchers who study communication estimate that a large share of meaning in face-to-face conversation comes not from words, but from posture, gesture and facial expression. This silent layer of communication can support what someone says — or quietly contradict it.

Some signals appear to be almost universal. A genuine smile, for example, tends to involve the muscles around the eyes as well as the mouth, which is why a forced smile can look slightly 'off' even to a stranger. Other signals, however, change enormously between cultures. Direct eye contact might suggest confidence and honesty in one country, while in another it can be read as rude or challenging.

This is exactly why body language must be interpreted carefully, never in isolation. A single crossed-arm gesture doesn't automatically mean someone is angry or closed off; they might simply be cold, or comfortable standing that way. Skilled observers look for clusters of signals and consider the context before drawing any conclusion about what a person really means.

Speaking

Why I'd Choose It

In pairs, discuss which education system or learning method you would choose if you could design your own school, and why. Use reason and result connectors (because, since, as, due to, so...that, such...that) as you explain your choices.

Speaking

What's the Body Saying?

In pairs, take turns acting out a short scene (a job interview, a first day at a new school, a disagreement between friends) using only body language for 30 seconds, while your partner guesses what you might be feeling. Then discuss your interpretations using modal verbs of deduction.

Writing

My Ideal Way to Learn

Write a short opinion essay about which education method or system you think works best, and why. Start with a clear topic sentence, add at least three supporting sentences using reason/result connectors, and finish with a concluding sentence.

Writing

Reading a Photograph

Choose (or imagine) a photograph of two or three people interacting. Describe their body language in detail and interpret what you think each person might be feeling, using modal verbs of deduction.
