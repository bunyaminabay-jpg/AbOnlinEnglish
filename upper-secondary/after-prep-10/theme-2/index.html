Simple Past, Past Continuous & used to: Narrating Past Events and Life Stories

1 · Notice
  • The young director grew up in a small coastal town before she moved to the city to study film.
  • While the actors were rehearsing the final scene, a sudden storm hit the old studio.
  • As a child, she used to watch black-and-white classics with her grandmother every Sunday.
  • By the time the credits rolled, most of the audience was wiping away tears.

Which sentences describe a single completed action, which describe an action in progress that was interrupted, and which describe a repeated habit from the past that isn't true anymore?

2 · Discover

B Build sentences

Use the cues to build true sentences about past events and life stories. Choose Simple Past, Past Continuous or used to.

  1. the author grow up / in a village before he became famous

    ✓ The author grew up in a village before he became famous.

  2. she write / her first novel at the age of nineteen
    Model answer

    She wrote her first novel at the age of nineteen.

  3. while the crew film / the final scene it started raining heavily
    Model answer

    While the crew was filming the final scene, it started raining heavily.

  4. as a teenager, he use to / read / one book a week
    Model answer

    As a teenager, he used to read one book a week.

  5. the composer not use to / like / his own soundtrack at first
    Model answer

    The composer didn't use to like his own soundtrack at first.

  6. ? / you ever / watch a film that changed the way you thought
    Model answer

    Did you ever watch a film that changed the way you thought?

C Your turn

Write four sentences about a book or film you remember from your childhood: what you used to do, one completed event, and one thing that was happening when something else interrupted it.

When I was younger, I used to…One day, while I was reading/watching…I finally finished the book/film when…I remember that I…
Rule

Use the Simple Past for completed past actions (grew up, wrote, watched). Use the Past Continuous (was/were + -ing) for actions in progress in the past, often interrupted by another action. Use used to + base verb for repeated past habits or states that are no longer true.

TRTamamlanmış geçmiş zaman eylemleri için Simple Past kullanılır (grew up, wrote, watched). Geçmişte devam eden ve genellikle başka bir eylem tarafından kesilen eylemler için Past Continuous kullanılır (was/were + -ing). Artık doğru olmayan, tekrarlanan geçmiş alışkanlıklar veya durumlar için used to + fiil kullanılır.

When?

We use these forms to narrate the plot of a book or film, to tell the life story of an author or director, or to talk about our own past habits and experiences with stories.

TRBir kitabın ya da filmin olay örgüsünü, bir yazarın veya yönetmenin hayatını anlatırken ya da geçmişteki alışkanlıklarımızdan bahsederken bu yapıları kullanırız.

3 · Form

Positive

  • She wrote three novels before she turned thirty.
  • He used to read comic books every evening.

Negative

  • They didn't finish the film because the ending was too sad.
  • She didn't use to enjoy classic literature.

Question

  • Did the director grow up near the sea?
  • What were you doing when the twist was revealed?

Short answers

  • Yes, she did. / No, she didn't.
  • I was watching the final scene.
Mistakes
  • While she was writing the novel, she has three young children.While she was writing the novel, she had three young children.

    When describing a past state alongside another past action, use the Simple Past, not the Present Perfect.

    TRGeçmişteki bir durumu anlatırken Present Perfect değil, Simple Past kullanılır.

  • He use to watch old films every Sunday.He used to watch old films every Sunday.

    In positive sentences, it is always 'used to', not 'use to'.

    TR'used to' olumlu cümlede her zaman 'used' şeklinde yazılır; 'use' değil.

  • The audience was crying when the film ended suddenly.The audience was crying when the film ended. / The audience cried when the film suddenly ended.

    Be careful which action is the ongoing background action (Past Continuous) and which one interrupts it (Simple Past).

    TRİki eylemden hangisinin arka planda devam ettiğine, hangisinin onu kestiğine dikkat edin.

Compare
Simple Past vs Past Continuous vs used to
Simple PastPast Continuous
Usea completed action at a specific timeShe published her first novel in 2015.an action in progress in the pastShe was writing her second novel when the film offer arrived.
Formbase verb + -ed / irregular formwrote, watched, grew upwas/were + verb-ingwas writing, were filming
Extra structureused to + base verb → a past habit that has now changedHe used to hate reading, but now he loves it.—

Expressing Preferences: prefer, would rather and comparatives

1 · Notice
  • I prefer reading the book before watching the film adaptation.
  • "Honestly, I'd rather watch a classic than a new blockbuster," she admitted.
  • For me, the novel is far more moving than the film ever could be.
  • Of all the director's works, most critics agree this one is the most thought-provoking.

Which structures compare two specific things directly, and which one picks out the best or most extreme example from a whole group?

2 · Discover

B Build sentences

Use the cues to build true sentences about preferences for books and films.

  1. I prefer / classic novels modern thrillers

    ✓ I prefer classic novels to modern thrillers.

  2. she would rather / read the book watch the adaptation
    Model answer

    She would rather read the book than watch the adaptation.

  3. this novel be / far more gripping the sequel
    Model answer

    This novel is far more gripping than the sequel.

  4. for many critics this / be / the most moving film of the decade
    Model answer

    For many critics, this is the most moving film of the decade.

  5. I not / prefer / remakes original films
    Model answer

    I don't prefer remakes to original films.

  6. ? / you would rather / read a poem or watch a film about the same story
    Model answer

    Would you rather read a poem or watch a film about the same story?

C Your turn

Write four sentences comparing a book and its film adaptation, or two classic stories you know, using prefer, would rather and at least one comparative or superlative.

I prefer…I'd rather…In my opinion, … is more … than …Of all the classics I know, … is the most …
Rule

'Prefer + noun/-ing' expresses a general preference (I prefer novels to films). 'Would rather + base verb' expresses a preference for a specific moment or situation (I'd rather read than watch TV). Use a comparative (more … than) to compare two specific things directly, and a superlative (the most …) to pick out the most extreme example in a whole group.

TR'Prefer + isim/-ing' genel bir tercihi ifade eder (I prefer novels to films). 'Would rather + fiilin yalın hali' belirli bir anda ya da durumda yapılacak tercihi belirtir (I'd rather read than watch TV). İki şeyi doğrudan karşılaştırmak için karşılaştırma sıfatı (more … than), bir grubun en uç örneğini belirtmek için ise üstünlük sıfatı (the most …) kullanılır.

When?

We use these structures when comparing a book with its film adaptation, choosing between two classic works, or describing a work as the best or most striking example within a group.

TRBir kitap ile filmini karşılaştırırken, iki klasik eser arasında tercih yaparken ya da bir eseri bir grup içinde en iyi/en etkileyici olarak nitelendirirken bu yapıları kullanırız.

3 · Form

Positive

  • I prefer reading to watching TV.
  • She'd rather finish the novel first.

Negative

  • He doesn't prefer remakes to originals.
  • I wouldn't rather watch the sequel; I'd rather reread the book.

Question

  • Do you prefer books or films?
  • Would you rather watch the classic or the remake?

Short answers

  • I prefer books. / I prefer films.
  • I'd rather watch the classic.
Mistakes
  • I prefer books than films.I prefer books to films.

    With 'prefer', use 'to' (not 'than') when comparing two things.

    TR'Prefer' ile karşılaştırma yaparken 'than' değil 'to' kullanılır.

  • I'd rather to read than watch.I'd rather read than watch.

    'Would rather' is followed by the base verb, without 'to'.

    TR'Would rather' fiilin yalın halini alır, 'to' kullanılmaz.

  • This novel is more interesting than the film ever could.This novel is more interesting than the film could ever be.

    Pay attention to word order in comparative structures with modal verbs.

    TRKarşılaştırmada fiil sırasına dikkat edilmelidir.

Compare
prefer vs would rather vs comparative/superlative
prefer / would rathercomparative / superlative
Usegeneral or specific preference between optionsI'd rather watch a classic tonight.direct comparison of two or more thingsThe book is more thought-provoking than the film.
Formprefer + noun/-ing (+ to); would rather + base verb (+ than)I prefer reading to watching.adjective + -er/more … + than; the + adjective + -est/most …the most gripping story I've ever read

A Life Behind the Camera

Long before he became known for his dreamlike animated worlds, the director grew up in a quiet town far from any film studio. As a boy, he used to spend hours drawing his own comic strips instead of doing his homework, and his teachers often complained that he never paid attention in class. Nobody in his family had worked in film before, so when he announced that he wanted to make movies, his parents were quietly worried.

His first years in the industry were far from glamorous. While he was working as a junior animator, he spent long nights redrawing scenes that senior directors rejected, and he nearly gave up more than once. Everything changed when he was finally allowed to direct a short film of his own. It told a simple story about a lonely girl and a magical forest, but audiences who saw it were deeply moved, and critics praised its gentle, thoughtful pace.

Decades later, his films are studied in universities and loved by children and adults alike. He still insists that every frame should be drawn by hand whenever possible, even in an age of computer animation. When a journalist once asked why he never used more modern techniques, he simply replied that a story feels more honest when you can see the artist's hand behind it.

What Makes a Story a Classic?

Every year, thousands of new novels and films are released, yet only a small number are still discussed decades later. So what separates a passing bestseller from a genuine classic? One answer lies in the characters themselves. A classic protagonist usually has flaws as well as strengths, which makes them feel believable rather than perfect. Readers remember characters who make difficult choices, not ones who always know exactly what to do.

Setting plays an important role too. Many classic stories are set in a specific time and place, yet they explore ideas that go far beyond that setting — loyalty, ambition, loss, forgiveness. A short story about a fisherman on a remote island can still say something true about any reader's own life, wherever and whenever they happen to be reading it.

Finally, a classic often rewards a second reading or a second viewing. On the first read, we usually focus on the plot: what happens next? On the second, we start to notice smaller details — a narrator who isn't entirely honest, an antagonist who has understandable reasons for their actions, a piece of dialogue that means something different once we know how the story ends. It's this depth, more than fame or sales figures, that keeps a story alive.
