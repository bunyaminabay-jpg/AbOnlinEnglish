Transitive vs Intransitive Phrasal Verbs

1 · Notice
  • Last year, my cousin decided to take up parkour after watching videos online.
  • The new skater didn't want to show off, but everyone was impressed anyway.
  • It took weeks to break in my new climbing boots before the expedition.
  • After ten years of extreme sports, the champion finally settled down and opened a training school.

In which sentences does the phrasal verb need an object (something after it), and in which sentences does it stand alone?

C Your turn

Write four sentences about sports you or someone you know has taken up, given up, shown off in, or how people get along in a team.

I once took up…I would never give up…My friend always shows off when…Our team gets along…
Rule

Transitive phrasal verbs need an object (take up a sport, set up a camp, check out the equipment, give up a hobby, break in new boots); some can be separated (take a hobby up). Intransitive phrasal verbs are used without an object (show off, settle down, get along, give in, catch on).

TRGeçişli (transitive) phrasal verb'ler bir nesneye ihtiyaç duyar (take up a sport, set up a camp, check out the equipment, give up a hobby, break in new boots). Bazıları ayrılabilir (take a hobby up). Geçişsiz (intransitive) phrasal verb'ler ise nesne almadan tek başına kullanılır (show off, settle down, get along, give in, catch on).

When?

We use these phrasal verbs to talk about starting or stopping a sport or hobby, checking equipment, or describing how teammates behave.

TRBir sporu ya da hobiyi anlatırken, ekipmanı incelerken ya da bir takım arkadaşlığını tarif ederken bu phrasal verb'leri kullanırız.

3 · Form

Positive

  • She took up climbing last year.
  • He always shows off after a win.

Negative

  • They didn't give up, even in the storm.
  • He doesn't get along with every teammate.

Question

  • Did you ever take up a dangerous sport?
  • Does the team get along well?

Short answers

  • Yes, I did. / No, I never did.
  • Yes, they do. / No, not really.
Mistakes
  • She took up it two years ago.She took it up two years ago.

    When the object is a pronoun (it, him, her), a separable phrasal verb must be split.

    TRNesne bir zamir (it, him, her) olduğunda, ayrılabilir phrasal verb'ler mutlaka ayrılmalıdır.

  • He showed off his new board to everyone.He showed off to everyone with his new board. / He showed off in front of everyone.

    'Show off' is intransitive; it doesn't take a direct object — use it with a preposition like 'to' or 'in front of'.

    TR'Show off' geçişsizdir; doğrudan nesne almaz, 'to/in front of' gibi bir edatla kullanılır.

  • The wrestler didn't give in the match.The wrestler didn't give in during the match.

    'Give in' is intransitive and takes no direct object; use a preposition like 'during' or 'to' instead.

    TR'Give in' geçişsizdir, doğrudan nesne almaz; 'during/to' gibi edatlarla kullanılır.

C Your turn

Write four sentences about sports: one general fact, one current trend or news item, and two sentences about something you or your friends are doing this week related to sport.

Extreme sports generally…Right now, more people…This week, I am…At the moment, our class…
Rule

Use the Simple Present for general facts, habits and regularly repeated sporting events. Use the Present Progressive (be + -ing) for something happening right now, or a current trend/news item.

TRGenel gerçekler, alışkanlıklar ve düzenli olarak tekrarlanan spor etkinlikleri için Simple Present kullanılır. Şu anda olan bir eylem ya da güncel bir trend/haber için Present Progressive (be + -ing) kullanılır.

When?

Use the Simple Present to describe a sport in general or when a traditional event takes place; use the Present Progressive for an action happening now or a current sports trend.

TRBir sporu genel olarak tanımlarken ya da geleneksel bir etkinliğin ne zaman yapıldığını anlatırken Simple Present; şu anda olan bir hareketi ya da güncel bir spor trendini anlatırken Present Progressive kullanırız.

Born for the Rush?

Why do some people happily jump out of aeroplanes or ride down a mountain on a board, while others would never dream of it? Decades ago, a psychologist proposed that certain people are simply wired to seek out risk. He called them 'Type T' individuals — the 'T' standing for thrill. According to his theory, these people don't just tolerate danger; they actively look for it.

Scientists who study this idea point to biology as one possible explanation. Type T brains may release dopamine, the chemical linked to pleasure, more strongly during risky activities than an average person's brain does. This could explain why a thrill-seeker's heart races with excitement, not fear, the moment a parachute opens.

Critics warn against judging every risk-taker as reckless, however. Many so-called Type T people take up creative or entrepreneurial paths instead of dangerous sports, channelling the same hunger for novelty into painting, invention or starting a business. Whether on a cliff edge or in a studio, it seems the same restless curiosity can drive very different kinds of lives.

Oil, Honour and Forty Springs

Every July, thousands of spectators travel to Edirne to watch one of the oldest sporting events in the world. Wrestlers, known as pehlivan, cover their bodies in olive oil and compete on open grass in leather trousers called a kispet. The tournament, known as Kırkpınar, has been held almost every year since 1924, making it a proud symbol of Turkish sporting heritage.

The event's name comes from an old legend. Two brothers are said to have wrestled each other for so long, and so evenly matched, that neither could win — until, exhausted, they both collapsed and died on the spot. According to the story, forty springs later bubbled up from the ground where they fell, giving the region its name: Kırkpınar, or 'Forty Springs'.

Beyond the oil and the crowds, Kırkpınar is built on respect. Wrestlers bow to their opponents before a match, and the strongest competitor, the başpehlivan, is expected to show humility, not arrogance. Similar values appear in other traditional Turkish sports, such as the horseback javelin game, where courage and fair play matter as much as winning.
