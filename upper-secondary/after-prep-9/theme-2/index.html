TR'Always' ile Present Progressive kullanıldığında, sık tekrar eden ve genellikle konuşanı rahatsız eden ya da şaşırtan bir alışkanlığı anlatır (nötr bir alışkanlıktan farklıdır). TRBir arkadaşının sürekli tekrar eden ve seni rahatsız eden bir davranışından şikayet ederken 'always + -ing' kullanırız; bir bilgiyi teyit etmek istediğimizde question tag ekleriz.

C Your turn

Write four sentences about how you or your classmates do things at school, using at least four different adverbs (regular and irregular).

Rule

Most adjectives add -ly to become adverbs (quick→quickly). Adjectives ending in -y change to -ily (easy→easily). Some adjectives keep the same form as adverbs (hard, fast, late). 'Good' has an irregular adverb: well.

TRÇoğu sıfat sonuna -ly eklenerek zarf olur (quick→quickly). -y ile biten sıfatlarda y, i'ye dönüşür ve -ly eklenir (easy→easily). Bazı sıfatlar zarf olarak da aynı kalır (hard, fast, late). 'Good' sıfatının zarfı düzensizdir: well.

When?

We use adverbs to describe HOW an action is done — how someone answered a question, gave a presentation, or completed a task.

TRBir eylemin NASIL yapıldığını anlatırken (bir sunumu nasıl yaptın, bir soruyu nasıl cevapladın gibi) zarf kullanırız.

  • She answered very quick.She answered very quickly.

    To describe how she answered (a verb), you need the adverb quickly, not the adjective quick.

Rule

'Either… or…' expresses a choice between two positive options. 'Neither… nor…' shows that two things are both not true (the verb stays positive because the negative idea is already in neither/nor). Elliptical short responses use 'So + auxiliary + subject' to agree with a positive statement, and 'Neither/Nor + auxiliary + subject' to agree with a negative one, instead of repeating the whole sentence.

TR'Either… or…' iki olumlu seçenekten birini ifade eder. 'Neither… nor…' ise iki şeyin de doğru/mevcut olmadığını anlatır (fiil olumlu formda kalır çünkü olumsuzluk zaten neither/nor'da vardır). Elliptical (kısa) yanıtlar, bir cümleyi tekrar etmek yerine 'So + yardımcı fiil + özne' (olumlu katılım) ya da 'Neither/Nor + yardımcı fiil + özne' (olumsuz katılım) ile aynı fikirde olduğumuzu kısaca gösterir.

When?

Use these structures to present two options (either…or), to say two things are both not true (neither…nor), or to briefly agree with what someone just said (So do I / Neither do I).

TRİki seçenek sunarken (either…or), iki şeyin de doğru/mevcut olmadığını belirtirken (neither…nor), ya da bir arkadaşının söylediğine kısaca katılırken (So do I / Neither do I) bu yapıları kullanırız.

Two Students, Two Favourite Subjects

Deniz has always loved Biology. Every week, her class does at least one hands-on activity — dissecting a flower, growing bacteria in a petri dish, or building a model of a cell. 'I remember facts much better when I've actually done something with my hands,' she explains. 'If a teacher just talks for forty minutes, I switch off after ten.' Deniz also keeps a set of flashcards with labelled diagrams, which she reviews for ten minutes every evening rather than cramming before a test.

Mira, on the other hand, is far more excited by Coding, an elective course she chose in Year 9. She enjoys the logical, step-by-step nature of writing a programme, and she likes that mistakes are simply part of the process — a wrong line of code just produces an error message, not a bad grade. Mira mostly studies using online tutorials and a mind-mapping app, which helps her see how different commands connect to each other before she starts typing.

Although Deniz and Mira are interested in completely different subjects, their stories share an important lesson: a subject feels enjoyable when the learning activities match the way a person naturally thinks. Some students learn best by doing, others by connecting ideas visually, and others by listening and discussing. Recognising your own learning style — and choosing study tools that fit it — can turn even a difficult subject into one you look forward to.

A Guide to Smart Study Tools

Walk into almost any classroom today and you will find a mix of old and new study tools working side by side. Alongside the familiar exercise book and paper textbook, many students now use a digital textbook that highlights key vocabulary automatically, or a flashcard app that shows them a word again just before they are likely to forget it. Teachers, too, increasingly rely on an interactive whiteboard to display diagrams, videos and quizzes that the whole class can respond to together.

One especially useful innovation is spaced repetition, the method behind many flashcard apps. Instead of reviewing everything the night before a test, students see each fact several times over a period of days or weeks, with longer gaps between each review. Research suggests that this approach helps information move from short-term to long-term memory far more effectively than last-minute cramming. Mind maps work on a similar principle: by connecting ideas visually, they make it easier to recall a whole topic rather than isolated facts.

However, no tool can replace regular practice and genuine understanding. A flashcard app can help you memorise vocabulary, but it cannot teach you to write a full paragraph; an interactive whiteboard can make a lesson more engaging, but students still need to take notes and think for themselves. The most effective students are usually not the ones with the most expensive gadgets, but the ones who choose a small number of tools that suit their own learning style and use them consistently.
