C Your turn

Write three zero conditional sentences about scientific facts related to space, technology or the environment.

If you...If there is/isn't...When scientists...
Rule

The zero conditional describes general truths and scientific facts that are always true. Both clauses use the Simple Present: If + Simple Present, Simple Present.

TRZero conditional, her zaman doğru olan genel gerçekleri ve bilimsel kuralları anlatmak için kullanılır. Her iki cümlecikte de Simple Present kullanılır: If + Simple Present, Simple Present.

When?

We use the zero conditional for scientific facts, laws of nature and results that are always true.

TRBilimsel gerçekleri, doğa kurallarını ve her zaman geçerli olan sonuçları anlatırken zero conditional kullanırız.

3 · Form

Positive

  • If you heat water, it boils.
  • Objects float if there is no gravity.

Negative

  • If a planet has no atmosphere, it doesn't support life.
  • Plants don't grow if they don't get light.

Question

  • What happens if an astronaut doesn't wear a suit?
  • Does ice melt if you heat it?

Short answers

  • Yes, it does. / No, it doesn't.
  • It floats. / It melts.
Mistakes
  • If a planet has no atmosphere, it will not support life.If a planet has no atmosphere, it does not support life.

    The zero conditional never uses 'will'; both clauses take the Simple Present.

    TRZero conditional'da 'will' kullanılmaz; iki cümlecik de Simple Present olmalıdır.

  • If you heated water, it boils.If you heat water, it boils.

    The if-clause also needs the Simple Present, not a past tense.

    TRIf cümleciğinde de Simple Present kullanılmalıdır, geçmiş zaman değil.

First Conditional (Real Future Possibilities)

1 · Notice
  • If scientists find a habitable planet, humanity will have a new home.
  • We will solve the overpopulation problem if we develop better technology.
  • If we don't reduce our resource consumption, future generations will suffer.
  • If the mission is successful, astronauts will land on Mars by 2040.

Are these situations real possibilities for the future, or impossible dreams? What tense follows 'if', and what tense is in the main clause?

C Your turn

Write three first conditional sentences predicting real possibilities for the future of technology or space exploration.

If scientists...If we don't...If humans...
Rule

The first conditional describes a real, likely situation in the future and its result. If + Simple Present, will + base verb.

TRFirst conditional, gelecekte gerçekleşmesi mümkün olan durumlar ve sonuçları için kullanılır. If + Simple Present, will + fiil.

When?

We use the first conditional to talk about realistic future predictions, plans and likely results.

TRGelecekle ilgili gerçekçi tahminler, planlar ve olası sonuçlar hakkında konuşurken first conditional kullanırız.

3 · Form

Positive

  • If we develop better technology, we will solve the problem.
  • Astronauts will land on Mars if the mission is successful.

Negative

  • If we don't act now, the climate crisis will get worse.
  • We won't find life if we don't keep exploring.

Question

  • What will happen if we discover a new planet?
  • Will humans live on Mars if it becomes habitable?

Short answers

  • Yes, they will. / No, they won't.
  • It will get worse. / We won't.
Mistakes
  • If we will develop better technology, we will solve the problem.If we develop better technology, we will solve the problem.

    Never use 'will' in the if-clause; use the Simple Present instead.

    TRIf cümleciğinde 'will' kullanılmaz, Simple Present kullanılır.

  • If scientists find a new planet, they named it quickly.If scientists find a new planet, they will name it quickly.

    The main clause needs 'will' to express a future result.

    TRAna cümlede gelecek sonuç için 'will' gerekir.

C Your turn

Write three second conditional sentences imagining unlikely or impossible future situations related to space or technology.

If I were...If we lived on...If scientists discovered...
Rule

The second conditional describes unreal, imaginary or unlikely present/future situations. If + Simple Past, would + base verb. With I/he/she/it, formal English often prefers 'were' instead of 'was'.

TRSecond conditional, gerçek olmayan, hayali ya da gerçekleşmesi pek olası olmayan şimdiki/gelecekteki durumlar için kullanılır. If + Simple Past, would + fiil. 'I/he/she/it' ile resmi İngilizcede 'was' yerine 'were' tercih edilir.

When?

We use the second conditional to imagine impossible or very unlikely future scenarios.

TRHayal kurarken, imkansız ya da çok düşük ihtimalli gelecek senaryolarını tartışırken second conditional kullanırız.

Positive

  2. scientists not know / more about distant galaxies (now)
    Model answer

C Your turn

Write three wish sentences: one about the present (something you want to be different now), one about the past (something you regret), and one using 'wish...could'.

I wish...I wish I had/hadn't...I wish I could...
Rule

Wish + Simple Past describes a present situation we want to be different. Wish + Past Perfect describes a past situation we regret. Wish + could + base verb expresses a present wish for ability or possibility.

TRWish + Simple Past, şimdiki zamanda farklı olmasını istediğimiz bir durumu anlatır. Wish + Past Perfect, geçmişte pişmanlık duyduğumuz bir durumu anlatır. Wish + could + fiil, şu anki bir yetenek ya da imkan isteğini anlatır.

When?

We use wish clauses when we are unhappy with a present situation, or when we regret something we did or didn't do in the past.

TRŞu anki durumdan memnun olmadığımızda ya da geçmişte farklı bir şey yapmış olmayı dilediğimizde wish cümlelerini kullanırız.

3 · Form

Positive

  • I wish we had cleaner energy.
  • I wish humanity had discovered a habitable planet.

Negative

  • I wish we didn't waste so much water.
  • She wishes she hadn't ignored the warnings.

Question

  • What do you wish scientists knew?
  • Do you wish you could travel to space?

Short answers

  • I wish I could. / I don't really wish that.
  • Yes, I do. / No, not really.
Mistakes
  • I wish we have cleaner energy.I wish we had cleaner energy.

    For a present wish, use the Simple Past after 'wish', not the Simple Present.

    TRŞimdiki durum için wish'ten sonra Simple Past kullanılır, Simple Present değil.

  • I wish humanity discovered a planet years ago.I wish humanity had discovered a planet years ago.

    For a past regret, use the Past Perfect after 'wish'.

    TRGeçmişteki pişmanlık için Past Perfect kullanılır.

Compare

Compare the time reference: 'I wish we had cleaner energy' (present wish, Simple Past) vs 'I wish we had invested in clean energy years ago' (past regret, Past Perfect) vs 'I wish I could visit space' (present wish for ability, could + base verb).

The Hunt for a Second Earth

For centuries, humans have looked up at the night sky and wondered whether we are alone in the universe. Today, that question is closer to being answered than ever before. Using powerful space telescopes, astronomers have identified thousands of exoplanets — planets that orbit stars outside our solar system — and a growing number of them lie in what scientists call the 'habitable zone', the narrow distance from a star where temperatures allow liquid water to exist.

Finding an exoplanet is only the first step. To know whether a planet could actually support life, researchers study its atmosphere, its size, and the type of star it orbits. If a planet is too close to its star, it will be far too hot; if it is too far away, any water on its surface will freeze. Even a planet in the habitable zone might lack a protective atmosphere, leaving it exposed to deadly radiation. Because of these strict conditions, scientists estimate that only a small fraction of the exoplanets discovered so far could realistically host life.

Even so, the search continues, and new missions are launched almost every year. Some researchers believe that if we keep improving our telescopes and analysis methods, we will find strong evidence of a genuinely habitable world within the next few decades. Others are more cautious, arguing that space is so vast that finding the right planet — let alone actual life on it — could still take much longer. Either way, the hunt for a second Earth has changed the way many people think about humanity's place in the universe.

Life in 2126: Three Predictions

What will daily life look like a century from now? No one can predict the future with certainty, but many scientists, engineers and futurists agree on a few likely directions, based on trends that are already visible today.

The first prediction concerns energy and the environment. If humanity continues to rely on fossil fuels, the climate crisis will almost certainly get worse, with more extreme weather and rising sea levels. Many experts believe, however, that renewable energy will become the main power source worldwide well before 2126, driven by both necessity and rapidly falling costs. Cities may be redesigned to be almost completely self-sufficient, generating their own energy and recycling nearly all their waste.

The second prediction involves artificial intelligence and automation. Machines already perform many tasks once done only by humans, from analysing medical scans to writing simple reports. If this trend continues, entire industries could look completely different, and people may spend far more time on creative, social or scientific work rather than routine tasks. Finally, many researchers expect real progress in space technology: permanent research bases on the Moon, regular cargo flights to Mars, and perhaps even the first small human settlements beyond Earth. Whether these predictions come true exactly as described, one thing seems certain — the next century will bring changes as dramatic as anything in the last hundred years.
