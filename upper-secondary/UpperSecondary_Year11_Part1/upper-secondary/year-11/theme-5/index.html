This theme explores unreal past situations — imagining how the past could have been different and expressing regrets about things we did or did not do.

Konular

  • Expressing wishes and regrets for past events
  • Talking about unreal past events

Dil yapıları

  • Third Conditional (unreal past)

Anlayabileceğim

  • I can understand people talking about how their past could have been different
  • I can understand someone expressing regret about a past decision
  • I can understand a story that imagines an alternative version of past events
  • I can understand the difference between a real past event and an imagined one
  • I can understand spoken reflections on missed opportunities
  • I can understand written texts about turning points in someone's life

Söyleyebileceğim

  • I can talk about how a past situation could have turned out differently
  • I can express regret about something I did or didn't do
  • I can imagine an unreal result of a past action
  • I can discuss a historical 'what if' scenario with a partner
  • I can give advice by referring to what someone should have done
  • I can compare my imagined past with what actually happened

Yazabileceğim

  • I can write sentences using the third conditional to describe unreal past results
  • I can write about a regret using wish + past perfect
  • I can write a short reflective paragraph about a turning point in my life
  • I can write an imagined alternative ending to a real past event

Third Conditional (unreal past)

1 · Notice
  • If she had studied harder, she would have passed the exam.
  • They wouldn't have missed the bus if they had left earlier.
  • If I had known about the meeting, I could have joined it.
  • He might have won the race if he hadn't fallen.

What do you notice about the verb forms in both parts of these sentences, and did these things really happen?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Complete the sentences with the correct third conditional form.

had leftwould have arrivedhadn't rainedwould have gonehad rememberedcould have called

Well done! You're noticing how both clauses look back at unreal past situations.

B Build sentences

Build a full third conditional sentence from each set of cues.

  1. I / study / harder I / pass / the test

    ✓ If I had studied harder, I would have passed the test.

  2. they / not lose / the map they / not get / lost
    Model answer

    If they hadn't lost the map, they wouldn't have got lost.

  3. he / listen / to the warning he / avoid / the accident
    Model answer

    If he had listened to the warning, he could have avoided the accident.

  4. we / book / earlier we / get / better seats
    Model answer

    If we had booked earlier, we would have got better seats.

  5. she / not forget / her passport she / catch / the flight
    Model answer

    If she hadn't forgotten her passport, she could have caught the flight.

  6. I / know / the truth I / act / differently
    Model answer

    If I had known the truth, I might have acted differently.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original third conditional sentences about a real past event that could have gone differently.

If I had ...If my school hadn't ...We would have ... if ...
Rule

We use the third conditional (If + past perfect, ... would/could/might + have + past participle) to talk about an imaginary result of a past situation that did not actually happen.

TRÜçüncü koşul cümlesini (If + past perfect, ... would/could/might + have + past participle) geçmişte gerçekleşmemiş bir durumun hayali sonucundan bahsetmek için kullanırız.

When?

We use it to imagine a different past: a situation that was untrue combined with an unreal result, often to explain regret or an alternative version of events.

TRGerçek olmayan bir geçmişi hayal etmek için kullanılır: gerçek dışı bir durum ile hayali bir sonuç birleştirilerek genellikle pişmanlığı veya olayların alternatif bir versiyonunu anlatmak için kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • If + subject + had + past participle, subject + would/could/might + have + past participle.

Negative

  • If + subject + hadn't + past participle, subject + wouldn't + have + past participle.

Question

  • Would/Could + subject + have + past participle if + subject + had + past participle?

Short answers

  • Yes, he would have. / No, she wouldn't have.
Mistakes
  • If I would have known, I would have told you.If I had known, I would have told you.

    Do not use 'would have' in the if-clause; use had + past participle instead.

    TRIf-cümleciğinde 'would have' kullanılmaz; onun yerine had + past participle kullanılır.

  • If she had study, she would have passed.If she had studied, she would have passed.

    The verb after 'had' must be in its past participle form.

    TR'had'dan sonraki fiil past participle biçiminde olmalıdır.

Compare
Second vs. Third Conditional
Second Conditional (unreal present/future)Third Conditional (unreal past)
Timepresent/futureIf I had more money, I would travel more.pastIf I had had more money, I would have travelled more.
Form of if-clausepast simpleIf she studied harder,past perfectIf she had studied harder,

Wish + Past Perfect (regrets)

1 · Notice
  • I wish I had listened to my parents' advice.
  • She wishes she hadn't sold her old guitar.
  • We wish we had visited that museum when we had the chance.
  • He wishes he had asked for help sooner.

What do you notice about the verb form after 'wish', and what feeling does this structure express about the past?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Complete the sentences with wish + past perfect.

had takenhadn't missedhad toldhadn't lost

Great! You're using wish to express real regrets about the past.

B Build sentences

Turn each regret into a full wish + past perfect sentence.

  1. I / not save / my money

    ✓ I wish I hadn't spent all my money.

  2. she / not argue / with her best friend
    Model answer

    She wishes she hadn't argued with her best friend.

  3. we / visit / grandma more often
    Model answer

    We wish we had visited grandma more often.

  4. he / not quit / the team
    Model answer

    He wishes he hadn't quit the team.

  5. they / ask / for directions
    Model answer

    They wish they had asked for directions.

C Your turn

Write 2-3 original sentences about your own regrets using wish + past perfect.

I wish I had ...I wish I hadn't ...My friend wishes ...
Rule

We use wish + subject + had + past participle to express regret about something in the past that we cannot change.

TRWish + özne + had + past participle yapısını, değiştiremeyeceğimiz geçmişteki bir durum için pişmanlık ifade etmek amacıyla kullanırız.

When?

We use it when we want to say that we would like something in the past to have been different, unlike how it really was.

TRGeçmişte gerçekte olandan farklı bir şeyin olmasını istediğimizi ifade etmek için kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Subject + wish(es) + subject + had + past participle.

Negative

  • Subject + wish(es) + subject + hadn't + past participle.

Question

  • Do/Does + subject + wish + subject + had + past participle?

Short answers

  • Yes, I do. / No, she doesn't.
Mistakes
  • I wish I would have gone to the party.I wish I had gone to the party.

    Avoid 'would have' after wish; use had + past participle.

    TRWish'ten sonra 'would have' kullanılmaz; had + past participle kullanılır.

  • She wish she had known earlier.She wishes she had known earlier.

    Remember the third person -s on 'wish' with he/she/it.

    TRHe/she/it öznesiyle 'wish' fiiline -s eklenmelidir.

Compare
Wish + past simple vs. Wish + past perfect
Wish + past simple (present regret)Wish + past perfect (past regret)
Time referred tonowI wish I had more free time.the pastI wish I had had more free time last year.

The Letter I Never Sent

When I was seventeen, my best friend Deniz moved to another city with her family. We promised to write letters every week, but somehow life got busy and I never kept my promise. If I had written to her just once a month, our friendship would have survived the distance. Instead, the letters stopped after only a few weeks, and slowly we lost touch completely.

Last year, I found an old, unfinished letter to Deniz in a box under my bed. I had started it years ago but never finished it, and I never sent it. Reading it again, I realised how much I regretted losing such an important friendship. If I hadn't been so lazy about writing back then, we might still be close friends today. I wish I had understood, at seventeen, how rare a good friendship really is.

This year, I finally decided to do something about it. I found Deniz on social media and sent her a message, admitting that I wished I had kept in touch all those years ago. To my surprise, she replied within a day. She told me that she had often wondered what would have happened if either of us had made more effort. We are now planning to meet for coffee next month, twenty years after we last saw each other.

Looking back, I don't think either of us was a bad friend — we were just young and didn't understand how quickly people can drift apart. If we had known then what we know now, we would have made more time for each other. But I've also learned that it's never too late to fix a mistake from the past, even if it takes twenty years to do it.

What If the Bridge Hadn't Been Built?

Every town has a story about a decision that changed everything. In one small coastal town, local leaders had to decide whether to build a bridge connecting it to the mainland. Many people were against the idea; they worried that a bridge would destroy the town's quiet, traditional way of life. If the town council had listened to these voices, the bridge would never have been built.

However, the council decided to go ahead with the project. The bridge opened fifteen years ago, and the town changed completely. Tourists began arriving by car instead of by the old, slow ferry. New hotels, cafés, and shops appeared along the coast. If the bridge hadn't been built, none of this growth would have happened, and the town might still look exactly as it did decades ago.

Not everyone is happy with the changes. Some older residents say that if the town had stayed isolated, it would have kept its peaceful character. They believe the fishing community that once defined the town has been replaced by a tourist economy that cares more about profit than tradition. They often wonder what would have happened if the bridge project had been rejected all those years ago.

Younger residents, on the other hand, tend to see the bridge differently. Many of them would have had to leave the town to find work if the bridge — and the tourism it brought — hadn't been built. For them, the bridge didn't destroy the town; it saved it from slowly disappearing as young people moved away. Both sides agree on one thing: that single decision changed the town's future forever.

SpeakingPairs8-10 min

What If...? History Discussion

In pairs, discuss two 'what if' scenarios about important moments in history or in your country, using the third conditional to imagine different results.

Useful language

If ... hadn't happened, ... would have ...I think things could have been very different because ...That's an interesting point, but what about ...?I wonder what would have happened if ...

Adımlar

  1. Choose one historical or well-known event you both know.
  2. Individually, think of one way the event could have gone differently.
  3. Take turns sharing your 'what if' idea using the third conditional.
  4. Ask your partner at least two follow-up questions about their idea.
  5. Together, agree on the most interesting 'what if' scenario and prepare to share it with the class.
DestekGive students a short list of well-known historical events to choose from, plus sentence starters on the board.
HedefStudents create at least two original third conditional sentences per event without prompts.
İleriStudents extend the discussion to a chain of consequences (e.g. 'If X hadn't happened, then Y wouldn't have happened, and then Z...').
SpeakingGroups of 3-410 min

My Biggest 'What If'

Take turns sharing a personal regret or a 'what if' moment from your own life, using wish + past perfect and the third conditional, while the group listens and reacts.

Useful language

I wish I had/hadn't ...If I had ..., my life would have been ...That sounds like a tough decision.Do you think you'll ever get another chance?

Adımlar

  1. Think privately of one small regret you feel comfortable sharing (school, sports, friendships, hobbies).
  2. In your group, take turns sharing your regret using at least one target structure.
  3. After each person speaks, the group asks one follow-up question.
  4. As a group, decide whose 'what if' story was the most surprising.
  5. Share your group's most surprising story with the whole class.
DestekProvide a model regret sentence and allow students to talk about a general topic rather than a deeply personal one.
HedefStudents share one full regret with a follow-up explanation using both target grammar points.
İleriStudents give their group members spoken advice using 'You should have...' in response to each regret.
Writing120-150 words

A Turning Point: What If It Had Been Different?

Write a short reflective paragraph about a real turning point in your life (or in the life of someone you know) and imagine how things could have been different, using the third conditional.

Plan

  1. Choose one real turning point (a decision, an event, a meeting).
  2. Briefly describe what actually happened.
  3. Write 2-3 third conditional sentences imagining a different result.
  4. End with one sentence about how you feel about the real outcome now.
