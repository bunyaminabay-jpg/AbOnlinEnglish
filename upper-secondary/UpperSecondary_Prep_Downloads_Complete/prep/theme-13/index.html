In Theme 13, you will explore Life in the Neighbourhood, City and Social Life: cultural and historical places, festivals and events, and problems in the neighbourhood and city. You will learn to talk about rules and improvements with passive voice and modals, make suggestions and offers with Shall, and use can/might for polite requests and probability.

Passive Voice with Modals

1 · Notice
  • This old building must be protected.
  • The streets should be cleaned every day.
  • A new park can be built here.
  • Litter mustn't be dropped in the square.

What comes after the modal verb (must, should, can)? Is it important here who does the action?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Look at the Notice sentences again. Complete the rule with the words in the box.

be + past participlemodal verbby + agentaction

Well done! You found the rule yourself. Now let's use it.

B Build sentences

Use the cues to write passive sentences with modals about your city. Look at the example first.

  1. this building must protect

    ✓ This building must be protected.

  2. the streets should clean every day
    Model answer

    The streets should be cleaned every day.

  3. a new park can build here
    Model answer

    A new park can be built here.

  4. litter mustn't drop in the square
    Model answer

    Litter mustn't be dropped in the square.

  5. old monuments should restore carefully
    Model answer

    Old monuments should be restored carefully.

  6. ? / the museum can visit for free
    Model answer

    Can the museum be visited for free?

C Your turn

Write three passive sentences with modals about problems or rules in your own neighbourhood. Use must/should/can.

In my neighbourhood, litter mustn't …The park should …A new … can be built …
Rule

We use modal + be + past participle (must/should/can/mustn't be + V3) to focus on the action, not on who does it. It is common for city rules, recommendations and projects.

TRModal + edilgen yapı (must/should/can/mustn't + be + fiilin 3. hâli) bir eylemin kim tarafından yapıldığından çok, neyin yapılması gerektiğine ya da mümkün olduğuna odaklanmak için kullanılır. Belediye kuralları, öneriler ve şehir projeleri için sık kullanılır.

When?

We use it for city rules, suggestions, projects, and situations where who does the action is not important.

TRŞehir kuralları, öneriler, projeler ve kimin yaptığının önemli olmadığı durumlarda kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • This building must be protected.
  • New parks can be built here.

Negative

  • Litter mustn't be dropped in the square.
  • This monument shouldn't be damaged.

Question

  • Can the museum be visited for free?
  • Should old buildings be restored?

Short answers

  • Yes, it can. / No, it can't.
  • Yes, they should. / No, they shouldn't.
Mistakes
  • This building must protect.This building must be protected.

    Don't forget 'be' after the modal: must + be + V3.

    TRModal'dan sonra edilgen için 'be' unutulmamalı.

  • The park can cleaning every week.The park can be cleaned every week.

    Use the past participle (V3), not the -ing form, after be.

    TRFiilin -ing hâli değil, 3. hâli (past participle) kullanılır.

Compare

Aktif: 'The council must clean the streets.' (kim yaptığı önemli). Pasif: 'The streets must be cleaned.' (eylem önemli, kim yaptığı önemli değil ya da bilinmiyor).

Shall: Suggestions and Offers

1 · Notice
  • Shall we visit the old castle this weekend?
  • Shall I book the tickets for the festival?
  • Shall we meet at the fountain at six?
  • Shall I carry your bag?

Who makes the suggestion or offer — the speaker alone, or the speaker and listener together?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Look at the Notice sentences again. Complete the rule with the words in the box.

Shall weShall Isuggestionoffer

Well done! You found the rule yourself. Now let's use it.

B Build sentences

Use the cues to make suggestions with Shall we…? or offers with Shall I…? Look at the example first.

  1. visit the old castle this weekend

    ✓ Shall we visit the old castle this weekend?

  2. book the tickets
    Model answer

    Shall I book the tickets?

  3. meet at the fountain at six
    Model answer

    Shall we meet at the fountain at six?

  4. carry your bag
    Model answer

    Shall I carry your bag?

  5. walk to the festival together
    Model answer

C Your turn

Write two suggestions with Shall we…? for things to do in your city, and one offer with Shall I…?

Shall we …Shall I …
Rule

Shall we…? is used to suggest doing something together. Shall I…? is used to offer to do something for the other person. It sounds friendly and polite.

TRShall we…? birlikte bir şey yapmayı önermek için kullanılır. Shall I…? ise konuşan kişinin karşısındaki için bir şey yapmayı teklif etmesi için kullanılır. Resmî olmayan, arkadaşça bir öneri/teklif ifadesidir.

When?

We use it to suggest visiting a place together, or to offer help to someone.

TRBir yeri birlikte ziyaret etmeyi önerirken ya da birine yardım teklif ederken kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • Shall we meet at six?
  • Shall I open the window for you?

Question

  • Shall we visit the museum?
  • Shall I carry your bag?

Short answers

  • Yes, let's. / No, let's not.
  • Yes, please. / No, thanks, it's fine.
Mistakes
  • Shall we going to the castle?Shall we go to the castle?

    Use the base verb after shall, not the -ing form.

    TRShall'dan sonra fiilin yalın hâli gelir.

  • Shall you help me?Shall I help you?

    Use 'Shall I' to offer help, not 'Shall you'.

    TRYardım teklif ederken 'Shall I' kullanılır, 'Shall you' değil.

Compare

Shall we…? ile Let's…? benzer anlama gelir, ama Shall we…? bir soru gibi nazikçe önerir, Let's…? daha doğrudan bir öneridir: 'Let's go to the museum.'

Can / Might: Polite Requests and Probability

1 · Notice
  • Can I take a photo of the monument, please?
  • Can you tell me the way to the old town, please?
  • It might rain during the outdoor festival.
  • The parade might start a bit late this year.

Which sentences are polite requests? Which sentences talk about something that is possible but not certain?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Look at the Notice sentences again. Complete the rule with the words in the box.

Can I / Can youmightpossiblepolite request

Well done! You found the rule yourself. Now let's use it.

B Build sentences

Decide if you need a polite request with can or a probability sentence with might. Look at the example first.

  1. ask permission take a photo

    ✓ Can I take a photo, please?

  2. ask someone the way to the old town
    Model answer

    Can you tell me the way to the old town, please?

  3. probability rain during the festival
    Model answer

    It might rain during the festival.

  4. probability the parade start late
    Model answer

    The parade might start late.

  5. ask permission open the window
    Model answer

    Can I open the window, please?

C Your turn

Write one polite request with Can I/you…? about visiting a place in your city, and one sentence with might about something that could happen at a festival.

Can I …, please?It might …
Rule

Can I…?/Can you…? are used to politely ask for permission or to make a polite request. Might is used to talk about something that is possible but not certain — a low-to-medium probability.

TRCan I…?/Can you…? izin istemek ya da nazikçe rica etmek için kullanılır. Might ise bir şeyin olması mümkün ama kesin olmadığını, yani düşük-orta ihtimali anlatmak için kullanılır.

When?

We use these to politely ask permission or directions, or to guess what might happen at an event.

TRBir yerde izin isterken, yol tarif ederken ya da bir etkinlikte olabilecek şeyleri tahmin ederken kullanılır.

3 · Form

Positive

  • It might rain during the festival.
  • You can take photos here.

Negative

  • It might not be open on Mondays.
  • You can't enter without a ticket.

Question

  • Can I take a photo, please?
  • Can you tell me the way, please?

Short answers

  • Yes, of course. / I'm sorry, you can't.
  • It might. / It might not.
Mistakes
  • Can I to take a photo?Can I take a photo?

    Don't use 'to' after can.

    TRCan'dan sonra 'to' gelmez.

  • It mights rain later.It might rain later.

    Don't add -s to might; it's a modal verb.

    TRMight'a -s eklenmez, modal fiildir.

Compare

Can (izin/rica) ile might (olasılık) karıştırılmamalı: 'Can I go?' = izin istiyorum. 'It might rain.' = belki yağmur yağar, emin değilim.

A Walk Through the Old Town

Last weekend, my cousin Deren visited our city for the first time, so I took her on a walking tour of the old town. We started at the main square, where a tall stone monument stands. It was built over a hundred years ago, and it must be protected carefully because it is part of our cultural heritage.

From there, we walked to the old bazaar, a busy street full of small shops and cafés. Deren loved the atmosphere. 'Shall we buy something here?' she asked, and we bought some handmade souvenirs from a local craftsman.

Next, we visited the history museum, which has a new exhibition about the city's past. Photos can be taken in most rooms, but Deren asked, 'Can I take a photo of this old map, please?' The guide said yes, but explained that flash photography mustn't be used near the oldest documents.

After the museum, we climbed up to the old castle. The view from the top was amazing, and we could see the whole neighbourhood below. Some parts of the castle are closed because they should be restored before visitors can walk there safely.

By the end of the day, Deren said the old town was definitely worth visiting. 'This landmark might become my favourite place in the city,' she laughed. I was happy she loved a place I see every day.

The Spring Street Festival

Every spring, our neighbourhood holds a street festival to celebrate the end of winter. It's an old tradition that takes place on the first Saturday of April, and almost every family gets involved.

In the morning, a colourful procession moves down the main street. Local schools and dance groups perform traditional dances, and children carry flags and flowers. 'Shall we join the procession this year?' my neighbour asked me, and I said yes.

By midday, the street is full of small stalls selling food, crafts and music. The council always asks residents to help keep the street clean, because litter mustn't be dropped during the festival — there are extra bins everywhere.

In the evening, there is live music in the square, and the festival usually ends with fireworks over the river. The weather forecast said it might rain last year, so many people brought umbrellas just in case, but the sky stayed clear.

For me, the Spring Street Festival is the best event of the year. It brings the whole community together and shows how our traditions can be enjoyed by everyone, old and young.

2 While listening

SelinPerfect. Can you check the opening times online, please?

Discussing Neighbourhood Problems
