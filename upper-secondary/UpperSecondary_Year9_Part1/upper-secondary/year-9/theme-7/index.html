In Theme 7, you will learn about endangered animals like tigers, whales and cheetahs, their natural habitats such as grasslands, oceans and deserts, and the ways people try to protect wildlife. You will learn to talk about the past with 'was/were' and 'there was/there were', and to give advice, recommendations and obligations about protecting nature with 'should'.

Past Simple: was/were & there was/there were

1 · Notice
  • Fifty years ago, there were thousands of tigers in this forest.
  • Ten years ago, this area was a safe habitat for cheetahs.
  • There wasn't a nature reserve here in 1990.
  • Was there a shelter for injured animals last year? — Yes, there was.

Which form goes with singular nouns and which with plural nouns? How do we talk about something existing (or not existing) in the past?

C Your turn

Write three sentences about an animal's habitat in the past, using was/were and there was/there were.

In the past, there was/were...This habitat was...There wasn't...
Rule

'Was/were' is the past form of 'to be': use 'was' with I/he/she/it and 'were' with you/we/they. 'There was/there were' says that something (or an animal) existed in the past: 'there was' for one, 'there were' for more than one.

TR'Was/were' geçmiş zamanda 'to be' fiilinin hâlidir; I/he/she/it ile 'was', you/we/they ile 'were' kullanılır. 'There was/there were' bir şeyin ya da hayvanın geçmişte var olup olmadığını anlatır: tekil için 'there was', çoğul için 'there were'.

When?

We use this to describe what an animal or a natural place was like in the past, and to say whether something existed in the past — for example, describing how a habitat used to be.

TRBir hayvanın veya doğal alanın geçmişte nasıl olduğunu ve geçmişte var olup olmadığını anlatırken kullanırız; örneğin bir habitatın eskiden nasıl olduğunu tarif ederken.

3 · Form

Positive

  • There were many whales in this ocean.
  • This forest was the tiger's habitat.

Negative

  • There wasn't a nature reserve here before.
  • The desert wasn't this dry fifty years ago.

Question

  • Was there a shelter for injured animals?
  • Were there cheetahs in this grassland?

Short answers

  • Yes, there was. / No, there wasn't.
  • Yes, there were. / No, there weren't.
Mistakes
  • There was thousands of tigers.There were thousands of tigers.

    Use 'there were' with plural nouns, not 'there was'.

    TRÇoğul isimle 'there were' kullanılır, 'there was' değil.

  • This forest were a safe habitat.This forest was a safe habitat.

    Use 'was' with a singular subject.

    TRTekil özneyle 'was' kullanılır.

Should: advice, recommendation and obligation

1 · Notice
  • We should protect endangered animals like tigers and whales.
  • Poachers shouldn't hunt endangered species illegally.
  • You should report illegal hunting to the local authorities.
  • Should we build more nature reserves? — Yes, we should.

Which sentences give advice or a recommendation? Which show that something is (or isn't) the right thing to do? How do we make 'should' negative and a question?

B Build sentences

Use the cues to write sentences with should/shouldn't about protecting wildlife. Look at the example first.

    ✓ We should protect endangered animals like tigers and whales.

  2. poachers not / hunt endangered species illegally

  3. you report illegal hunting to the local authorities

  4. ? / we build more nature reserves

C Your turn

Write three sentences with should/shouldn't giving advice about protecting an endangered animal or its habitat.

We should...People shouldn't...Governments should...
Rule

We use 'should' to give advice, make a recommendation or talk about an obligation — 'ought to'. Negative: shouldn't. Question: Should + subject + base verb? Always use the base verb after should/shouldn't.

TR'Should' tavsiye, öneri veya bir yükümlülük belirtmek için kullanılır; 'gerekir/-meli/-malı' anlamı taşır. Olumsuzda 'shouldn't', soruda 'should' özneden önce gelir. Should'dan sonra fiilin yalın hâli kullanılır.

When?

We use this to give people or governments advice about protecting wildlife and habitats, and to say what is or isn't the right thing to do.

TRİnsanlara veya hükümetlere yaban hayatını ve habitatları korumaları için tavsiye verirken, doğru ya da yanlış davranışları belirtirken kullanırız.

3 · Form

Positive

  • We should protect this species.
  • Governments should build more reserves.

Negative

  • Poachers shouldn't hunt illegally.
  • You shouldn't disturb wild animals.

Question

  • Should we report illegal hunting?
  • Should people buy products from endangered animals?

Short answers

  • Yes, we should. / No, we shouldn't.
  • Yes, you should. / No, you shouldn't.
Mistakes
  • We should to protect endangered animals.We should protect endangered animals.

    Don't use 'to' after should.

    TRShould'dan sonra 'to' gelmez.

  • He shoulds report illegal hunting.He should report illegal hunting.

    Should never takes -s, even with he/she/it.

    TRShould, he/she/it ile de değişmez, -s almaz.

Saving the Bengal Tiger

A hundred years ago, there were more than 100,000 tigers living in the wild across Asia. Today, there are only a few thousand left, and the Bengal tiger is one of the most endangered big cats in the world.

Bengal tigers live in grasslands and forests, mostly in India, Bangladesh and Nepal. Fifty years ago, their habitat was much bigger, and there were plenty of grasslands and forests for them to hunt in. Today, much of that habitat has disappeared because of farming and new towns.

Illegal hunting is another serious threat. Poachers sometimes kill tigers illegally and sell their skin and bones, even though this is against the law almost everywhere. Climate change is also a growing threat, because it changes the grasslands and forests where tigers live.

Conservation groups are working hard to help. They protect large areas of grassland as nature reserves, where tigers can live safely. In some reserves, there weren't many tigers ten years ago, but numbers are slowly growing again thanks to this protection.

Experts say that everyone should help protect the Bengal tiger. Governments should create more nature reserves, and people shouldn't buy products made from endangered animals. If we protect their habitat now, future generations should still be able to see wild tigers.

A Diary from Green Valley Nature Reserve

Monday, 14 September — Today our class visited Green Valley Nature Reserve, and it was an unforgettable day! Our guide, Ms Aylin, told us that fifty years ago, this whole area was open grassland, and there were cheetahs and other wild animals living here freely.

We started with a short talk about the reserve's history. Ms Aylin explained that there wasn't a proper shelter for injured animals here until 2015, when the reserve built a new animal hospital. Now the shelter helps hundreds of animals every year.

Next, we walked along a nature trail and saw the reserve's small lake, where migrating birds stop to rest. Ms Aylin said that this species of bird was almost extinct thirty years ago, but thanks to conservation work, there are now hundreds of them again.

In the afternoon, a ranger explained why illegal hunting is such a big problem for wildlife, and why poachers are a serious threat to endangered species. She said that visitors should always stay on the paths and shouldn't feed the wild animals, because it can be dangerous for both people and animals.

By the end of the day, I understood something important: we should all do our part to protect wildlife, whether that means supporting a nature reserve, reporting illegal hunting, or simply learning more about the animals that share our planet.

A Class Trip to the Nature Reserve

2 While listening

Transkripti göster

DenizThat trip to Green Valley Nature Reserve was amazing! I didn't know there were cheetahs so close to our town.

ElifI know! Ms Aylin said that fifty years ago, this whole area was open grassland, full of wild animals.

DenizReally? So there weren't any farms or roads here back then?

ElifNo, there weren't. It was all natural habitat. Now only the reserve protects that grassland.

DenizI liked the animal shelter too. Was there always a shelter like that?

ElifNo, there wasn't — it opened in 2015. Before that, injured animals didn't have anywhere safe to go.

DenizWe should visit again next year. Maybe we can join a conservation project.

ElifGood idea! I think we should ask our teacher about that tomorrow.

Choose a habitat (real or invented, for creative-speaking purposes) and describe how it was in the past and what it is like now, using was/were and there was/there were.
