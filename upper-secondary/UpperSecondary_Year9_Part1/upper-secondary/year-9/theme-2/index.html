In Theme 2, you will meet classmates at a new school, learn about friendship, and talk about daily and study routines and habits. You will learn to describe routines and habits with the Simple Present Tense (I wake up / she revises), and to use time expressions (every day, often, usually) and adverbs of manner (slowly, quickly, carefully) to say how and when you do things.

Simple Present Tense: Daily and Study Routines

1 · Notice
  • I wake up at seven every morning.
  • Zeynep revises her notes every evening.
  • We don't have breakfast together on school days.
  • He doesn't plan his homework before dinner.

Which sentences are about 'I/we' and which are about 'he/she'? What changes on the verb with he/she? How is the negative different for he/she?

Write four sentences about your own daily or study routine: two affirmative and two negative.

Rule

We use the Simple Present Tense to talk about daily and study routines and habits. Use the base verb with I/you/we/they, and add -s/-es with he/she/it. Negative: don't (I/you/we/they), doesn't (he/she/it) + base verb.

TRSimple Present Tense, günlük ve çalışma rutinlerini, alışkanlıkları anlatmak için kullanılır. I/you/we/they ile fiilin yalın hâli, he/she/it ile fiile -s/-es eklenir. Olumsuzda I/you/we/they ile 'don't', he/she/it ile 'doesn't' kullanılır.

When?

We use this to talk about things we do every day or regularly — our daily and study routines and habits.

TRHer gün ya da düzenli olarak yaptığımız şeyleri; günlük ve çalışma rutinlerimizi, alışkanlıklarımızı anlatırken kullanırız.

Simple Present Questions, Time Expressions and Adverbs of Manner

1 · Notice
  • Do you usually hand in your homework on time?
  • Does Emir take notes quickly in class?
  • I always comb my hair before school, but I never plan my bag the night before.
  • She listens carefully and writes slowly but neatly.

Which word starts a question about I/you/we/they, and which about he/she/it? Where do 'always' and 'never' go in the sentence? Where do 'carefully' and 'slowly' go?

2 · Discover

A Complete the rule

Look at the Notice sentences again. Complete the rule with the words in the box.

DoDoesbefore the main verbafter the verb (or object)

Well done! You found the rule yourself. Now let's use it.

B Build sentences

Use the cues to write sentences with questions, time expressions or adverbs of manner. Look at the example first.

  1. ? / you usually hand in your homework on time

    ✓ Do you usually hand in your homework on time?

  3. I always comb my hair before school
    Model answer

  4. she listen carefully in class
    Model answer

    She listens carefully in class.

  5. he write slowly but neatly
    Model answer

    He writes slowly but neatly.

  6. ? / your classmates often revise together
    Model answer

C Your turn

Write three sentences: one question with Do/Does, one with an adverb of frequency, and one with an adverb of manner.

Do you...?I always/usually/never...I... carefully/quickly/slowly.
Rule

In Simple Present questions, use Do with I/you/we/they and Does with he/she/it, and keep the base verb. Adverbs of frequency (always, usually, often, sometimes, never) go before the main verb; adverbs of manner (quickly, slowly, carefully) usually go after the verb or object.

TRŞimdiki zaman sorularında I/you/we/they ile 'Do', he/she/it ile 'Does' kullanılır ve fiil yalın hâlde kalır. Sıklık zarfları (always, usually, often, sometimes, never) ana fiilden önce; tarz zarfları (quickly, slowly, carefully) genellikle fiilden (veya nesneden) sonra gelir.

When?

We use this to ask questions about routines, and to say how often (frequency) or how (manner) we do something.

TRRutinler hakkında soru sorarken, bir şeyi ne sıklıkla yaptığımızı (sıklık zarfı) ve nasıl yaptığımızı (tarz zarfı) anlatırken kullanırız.

  • She listens careful in class.She listens carefully in class.

    Adverbs of manner end in -ly: carefully, quickly, slowly.

    TRTarz zarfı '-ly' ile biter: carefully, quickly, slowly.

A New Friend in Room 9B

My name is Emir, and today I have a new classmate — his name is Kerem, and he sits next to me in Room 9B. At first, we don't talk much, but at break time, Kerem asks me about my daily routine, and we quickly become friends.

I tell him about my typical school day. I wake up at half past six, get dressed quickly, and have a fast breakfast with my family. I check my timetable every morning before I leave the house, so I never forget my books.

Kerem laughs and says his morning routine is very different. He wakes up early too, but he doesn't have breakfast at home — he usually has it at school, at break time, with his new classmates. He combs his hair carefully and always plans his school bag the night before.

In class, Kerem takes notes quickly, but he also listens carefully to the teacher. I write more slowly, but I revise my notes every evening, and that habit helps me remember new words. We both hand in our homework on time, and our teacher says that's an excellent habit for a good student.

By the end of the day, Kerem and I aren't strangers any more — we're friends. Our friendship starts with a simple conversation about daily routines, but I think it's going to be a strong one.

Zeynep's Study Routine

My classmate Zeynep always has the best grades in our class, so one day I ask her about her study routine. She smiles and says it isn't a secret — she just has good habits.

Zeynep gets home from school at half past three, and she doesn't start studying immediately. First, she has a snack and rests for twenty minutes. Then she checks her timetable for tomorrow and plans her homework carefully.

She revises her notes every evening, usually for about forty-five minutes. She reads slowly and takes notes as she reads, because she says that helps her remember new information. She never leaves her homework until the last minute — she always hands it in early.

I ask her if studying every day is difficult, and she says no, because she does it in small steps, not all at once. On test days, she often repeats key vocabulary quietly to herself on the bus to school.

After we talk, I decide to try Zeynep's study routine myself. I don't become a perfect student overnight, but slowly, my new habits start to work — and my classmate Zeynep is happy to help me along the way.

KeremNo, I don't. I usually have it at school with my classmates, at break time.

Guess My Routine

Think of a daily or study habit (real or invented, for creative-writing purposes). Walk around the class and answer your classmates' yes/no questions with Do/Does so they can guess your habit. Then guess theirs.

Useful language

Do you do this every day?Does it happen at home or at school?Do you do it quickly or slowly?Yes, I do. / No, I don't. / Yes, he/she does. / No, he/she doesn't.

Adımlar

  1. Secretly choose a daily or study habit.
  2. Find a partner and let them ask up to 5 yes/no questions with Do/Does.
  3. Answer honestly with short answers.
  4. Swap roles, then find a new partner.
  5. After 4-5 rounds, share one interesting guess with the class.
DestekGive students a list of sample questions before they start.
Hedef5 rounds of guessing with a partner.
İleriFast finishers add one extra clue using an adverb of manner.
SpeakingPairs15-20 dk

Interview: My Classmate's Study Habits

Interview a partner about their daily routine and study habits: wake-up time, breakfast, homework routine, and how they revise. Take notes, then prepare to present your partner to the class.

Useful language

What time do you usually wake up?Do you plan your homework before or after dinner?How often do you revise your notes?This is [name]. He/She usually... He/She never...

Adımlar

  1. In pairs, take turns asking the interview questions and noting the answers.
  2. Check that you use Do/Does correctly in the questions.
  3. Prepare 4-5 sentences introducing your partner and their routine.
  4. Practise your short presentation with your partner.
  5. Present your partner to a small group or the whole class.

HedefFull interview + a short presentation about your partner.

My Daily Routine
